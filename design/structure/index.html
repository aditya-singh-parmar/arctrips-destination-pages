Three structures, one theme

Same content, same components, same colours and type as the app today. Only the structure changes. Each pane stacks the home page, the destination page (where the three genuinely differ), and the article page (identical in all three). Scroll any pane, or open one full screen.

S1. Flat

Open →

One destination page, one undivided grid of all 13 guides, then stays.

+ Nothing to learn, nothing to explain
+ Fastest to build, closest to what exists
- Planning pieces sit in the sell row and dilute it
- Falls apart at 20 guides, which Victoria will have

S2. Grouped by intent

Open →

Four labelled groups plus a sticky jump bar. Groups are labels, never pages, so no extra click.

+ Planning pieces get their own home, sell rows stay clean
+ Scales to 20 guides without becoming a wall
+ Bookable guides cluster at the top under On the water
- One more concept to name and keep consistent

S3. Trip-shaped

Open →

Four questions in the order people ask them: when to go, what to do, where to sleep, getting around.

+ Reads as advice rather than a directory
+ Answers the question before showing the cards
+ Stays connect to what you said you came to do
- The sell sits at step 2, below a planning question

What is gone from the structure you rejected.

  • The region tier. Home goes straight to Tofino. No Vancouver Island page holding two cards.
  • The category layer. There is no /tofino/beaches index sitting between the destination and the guide. It held nothing and cost a click.
  • Place pages. Long Beach is a section inside the beaches guide, not thirteen thin pages.
  • The things-to-do / guides / gallery split. They were three doors to the same content. Now there is one list of guides.

Depth went from five levels to two. Home to Tofino to the whale watching guide, and the booking is on that page. Previously: home, destinations, region, city, things-to-do, category, place.

My recommendation is S2. S1 is what you have already seen fail once the list grows, and Victoria will land with roughly twenty guides. S3 is the most interesting and the best fit for a travel brand, but it pushes the bookable guides below a planning question, which works against the sales-driven goal. S2 keeps the sell at the top, gives the planning pieces somewhere to live, and costs one label that never becomes a page. If you like S3's framing, the compromise is S2's grouping with step 1 collapsed into a single answer line above the groups.
