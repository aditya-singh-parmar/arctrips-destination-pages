Whale watching in Tofino

By the Arc Trips editorsUpdated March 20268 min read

Somewhere around the middle of March, roughly twenty thousand grey whales pass the mouth of Clayoquot Sound on their way north. Most people miss them by two weeks.

The migration is the loud part of the season, but it is not the whole of it. A group of resident greys peels off and stays through summer, feeding in the shallows off Cow Bay. Humpbacks arrive in June and stay until the light goes. Orcas turn up unannounced.

What follows is when to come, what you are likely to see, and how to get on the water without wasting a morning.

When to come

March to May is the migration. Boats run daily, sightings are close to guaranteed, and the weather is doing whatever it likes. June to September is calmer water, resident greys, and humpbacks. October onward the tours thin out and storm season takes over.

What you will see, by month

  • March to April, grey whales heading north, often several at once
  • May, the tail of the migration plus the first residents settling in
  • June to September, humpbacks offshore and resident greys close in
  • Year round, sea lions, harbour seals, and bald eagles on the way out

Where they actually are

The boats head for the same handful of places. Cow Bay on the west side of Flores Island is the reliable one for residents. In migration season the skippers push further out toward the open coast, which is why the ride gets rougher and the trip gets longer.

You do not need a boat to see one. From shore, the best odds are the headland at Cox Bay, the rocks at the end of Chesterman, and the lookout on the Wild Pacific Trail across the water in Ucluelet. Bring binoculars and low expectations.

Doing it properly

Dress for one season colder than the forecast. The open water off Tofino is reliably ten degrees meaner than the harbour, and a zodiac at speed removes any warmth you were holding on to.

  • Book the first sailing of the day, the water is flattest before eleven
  • Take the covered boat in March, take the zodiac in July
  • Keep your distance from wildlife, never crowd a surfacing animal
  • Seasickness tablets work, but only if taken an hour before

Common questions

What is the best month for whale watching in Tofino?
Mid March to early May for the grey whale migration, when sightings are close to guaranteed. July and August are better if you want calm water and humpbacks.
Can you see whales from shore in Tofino?
Yes, though it takes patience. The headland at Cox Bay and the end of Chesterman Beach are the two best vantage points from land.
Are the tours suitable for children?
Covered vessels take children from about five. Zodiacs are usually eight and up, and they are a genuinely rough ride in migration season.
What happens if we do not see a whale?
Every operator listed here runs a sightings guarantee. If the trip comes up empty you go again at no cost, subject to availability.
