The whales come for the food, and the food is here

Tofino sits beside Clayoquot Sound, a rich coastal area with ocean, islands, beaches, rainforest and rocky shorelines. It is one of the best places on Vancouver Island to see wild animals, and the reason is structural: the shelf here pushes cold water up, the cold water feeds the herring and the krill, and everything larger follows.

Gray whales pass on their way from the calving lagoons in Baja California to the feeding grounds off Alaska. Humpbacks stay to feed through the summer. Orcas turn up when they turn up. The same water holds sea lions, harbour seals, sea otters, bald eagles, and a seabird passage twice a year.

Forty minutes south, Ucluelet faces Barkley Sound and the Broken Group Islands. That is the quieter half of the same shelf, and it is where the viewpoints and boat routes for this coast are.

Boats and cost

Three kinds of boat, one honest note about price

Tours run March to October, most of them two and a half to three hours. Operators pick the route on the day from weather, swell and the last few days of sightings, so nobody sells you a fixed itinerary.

Covered boat

Better for comfort and cooler days. You are out of the wind and the spray, and you can sit down for three hours without regretting it.

Best forComfort, cool days, long trips

Zodiac

Faster, open air, and a rougher ride. Better for travellers who want the adventure version and are happy in a survival suit on the open Pacific.

Best forSpeed and open air

Small or private charter

Better for families, photographers, and groups who want the boat to stop where they want it to stop. Fewer people at the rail, more say in the route, and nobody minds if a child needs to go back early.

Best forFamilies, photographers, small groups

What it costs

We do not publish an operator price until we have paid it ourselves. Every Tofino and Ucluelet operator sets its own rate by boat type, season and group size, and a number copied from a website in February is wrong by June.

The one number we do stand behind is the stay: 134 places in Tofino, from $320 a night, and 158 more in Ucluelet forty minutes down the road.

What to bring

Six things, in the order they matter

Number one changes the day more than the other five together. The water is colder than the forecast you read at breakfast, and it is always windier at the rail than on the dock.

Binoculars
The single biggest upgrade to a day from shore. On the Wild Pacific Trail the wildlife is often a long way out, and without them you are looking at empty water.
A waterproof shell
Tofino gets rain on 22 days in January and still 9 in July. A shell is never the wrong call here.
Warm layers under it
The August average high is 18.6 degrees on land. On the water, moving, it is not.
Something for motion
The open Pacific has swell most days. If you are prone to it, take something before you board rather than after.
A national park pass
You may need one if you stop in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve, which is most of the coast between the two towns.
Sunglasses and sunscreen
Flat grey water reflects hard, and the three hours you spend squinting at the horizon are the three hours you get burned.
