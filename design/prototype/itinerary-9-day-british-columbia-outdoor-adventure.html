9-Day British Columbia Outdoor Adventure

Seawall, Summits, and Canyon Trails

Length
9 days
Towns
Vancouver, Squamish, Whistler, Okanagan, Kelowna
Best for
Active travelers

BC is a good place to mix walks and bike rides on the same trip. The coast has easy paths by the water and shady forest trails, while Whistler and the Okanagan give you more room for longer rides. Nine days suits active travelers who want to spend time outside and choose how hard to go each day. Keep the long drive east separate from your main outings. Check lift and trail access, and plan to head out early when it’s hot inland.

The plan

  1. Day 1: Vancouver by bike or on foot

    Follow an open section of the Stanley Park Seawall, then eat dinner in the West End.

  2. Day 2: North Shore forest

    Take the SeaBus to North Vancouver and visit Lynn Canyon if trails are open. Stay on marked paths near the canyon and water.

  3. Day 3: Shannon Falls and Squamish

    Pick up your car and drive north. Stop at Shannon Falls, then spend the afternoon in Squamish. If you want a longer outing, try open upper trails via the Sea to Sky Gondola if conditions suit.

  4. Day 4: Whistler valley ride

    Continue to Whistler and ride the Valley Trail to an open lake park. Check signs for shared path rules and turn back when you’re ready.

  5. Day 5: Whistler mountain day

    Pick a guided downhill lesson or take sightseeing lifts and hike an open trail that suits your group. Check the last lift down.

  6. Day 6: Drive to the Okanagan

    Give the transfer the full day. Check DriveBC before taking any mountain route and bring food and water for stretches between towns. Stay in Kelowna.

  7. Day 7: Myra Canyon

    Walk or bike the trestles and return to Kelowna for a late lakeside lunch.

  8. Day 8: Naramata Bench

    Drive south to Penticton and walk a section of the Kettle Valley Rail Trail. In very hot weather, go early and keep the afternoon easy by Skaha Lake.

  9. Day 9: Exit via Kelowna or Vancouver

    Return to Kelowna for a flight if it fits. Use the day as a drive day if you’re returning west and leave your flight for the next day.

Recommended stops

  • Stanley Park SeawallBegin with a city ride or a shorter walk.
  • Shannon Falls and Whistler Valley TrailSpread the walks and rides over separate days.
  • Myra Canyon and the Naramata BenchEnd with two rail trail outings you can shorten as needed.

Tips for this trip

  • Rent the right bike for the route, and check operating dates if you plan to ride a lift.
  • Plan exposed Okanagan walks for early morning in hot weather and keep an indoor backup if smoke is heavy.

Frequently asked questions

Do you need a park day pass in BC?

Some parks require a free day-use pass at certain times. Check your chosen park before adding hikes in places such as Garibaldi or Joffre Lakes.

What if wildfire smoke changes your plans?

Check the air quality, wildfire updates, and road reports. Move hikes to a better day or choose an indoor stop when conditions call for it.

Can beginners do this outdoor itinerary?

Yes. Use paved paths or short out-and-back walks and book a lesson if you want to try downhill biking.

Which days should you book ahead?

Reserve lessons, bike rentals, and any timed lifts you need. Check park pass rules before adding a popular hike.

Can you do the trip without renting a bike?

Yes. Walk the Seawall, follow short valley paths, and turn back after the first few trestles at Myra Canyon.
