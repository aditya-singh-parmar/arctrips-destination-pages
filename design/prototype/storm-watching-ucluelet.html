Storm watching in Ucluelet

High rocky bluffs instead of low sandy beaches, so you watch the swell explode from safe, high ground.

Ucluelet is widely considered the storm-watching capital of Vancouver Island. It sits in front of the big low-pressure systems that roll in from the North Pacific, and its high rocky bluffs give a safe but close view of big swells and exploding waves.

The Wild Pacific Trail was built for it: its elevated sections keep you above the spray and surge while the waves come in below.

Pick the day

The best viewing comes when high tide meets high wind and a big swell, around 5 meters or more. Look for gale or storm warnings.

Where to watch

From the lighthouse on the point to the beaches in the forest, all free and on foot.

  1. 01

    Amphitrite Point Lighthouse and the Lighthouse Loop

    The most iconic storm watching in the region. Waves hit the black volcanic rock and throw salt spray high above the lighthouse tower. The gravel trails keep you on high ground, well above the reach of rogue waves.

    The lighthouse was built in 1915 to replace an earlier wooden tower destroyed by a wave, and its stout shape is made to take gale-force wind.

    Good forFirst-time storm watchers · Short walks · Photography

    Good to know. Stay on the trail and behind the barriers, however good the view looks from the rocks.

  2. 02

    Wild Pacific Trail

    In winter the trail is an elevated platform for watching big waves. Its bog-style sections are built to keep you above the spray and surge.

    Good forLonger walks · Big swell · Quiet winter days

    Good to know. Bring a waterproof jacket and boots with grip: the path stays wet all winter.

  3. 03

    Big Beach Picnic Park

    A more grounded view from the forest edge. Big logs are often thrown onto the shore in a storm, and can reshape the beach in a single afternoon. The gazebo and the tree canopy give some shelter from the wind.

    Good forFamilies · Watching from shelter · Wind and surf

    Good to know. Keep off the logs on the beach in a storm. They move.

  4. 04

    Terrace Beach

    When mist hides the view at the lighthouse, head lower. From Terrace Beach you see the white water closer to the shore.

    Good forMisty days · A closer view

    Good to know. Watch from well back, and leave before the tide turns.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

When is storm watching season in Ucluelet?

The most consistent and powerful storms come from November through February, and October and March can also be impressive. Amphitrite Point and the Wild Pacific Trail are the classic viewing spots.

Where is the best place to watch storms in Ucluelet?

Amphitrite Point Lighthouse and the Lighthouse Loop. The waves hit the rocks below the lighthouse and the gravel trails keep you on high ground.

How do you know a storm is coming?

Look for gale or storm warnings on a weather app. The biggest show comes when high tide lines up with strong wind and a big swell, around 5 meters or more.

What if it is too misty to see?

Storms often bring heavy mist. If you cannot see from the lighthouse, go lower, to Terrace Beach, where the white water is closer to shore.
