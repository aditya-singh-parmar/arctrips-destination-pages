Three Days in the Okanagan

From Kelowna to the Desert Lakes

Length
3 days
Towns
Okanagan, Kelowna, Penticton, Oliver, Osoyoos

The Okanagan changes as you go south. Around Kelowna, there’s lots to do by the lake. Near Osoyoos, the hills look drier and the towns feel smaller. Three days gives first-time visitors enough time to notice the change, with room for short walks and a tasting if that’s your thing. Traveling in one direction keeps the driving down. If you’re flying out of Kelowna, leave time to come back north.

The plan

  1. Day 1: Kelowna and Myra Canyon

    Walk or bike a section of Myra Canyon from Myra Station if the access road and trail are in good shape. Return to downtown Kelowna for a late lunch and a stroll along Okanagan Lake.

  2. Day 2: Penticton and the Naramata Bench

    Stop at the Peachland waterfront on the way south. In Penticton, walk by Okanagan Beach and pick up lunch. Visit a winery on the Naramata Bench with a sober driver or walk a section of the Kettle Valley Rail Trail.

  3. Day 3: Oliver and Osoyoos

    Head south to Oliver, then continue to Osoyoos for a waterfront walk. If open, visit the Nk’Mip Desert Cultural Centre to learn about the Osoyoos Indian Band and the plants and wildlife of the area. If you’re returning north the same day, skip the extra tasting stop.

Recommended stops

  • Myra CanyonWalk or bike part of the old rail route.
  • Naramata BenchChoose a tasting or a rail trail walk.
  • Nk’Mip Desert Cultural CentreMake this your main Osoyoos stop if open.

Tips for this trip

  • Pack water and plan exposed walks early in warm weather.
  • If you’re flying from Kelowna, check how much of Day 3 you can spend south of Penticton.

Frequently asked questions

Is three days enough for the Okanagan?

Yes for a short trip through the main towns. Five days gives you more time at the lakes and fewer hotel changes.

When is a good time for this route?

Summer suits beach days. Spring and fall suit walks and tastings, with fewer daylight hours or shorter business hours to plan around.

Where should you stay on the second night?

Pick Penticton for easy access to Naramata or Osoyoos for more time at the south end.

Can you do this route with children?

Yes. Choose a short trestle walk, an afternoon at Okanagan Beach, and a stop at the cultural center if open.

What can you do on a hot Okanagan afternoon?

Take a long lunch indoors or choose a shaded stop by the lake. Save sunny trails for morning.
