Whale watching in Ucluelet

Gray whales heading north from March, humpbacks through the summer, orcas whenever the water allows. Four of the six places to watch from are free and on foot.

Ucluelet sits on the short run to Barkley Sound, the Broken Group Islands and the open Pacific, which is why a town of this size has a whale watching fleet at all. Gray whales pass on their way north from the calving lagoons in Baja California toward Alaska. Humpbacks stay to feed. Orcas turn up when they turn up, and nobody sells you a guarantee on the third.

The same water holds sea lions, harbour seals, sea otters and bald eagles all year, which is why a day from shore is rarely wasted even out of season.

From shore, for nothing

Four places you can walk to and watch from without booking anything. Bring binoculars: from land the animals are usually a long way out.

  1. 01

    Wild Pacific Trail

    The Wild Pacific Trail is one of the best places in Ucluelet to watch wildlife from land. It follows the rocky coast and gives you many ocean viewpoints.

    You may see whales offshore, eagles flying above the trees, sea lions on rocks, seals in the water, cormorants near the shore, and many types of coastal birds.

    The trail is also one of the easiest ways to enjoy wildlife without booking a tour.

    Good forWhale watching from shore · Bald eagles · Sea lions and seals · Coastal birds · Storm watching · Photography · Easy walking

    Good to know. Bring binoculars. Wildlife may be far from shore.

  2. 02

    Amphitrite Point Lighthouse

    Amphitrite Point Lighthouse is one of the most famous stops on the Wild Pacific Trail. It has wide ocean views and is a good place to watch for whales, sea lions, seals, and birds.

    In spring, you may have a chance to spot migrating gray whales offshore. In other seasons, you may still see eagles, seals, sea lions, and seabirds.

    Good forWhale watching from land · Ocean views · Photography · Eagles · Seals and sea lions · Storm watching

    Good to know. Stay on marked paths and away from wet rocks. Large waves can be dangerous.

  3. 03

    Ucluelet Harbour

    Ucluelet Harbour is a good place to look for wildlife close to town. You may see seals, eagles, gulls, cormorants, river otters, and shorebirds.

    This is also where many wildlife and whale watching tours depart.

    Good forEasy wildlife watching · Boat tour departures · Seals · Eagles · River otters · Harbour birds

    Good to know. Watch for boat traffic and keep a safe distance from docks and working harbour areas.

  4. 04

    Pacific Rim National Park Reserve

    Pacific Rim National Park Reserve is near Ucluelet and includes the Long Beach Unit, Broken Group Islands, and the West Coast Trail area. It is a major protected area for coastal rainforest, beaches, and marine ecosystems.

    Wildlife here may include black bears, wolves, cougars, deer, whales, seals, sea lions, shorebirds, and many other coastal species.

    Visitors should give wildlife space, store food properly, and never feed animals.

    Good forBird watching · Beach wildlife · Whale watching from viewpoints · Black bear awareness · Coastal rainforest wildlife · Tidepool life · Guided nature learning

    Good to know. You may need a national park pass if you stop in the park.

By boat

Two areas you cannot reach on foot, and where most of the whales are. Tours run March to October out of the harbour, about two and a half to three hours.

  1. 05

    Barkley Sound

    Barkley Sound is one of the top wildlife watching areas near Ucluelet. It includes islands, channels, rocky shores, kelp beds, and sheltered waters.

    This is where many boat and kayak trips go. It is also the gateway to the Broken Group Islands.

    Good forWhales · Sea lions · Seals · Sea otters · Bald eagles · Seabirds · Black bears along shorelines · Boat tours and kayaking

    Good to know. The best way to explore Barkley Sound wildlife is by guided boat or kayak tour.

  2. 06

    Broken Group Islands

    The Broken Group Islands are part of Pacific Rim National Park Reserve in Barkley Sound. They are known for forested islands, shell beaches, sheltered channels, reefs, and rocky outcrops.

    This is a great area for wildlife watching by kayak or boat. You may see seals, sea lions, sea otters, bald eagles, seabirds, shorebirds, and sometimes whales in nearby waters.

    Good forKayak wildlife watching · Boat tours · Sea otters · Seals and sea lions · Bald eagles · Seabirds · Multi-day paddling trips

    Good to know. The Broken Group Islands are boat-access only. Plan ahead if you are kayaking or camping.

Questions

Sixteen things people ask before they go out

Every answer below comes from one of the four Ucluelet whale guides, and that guide is under each one.

Is Ucluelet good for whale watching?

Yes. Ucluelet is one of the best whale watching bases on Vancouver Island because it gives access to Barkley Sound, the Broken Group Islands area, and Pacific feeding and migration waters.

Whale Watching in Ucluelet
What whales can you see in Ucluelet?

You may see gray whales, humpback whales, and sometimes orcas. Tours may also see sea lions, harbor seals, sea otters, bald eagles, and seabirds.

Whale Watching in Ucluelet
When is the best time for whale watching in Ucluelet?

March to May is best for gray whale migration. Mid-summer to September is often good for humpback whales. Orcas are possible but less predictable.

Whale Watching in Ucluelet
Can you see gray whales in Ucluelet?

Yes. Gray whales are one of the main whale species associated with Ucluelet, especially during spring migration.

Whale Watching in Ucluelet
What whales migrate past Ucluelet in spring?

Pacific gray whales are the main whales associated with the spring migration in Ucluelet. Humpbacks may also appear later in spring and become more common during summer.

Whales in Ucluelet: The Spring Migration Explained
When is the gray whale migration in Ucluelet?

The main spring migration period is usually March to May. Early sightings may happen in February, and some whales may still be present into early summer.

Whales in Ucluelet: The Spring Migration Explained
Why do gray whales migrate past Ucluelet?

Gray whales pass Ucluelet as they travel north from winter breeding and calving areas in Baja California, Mexico, toward northern feeding grounds near Alaska and the Arctic.

Whales in Ucluelet: The Spring Migration Explained
Is March a good time for whale watching in Ucluelet?

Yes. March is a key month because gray whales are returning along the coast, and the Pacific Rim Whale Festival celebrates their migration.

Whales in Ucluelet: The Spring Migration Explained
Are whale watching tours in Ucluelet worth it?

Yes. Ucluelet is a strong whale watching base because tours can access Barkley Sound, the Broken Group Islands area, and nearby Pacific waters with whales and other marine wildlife.

Whale Watching Tours in Ucluelet
What is the best whale watching tour in Ucluelet?

The best tour depends on your needs. Families and comfort-focused travelers may prefer a covered boat. Adventure travelers may prefer a zodiac. Photographers or groups may prefer a smaller tour or private charter.

Whale Watching Tours in Ucluelet
When do whale watching tours run in Ucluelet?

Many whale watching tours run from March to October, but exact dates depend on the operator, weather, and season.

Whale Watching Tours in Ucluelet
How long are Ucluelet whale watching tours?

Many tours are about 2.5 to 3 hours, but timing can vary. Always check the operator’s current tour details.

Whale Watching Tours in Ucluelet
Is Ucluelet good for wildlife watching?

Yes. Ucluelet is one of the best wildlife watching areas on Vancouver Island. You can see whales, black bears, sea lions, seals, sea otters, bald eagles, shorebirds, seabirds, and more.

Whale watching FAQs, Ucluelet
What animals can you see in Ucluelet?

You may see gray whales, humpback whales, orcas, black bears, sea lions, harbor seals, sea otters, river otters, bald eagles, great blue herons, shorebirds, and seabirds.

Whale watching FAQs, Ucluelet
Can you see whales from Ucluelet?

Yes. You can see whales on boat tours, and sometimes from shore along the Wild Pacific Trail, especially during migration seasons.

Whale watching FAQs, Ucluelet
Where is the best place to see whales from shore in Ucluelet?

The Wild Pacific Trail and Amphitrite Point Lighthouse area are among the best shore-based places to scan for whales. Bring binoculars.
