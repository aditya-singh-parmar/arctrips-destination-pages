Two Days in the Okanagan

Okanagan Lakes and the Naramata Bench

Length
2 days
Towns
Okanagan, Kelowna, Penticton
Best for
Couples or families

Two days is enough to get a feel for the Okanagan if you stick to one stretch of the lake. Kelowna has the busier waterfront, while Penticton and Naramata are good places to slow down. This trip works for couples or families who want some time outdoors and don’t mind a short drive between towns. Decide whether your afternoons are for the beach or a tasting. If it’s a tasting, sort out your ride early.

The plan

  1. Day 1: Kelowna and Myra Canyon

    Start at Myra Station if the road is suitable for your car. Walk a short stretch of the trestles or rent a bike in season. Go back downtown for lunch and spend the late afternoon between Waterfront Park and City Park. Stay downtown for a walk to dinner.

  2. Day 2: Penticton and Naramata

    Drive south along the west side of Okanagan Lake. Stop in Peachland for coffee by the water, then continue to Penticton for lunch. Spend the afternoon on the Naramata Bench with one or two tasting stops, or take a walk along the lake in Penticton. Book a ride if you plan to drink.

Recommended stops

  • Myra CanyonChoose a short trestle walk or a longer bike ride.
  • Peachland waterfrontStop for coffee on the way south.
  • Naramata BenchPick a tasting or a walk on the Kettle Valley Rail Trail above the lake.

Tips for this trip

  • Stay in Penticton on the second night if you want to walk to dinner after visiting the bench.
  • In hot weather, walk the trestles early and leave the afternoon for shade or the lake.

Frequently asked questions

Should you stay in Kelowna or Penticton?

Choose Kelowna for city dining and Myra Canyon. Choose Penticton if you want more time near the beach and Naramata.

Can you visit the Okanagan without going to wineries?

Yes. Swap the tastings for Penticton’s waterfront, a beach afternoon, or a stroll in Naramata village.

Where should you stop between Kelowna and Penticton?

Peachland is an easy place for coffee, a meal, or a stretch by the lake.

Do you need a car for this two-day route?

A car helps with Myra Canyon and the turnoffs. Book separate transport for tastings if you plan to drink.

Is the Naramata Bench worth visiting without a tasting?

Yes. Walk an open section of the rail trail or head down to Naramata village for a picnic.
