Things to do in Tofino

Hiking and trails in Tofino

Rainforest boardwalks, beach walks and two island climbs. Most of them are short, and the muddy ones are muddy all year.

April to October

The long, dry-ish window. Boardwalks are still slippery after rain, but the light lasts and the trailhead parking is open.

Winter

Many short trails stay open. Winter weather makes them muddy, slippery and windy, so choose shorter trails and start in daylight.

Rain, any month

Rainforest trails are at their best in the rain. Wear waterproof clothing and shoes with grip, because boardwalks, stairs and roots turn greasy.

Where to walk

Eighteen walks, from the village outward

The ones you can start on foot first, then the national park trails strung along the highway, then the three that need a boat. Difficulty climbs with the distance.

All nine subjects in Tofino

From the village and the town beaches

01 to 05

No park pass, no car needed for most of them.

Easy to moderateForest, beaches, sunset

Tonquin Trail

The Tonquin Trail is one of the best easy trails in Tofino. It starts near the village and leads through coastal forest to Tonquin Beach, Third Beach, and Middle Beach.

This trail is a great choice if you want forest and beach in one walk. You will pass old-growth forest, ocean viewpoints, stairs, and small beaches. It is also a popular sunset walk.

Good for easy hiking, families, forest views, and 4 more.

Good to know. The trail can be muddy after rain, and there are stairs in some areas. Wear shoes with good grip.

EasyShort beach walk

Tonquin Beach Trail

If you do not want to walk the full Tonquin Trail, you can make a shorter visit to Tonquin Beach. This is one of the closest beach walks to Tofino village.

The walk is short and scenic. It takes you through forest and down to a sandy beach with views across the water. It is a nice choice if you only have a little time.

Good for short walks, sunset views, picnics, and 2 more.

Good to know. The beach is smaller than Chesterman or Cox Bay, so it may feel more closed in at high tide.

Easy to moderateQuiet beaches

Third Beach and Middle Beach Walk

Third Beach and Middle Beach are small beaches along the Tonquin Trail. They are less famous than Tonquin Beach, but they are worth visiting if you want a quieter walk.

This part of the trail has rocky viewpoints, forest sections, and a peaceful feel. It is not a big beach day walk. It is better for people who like hidden corners and short nature stops.

Good for quiet beach stops, forest-to-beach scenery, photography, and 2 more.

Good to know. Beach access can be a little rough in places. Watch your step, especially after rain.

EasyWalking, biking, running

Multi-Use Path in Tofino

Tofino also has a local multi-use path that is good for walking, running, and biking. This is not a wilderness hike, but it is useful if you want a safe and easy way to move around without driving.

The path connects parts of Tofino and links with the larger route toward Pacific Rim National Park Reserve.

Good for walking, running, biking, and 3 more.

Good to know. Share the path with bikes, walkers, and runners. Stay to the right and be aware of faster users.

ModerateViews and photos

Cox Bay Lookout

Cox Bay Lookout is one of the most popular viewpoint walks near Tofino. The trail is short, but it can be steep, muddy, and slippery.

The reward is a beautiful view over Cox Bay, the beach, the forest, and the coastline. It is one of the most photographed viewpoints in the Tofino area.

Good for ocean views, photos, sunset views, and 2 more.

Good to know. This is not a polished boardwalk trail. It can be muddy and rough. Wear proper shoes and avoid it if conditions are very slippery.

Along the park highway

06 to 15

Inside Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. You may need a Parks Canada pass to stop.

EasyQuick viewpoint stop

Radar Hill Trail

Radar Hill is in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve, between Tofino and Ucluelet. It is one of the easiest viewpoints near Tofino and a good stop if you want a short walk with a big reward.

The walk is short and simple compared with many other trails. From the top, you can see forest, ocean, islands, and the wide west coast landscape. It is also a place with World War II history, as the hill was once used as a radar station.

Good for easy walking, ocean and forest views, a quick stop, and 3 more.

Good to know. This is more of a short viewpoint walk than a long hike. You may need a national park pass when stopping in this area.

Easy to moderateBig trees and boardwalks

Rainforest Trail

The Rainforest Trail is in the Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. It is one of the best short walks near Tofino if you want to see the classic west coast rainforest.

The trail has boardwalks, stairs, giant trees, moss, ferns, and deep green forest. It feels like walking inside a living postcard.

There are two short loops on different sides of the highway. You can do one loop or both.

Good for first-time visitors, families, rainy days, and 3 more.

Good to know. The boardwalk can be slippery when wet. You may need a national park pass when stopping in this area.

EasyFlat boardwalk, bog plants

Shorepine Bog Trail

The Shorepine Bog Trail is a short and easy boardwalk trail in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. It feels very different from the deep rainforest trails.

Instead of giant trees, you will see small twisted shore pines, bog plants, moss, and open wetland scenery. It is a great trail if you want something flat, quick, and different.

Good for easy walking, families, short stops, and 3 more.

Good to know. Stay on the boardwalk to protect the fragile bog plants.

EasyShort forest walk, beach access

Spruce Fringe Trail

Spruce Fringe Trail is a short forest trail in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. It is often linked with Combers Beach because it gives access toward the Combers Beach area.

This trail is a good choice if you want a quick forest walk with a coastal feel. It is not as famous as the Rainforest Trail, but it is a quieter easy stop in the Long Beach area.

Good for short forest walks, beach access, nature photos, and 2 more.

Good to know. Trail access and nearby beach rules can change. Check Parks Canada updates before visiting, especially if you plan to bring a dog.

ModerateRainforest, beach views, learning

Nuu-chah-nulth Trail

The Nuu-chah-nulth Trail is in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. It connects rainforest, boardwalks, beach views, and cultural learning.

This trail is longer than some of the short boardwalk trails, but it is still a good choice for many visitors. Along the way, you can learn more about the land, culture, and natural history of the area.

Good for rainforest walking, ocean views, learning, and 3 more.

Good to know. Expect stairs, boardwalks, and uneven sections. Give yourself enough time and wear proper shoes.

Easy to moderateShort coastal walk, rocky views

South Beach Trail

South Beach Trail is in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve near the Kwisitis Visitor Centre and Wickaninnish Beach. It is a short coastal trail with a strong west coast feel.

The trail leads through forest toward a rocky beach area. It is a good choice if you want a quick walk, ocean sounds, and dramatic coastal views without doing a long hike.

Good for short hikes, rocky coast views, photography, and 3 more.

Good to know. South Beach is better for viewing than swimming. Stay back from waves and wet rocks, especially during high tide or stormy weather.

EasyLong beach walk, ocean views

Long Beach Hike

Long Beach is not a formal hike in the same way as a forest trail, but it is one of the best long beach walks near Tofino. It is part of Pacific Rim National Park Reserve and is one of the most famous coastal areas on Vancouver Island.

A Long Beach hike is simple: walk along the sand, watch the waves, enjoy the wide-open views, and turn back when you are ready. It is a great choice for travelers who want an easy walk with big scenery.

Good for long beach walks, surf watching, photography, and 3 more.

Good to know. Check tide times before walking far. Stay away from large waves and never turn your back on the ocean.

EasyWild beach access

Combers Beach Trail

Combers Beach Trail is a short trail in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve that leads to a wild beach. It is a good choice if you want a quiet beach walk and a more natural feel.

This trail is more about beach access than a long forest hike. Once you reach the beach, you can walk along the sand when tide and conditions are safe.

Good for beach walks, quiet nature time, photography, and 2 more.

Good to know. Check current park access and dog rules before visiting. Tide and seasonal rules can affect your visit.

Easy to moderateForest to beach scenery

Schooner Cove Trail

Schooner Cove Trail is another trail in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. It is known for forest-to-beach scenery and a quieter feel.

It is a good stop if you like peaceful walks, rainforest paths, and beach access. Like many coastal trails, access can change due to weather, maintenance, or park updates.

Good for forest walks, beach access, quiet exploring, and 2 more.

Good to know. Check current Parks Canada updates before planning this trail.

Easy to moderateBiking, walking, longer path

ʔapsčiik t̓ašii Multi-Use Pathway

ʔapsčiik t̓ašii, pronounced “ups-cheek ta-shee,” is a paved multi-use pathway in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. It runs through the Long Beach Unit and connects many points of interest between the Tofino and Ucluelet area.

This is not a forest hike in the usual sense. It is best for biking, walking, and longer active days. It passes through forest and bog areas, and it is a way to reach the park without driving to every stop.

Good for biking, walking, running, and 3 more.

Good to know. The path is paved, but there are some hills and curves. Bring water and plan your return route.

Across the water

16 to 18

Meares Island and Flores Island. Each of these starts with a boat, so the day starts the night before.

ModerateOld-growth trees

Big Tree Trail on Meares Island

Big Tree Trail is on Meares Island, not in Tofino town. To visit, you usually need a water taxi or guided trip from Tofino.

This trail is known for huge old-growth trees. It is one of the best choices if you want to see very large cedars and learn why the old-growth rainforest is so important.

Good for big trees, old-growth forest, nature lovers, and 3 more.

Good to know. Plan ahead because you need boat access. Wear good shoes because the trail can be wet and uneven.

HardA hard climb and a big view

Lone Cone Trail

Lone Cone is also on Meares Island. It is much harder than most trails near Tofino. It is a steep hike with a viewpoint at the top.

This trail is best for fit hikers who want a challenge. It is not the best choice for young kids, casual walkers, or people who only want an easy nature trail.

Good for experienced hikers, big views, a hard workout, and 2 more.

Good to know. This is a steep and challenging hike. You need boat access, proper shoes, water, and enough time.

Moderate to hardRemote beaches, backpacking

Wild Side Trail on Flores Island

The Wild Side Trail is one of the most special hikes near Tofino. It is on Flores Island in Clayoquot Sound and starts near the village of Ahousaht.

This trail is more remote than most walks in the Tofino area. You need to reach Flores Island by water taxi, boat, kayak, or float plane. The trail follows sandy beaches and forested headlands along the wild west side of the island.

The Wild Side Trail is best for travelers who want a bigger adventure, not just a short walk. It can be done as a longer day hike by experienced visitors, but many people treat it as an overnight or multi-day trip.

Good for remote beach hiking, wilderness scenery, backpacking, and 4 more.

Good to know. Plan ahead. You may need water taxi transport, local permissions or stewardship fees, camping plans, and current trail information. This is not a casual town trail like Tonquin Trail.

Before you go

Four rules the trails here run on

Tofino trails are short, well used and still wild places. Every line below comes from the trail notes above.

Stay on the marked trail

Do not climb over railings. If a trail looks closed, do not enter it: closures protect visitors, wildlife and the ground under the boardwalk.

Grip beats boots

You do not always need hiking boots, but you do need shoes with grip. Roots, stairs and wet boardwalk are the most common way people get hurt here.

Every trail that ends at the ocean

Check tide times before a beach walk, keep children close near cliffs and stairs, and never turn your back on the water.

Wildlife, dogs and rubbish

Never feed wildlife. Make noise in quiet forest. Keep dogs leashed where required, and pack out everything you carried in.

Questions

Asked before people book

Open the ones you need. Everything here is answered in the same words the guide uses.

What is the best easy trail in Tofino?

The Tonquin Trail is one of the best easy trails in Tofino because it is close to town and includes forest, beaches, and ocean views.

What is the best rainforest trail near Tofino?

The Rainforest Trail in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve is one of the best rainforest trails near Tofino. It has boardwalks, huge trees, ferns, moss, and a classic west coast forest feel.

Are Tofino trails good for kids?

Yes, many Tofino trails are good for kids. Tonquin Trail, Shorepine Bog Trail, Rainforest Trail, and South Beach Trail are good choices. Keep kids close near stairs, beaches, cliffs, and slippery boardwalks.

Can you walk from Tofino to the beach?

Yes. Tonquin Trail is one of the easiest ways to walk from Tofino village to a beach. Some other beaches are easier to reach by car or bike.

Do you need hiking boots for Tofino trails?

You do not always need hiking boots, but you should wear shoes with good grip. Trails can be wet, muddy, and slippery, even on short walks.

Are dogs allowed on Tofino trails?

Dogs are allowed on some trails, but rules change by location. In Pacific Rim National Park Reserve, dogs must be leashed where allowed, and some areas may have restrictions. Always check signs before starting.

What is the best trail for storm watching near Tofino?

The Wild Pacific Trail in Ucluelet is one of the best nearby trails for storm watching from safe viewpoints. Near Tofino, Tonquin Trail and Cox Bay Lookout can offer views, but avoid unsafe areas during strong storms.

What is the best trail for big trees near Tofino?

Big Tree Trail on Meares Island is one of the best trails for seeing huge old-growth trees. The Rainforest Trail is also a great choice and is easier to reach by car.

Can you hike in Tofino when it rains?

Yes. Rainforest trails can be beautiful in the rain. Wear waterproof clothing and shoes with good grip because boardwalks, stairs, and roots can be slippery.

Do you need a park pass for trails near Tofino?

You may need a Parks Canada pass if you stop in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. This includes many trails between Tofino and Ucluelet, such as Rainforest Trail, Shorepine Bog Trail, South Beach Trail, Nuu-chah-nulth Trail, Combers Beach Trail and Schooner Cove Trail. Trails inside Tofino town, such as Tonquin Trail, do not usually need one.

Can you hike in Tofino in winter?

Yes. Many short trails are open year-round, but winter weather can make trails muddy, slippery, and windy. Winter is a great time for rainforest walks and storm watching from safe viewpoints. Choose shorter trails and start during daylight.
