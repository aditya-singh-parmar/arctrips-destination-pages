Suggested itineraries, 2 to 10 days

Day by day plans for one town or several: the west coast, Whistler and Squamish, the south of Vancouver Island, and the whole island end to end. Each stop links to its town.

Tofino and Ucluelet

Tofino and Ucluelet share one stretch of coast: Tofino for long beaches and surf, Ucluelet for a quieter harbour town and rugged shoreline. Towns: Tofino, Ucluelet.

2days

Two days on the west coast

Beach, rainforest and a sunset cove in Tofino, then the Wild Pacific Trail, lighthouse and harbour in Ucluelet.

Every season works. Summer is busiest, spring and fall are quieter, winter is for storm watching.

Day 1: Tofino beaches and rainforest

  1. Chesterman BeachA 2.7-kilometre stretch of white sand shaped like a W. Walk it, or take a beginner surf lesson on the sandy bottom.
  2. Rainforest TrailTwo boardwalk loops through the old-growth forest of Pacific Rim National Park Reserve, right off the Pacific Rim Highway.
  3. Tonquin BeachA sunset-facing cove reached by an 800-metre gravel path with viewing platforms. Calmer water than the surf beaches.

Day 2: Ucluelet coast

  1. Wild Pacific Trail, Lighthouse LoopThe 2.6-kilometre Lighthouse Loop takes about 45 minutes along black volcanic rock and leads to Amphitrite Point Lighthouse.
  2. Ancient Cedars LoopPasses two red cedars estimated to be over 800 years old. The nearby Rocky Bluffs are a whale watching vantage point in migration season.
  3. Ucluelet AquariumA catch-and-release aquarium at the foot of Main Street with touch tanks. Fully indoor and seasonal, typically March through early December.
  4. Ucluelet Harbour WalkBoardwalks through the inner boat basin, with fishing vessels, herons, bald eagles and the occasional sea otter.
4days

Four days on the west coast

Tofino's beaches, a day on Clayoquot Sound, then Ucluelet's coast, bog boardwalk, harbour and a paddle.

Every season works. Summer is busiest, spring and fall are quieter, winter is for storm watching.

Day 1: Tofino beaches

  1. Long BeachPart of the Long Beach Unit of Pacific Rim National Park Reserve, between Tofino and Ucluelet. A Parks Canada pass is required.
  2. Chesterman BeachA 2.7-kilometre stretch of white sand shaped like a W. Walk it, or take a beginner surf lesson on the sandy bottom.
  3. Tonquin BeachA sunset-facing cove reached by an 800-metre gravel path with viewing platforms. Calmer water than the surf beaches.

Day 2: Clayoquot Sound by boat

  1. Hot Springs CoveReached only by water or air from Tofino: a 2-kilometre cedar boardwalk to five natural pools. A typical excursion lasts 6 to 7 hours.
  2. Whale and wildlife tour (shorter option)Many tours last about two to three hours. Main whale watching season is March through November, with gray whales in spring.

Day 3: Ucluelet shoreline

  1. Wild Pacific Trail, Lighthouse LoopThe 2.6-kilometre Lighthouse Loop takes about 45 minutes along black volcanic rock and leads to Amphitrite Point Lighthouse.
  2. Amphitrite Point LighthouseAn active beacon on the southern tip of the peninsula, good for spotting migrating whales in spring and sea lions on the offshore rocks.
  3. Ucluelet AquariumA catch-and-release aquarium at the foot of Main Street with touch tanks. Fully indoor and seasonal, typically March through early December.

Day 4: Bog, harbour and paddle

  1. Ucluelet Inlet paddleThe sheltered harbour suits beginners and a calm morning paddle past fishing docks, with bald eagles and kingfishers along the shore.
  2. Shorepine Bog TrailAn 800-metre boardwalk loop through a stunted forest in the national park. Stay on the boardwalk to protect the slow-growing plants.
  3. Whiskey Dock and Ucluelet Harbour WalkA historic pier with one of the best views of the harbour, next to boardwalks through the working boat basin.
2days

Two days at the Pacific Rim Whale Festival

The festival guide's own one-day plan followed by its weekend list: whale spotting, a tour, food, a talk and a coastal hike.

March. 2026 festival dates are March 14 to 21, and 2027 dates are March 13 to 20.

Day 1: Festival day plan

  1. Morning whale spotting or beach walkLearn to look for blows, dark backs and flukes. Bring binoculars, warm layers and patience, as sightings are never guaranteed.
  2. Midday festival event or marketLook for the Parade of Whales and Wonders, which opens the week, or the Chowder Chowdown, where local restaurants and chefs showcase coastal chowder.
  3. Afternoon Wild Pacific Trail or Long BeachCoastal walks in Ucluelet or the national park. Dress for cool, wet, windy March conditions.
  4. Dinner in Ucluelet or TofinoSupport local restaurants. Choose a mix of outdoor and indoor events in case the weather changes.

Day 2: Festival weekend list

  1. Whale watching tourSpring tours focus on the gray whale migration, from Barkley Sound or Clayoquot Sound. Reserve ahead, as tours and lodging are in demand.
  2. Food or art eventThe festival mixes free and ticketed events, including food, art, markets and family activities. Check ticket details first.
  3. Speaker or education eventTalks, workshops and education sessions on whales, marine mammals and coastal ecosystems. Some are free, others need registration.
  4. Coastal hikeWalk the Wild Pacific Trail or take a rainforest walk, and leave time to drive between the towns and the national park.

Whistler

Whistler is a mountain resort that works in every season, with a walkable village at the base. Towns: Whistler.

2days

Two days in Whistler

A classic first day in the village and on the gondola, then an outdoor adventure day chosen by season.

Winter (December to February) for snow; summer (June to August) for biking and lakes; fall is quieter.

Day 1: Classic Whistler

  1. Whistler VillageThe heart of the resort: walkable, with shops, restaurants, cafes, bars and lift bases, and easy to enjoy without a car.
  2. Peak 2 Peak GondolaConnects Whistler and Blackcomb mountains with big alpine views. Mountain temperatures are often 5 to 10°C cooler than the village, so dress in layers.
  3. Squamish Lil'wat Cultural CentreShares the living cultures, stories, art and traditions of the Squamish Nation and Lil'wat Nation. A good rainy-day stop and a strong choice for families.
  4. Dinner in the villagePatios and mountain views in summer, a cozy and festive village in winter.

Day 2: Outdoor Adventure

  1. Winter: ski, snowboard, snowshoe or cross-country skiWhistler Blackcomb has two mountains, ski schools and rentals. Lost Lake is a convenient area for Nordic skiing and snowshoeing.
  2. Summer: bike, hike, paddle, zipline or lake dayWhistler Mountain Bike Park offers lift-access downhill riding. Alpine trails, lakes and ziplines round out the summer choices.
  3. Scandinave Spa or a good mealOutdoor hot baths, cold plunges, steam rooms and saunas in a forest setting. Quiet and phone-free.
3days

Three days in Whistler

Village and mountain views, one big activity day of your choice, then a slow day of lake, culture and spa.

Winter (December to February) for snow; summer (June to August) for biking and lakes; fall is quieter.

Day 1: Village and Mountain Views

  1. Arrive in WhistlerStaying near the village gives the easiest access to restaurants, shops, lifts and tours.
  2. Explore the villageThe heart of the resort: walkable, with shops, restaurants, cafes, bars and lift bases, and easy to enjoy without a car.
  3. Peak 2 Peak GondolaConnects Whistler and Blackcomb mountains with big alpine views. Mountain temperatures are often 5 to 10°C cooler than the village, so dress in layers.
  4. A relaxed dinnerChoose a patio or a village restaurant and keep the evening easy.

Day 2: Big Activity Day (choose one)

  1. Ski or snowboardWhistler Blackcomb is known as North America's largest ski resort, with runs from beginner slopes to advanced bowls. Winter is busy, so book early.
  2. Mountain bikeWhistler Mountain Bike Park is famous for lift-access downhill, flow trails and jump lines. Start with lessons if you are new.
  3. Alpine hikeAlpine trails reached by gondola, with wildflowers, viewpoints and lakes in summer and early fall. Bring layers and proper footwear.
  4. ZiplineAdventure tours are easy to book, especially in summer. Check age, height, weight and weather rules before booking.
  5. Rafting or adventure tourRafting, ATV, off-road and other tours suit groups, families with older kids and first-time visitors who do not have their own gear.

Day 3: Slow Whistler Day

  1. Lost LakeClose to the village but peaceful and forested. Swim, picnic, walk, bike or paddle in summer; snowshoe or cross-country ski in winter.
  2. Valley TrailA paved path system connecting neighbourhoods, lakes, parks, viewpoints and the village. Walk, bike or run without driving.
  3. Audain Art Museum or Squamish Lil'wat Cultural CentreArt from British Columbia, including important Indigenous art and works by major regional artists. Good for rainy days and slower afternoons.
  4. Scandinave SpaOutdoor hot baths, cold plunges, steam rooms and saunas in a forest setting. Quiet and phone-free.
  5. A final village mealEnd the trip with dinner in the village.

Squamish

Squamish sits where the mountains meet Howe Sound, with big views, waterfalls and rugged outdoor adventure close to town. Towns: Squamish.

2days

Two days in Squamish

Views and waterfalls on day one, then one or two outdoor adventures of your choice on day two.

Year-round. Add Brackendale eagle watching in winter, best from late fall into winter.

Day 1: Classic Squamish Views

  1. Shannon FallsA tall waterfall dropping down granite cliffs after a short, easy walk from the parking area, beside Stawamus Chief Provincial Park.
  2. Sea to Sky GondolaRides up for views over Howe Sound, Shannon Falls and the Stawamus Chief, with easy trails, lookout platforms and a summit lodge at the top.
  3. Sky Pilot Suspension BridgeAt the top of the Sea to Sky Gondola, with mountain and Howe Sound views. A gondola ticket is needed; go early for fewer people.
  4. Howe Sound viewpointsSquamish sits where mountains meet the ocean. Enjoy the sound from the gondola, the Chief, highway viewpoints and waterfront areas.
  5. Downtown cafes or breweriesA growing food, coffee and craft beer scene. Stop for coffee, visit a brewery or browse the shops.

Day 2: Outdoor Adventure (choose one or two)

  1. Hike the Stawamus ChiefSquamish's most famous landmark. The hike is steep and challenging, with stairs, rocks, roots and three peaks; the First Peak is the shortest summit.
  2. Go mountain bikingOne of Canada's best mountain biking destinations, with forest trails, technical terrain and flow trails. Book a lesson or guided ride if new.
  3. Alice Lake Provincial ParkLakes, forest, walking trails, picnic areas and swimming in season. Nature without a hard climb; arrive early on warm summer days.
  4. Smoke Bluffs ParkBest known for rock climbing, with short walks, forest trails and small viewpoints close to town.
  5. Squamish EstuaryWhere river, marsh, mountain and ocean meet. Good for easy walks, birdwatching and quiet views.
  6. Railway Museum of British ColumbiaBritish Columbia's railway history and heritage, with train equipment and exhibits in a mountainside setting.
  7. Winter option: Brackendale eagle watchingBald eagles gather around the Squamish River during the salmon run. Bring binoculars and keep a respectful distance.

Squamish and Whistler

Squamish and Whistler sit on the same Sea to Sky Highway, so a rugged-to-resort trip pairs naturally. Towns: Squamish, Whistler.

2days

Two days from Squamish to Whistler

Waterfall, gondola and downtown Squamish on day one, then village, gondola, lake and culture in Whistler.

Both are great in summer. Whistler is best in winter for snow; Squamish is a quieter winter stop.

Day 1: Squamish

  1. Shannon FallsA tall waterfall dropping down granite cliffs after a short, easy walk from the parking area, beside Stawamus Chief Provincial Park.
  2. Sea to Sky GondolaRides up for views over Howe Sound, Shannon Falls and the Stawamus Chief, with easy trails, lookout platforms and a summit lodge at the top.
  3. Downtown SquamishCafes, breweries and shops, with a relaxed local feel. Stay in Squamish or continue to Whistler.
  4. Optional Chief hike or easy trailSquamish's most famous landmark. The hike is steep and challenging, with stairs, rocks, roots and three peaks; the First Peak is the shortest summit.

Day 2: Whistler

  1. Whistler VillageThe heart of the resort: walkable, with shops, restaurants, cafes, bars and lift bases, and easy to enjoy without a car.
  2. Peak 2 Peak GondolaConnects Whistler and Blackcomb mountains with big alpine views. Mountain temperatures are often 5 to 10°C cooler than the village, so dress in layers.
  3. Lost LakeClose to the village but peaceful and forested. Swim, picnic, walk, bike or paddle in summer; snowshoe or cross-country ski in winter.
  4. Squamish Lil'wat Cultural CentreShares the living cultures, stories, art and traditions of the Squamish Nation and Lil'wat Nation. A good rainy-day stop and a strong choice for families.
  5. Dinner in the villageA village meal to finish the trip.
3days

Three days from Squamish to Whistler

Squamish views, a Squamish adventure day, then a Whistler resort day with gondola, lake, shopping and spa.

Both are great in summer. Whistler is best in winter for snow; Squamish is a quieter winter stop.

Day 1: Squamish Nature and Views

  1. Shannon FallsA tall waterfall dropping down granite cliffs after a short, easy walk from the parking area, beside Stawamus Chief Provincial Park.
  2. Sea to Sky GondolaRides up for views over Howe Sound, Shannon Falls and the Stawamus Chief, with easy trails, lookout platforms and a summit lodge at the top.
  3. Easy hike or viewing platformsWalk easy trails at the top of the gondola and enjoy the lookout platforms over Howe Sound.
  4. Dinner in SquamishA growing local food and craft beer scene.

Day 2: Squamish Adventure

  1. Hike the Chief, climb, mountain bike or try a guided activitySquamish's most famous landmark. The hike is steep and challenging, with stairs, rocks, roots and three peaks; the First Peak is the shortest summit.
  2. Brewery or cafeSlow down between bigger outdoor activities with coffee or a local brewery.
  3. Drive to Whistler in the eveningWhistler is farther north along the Sea to Sky Highway than Squamish.

Day 3: Whistler Resort Day

  1. Peak 2 Peak GondolaConnects Whistler and Blackcomb mountains with big alpine views. Mountain temperatures are often 5 to 10°C cooler than the village, so dress in layers.
  2. Alpine walk or Lost LakeClose to the village but peaceful and forested. Swim, picnic, walk, bike or paddle in summer; snowshoe or cross-country ski in winter.
  3. Village shoppingWander between shops, galleries, gear stores and cafes in the walkable village.
  4. Spa, dinner or nightlifeOutdoor hot baths, cold plunges, steam rooms and saunas in a forest setting. Quiet and phone-free.

Vancouver Island south

Victoria, Sooke and Sidney sit at the southern tip of Vancouver Island: harbour landmarks, wild coast and a booktown on the Saanich Peninsula. Towns: Victoria, Sooke, Sidney.

3days

Three days on southern Vancouver Island

Victoria's harbour and museum, Sooke's river pools and beaches, then Butchart Gardens and the Sidney waterfront.

Day 1: Victoria harbour

  1. Inner HarbourThe heart of Victoria, framed by the Neo-baroque Parliament Buildings and the Fairmont Empress Hotel.
  2. BC Parliament BuildingsVictoria's premier architectural landmark: a grand Neo-baroque complex with a copper dome, on the Inner Harbour.
  3. Royal BC MuseumWalk through a replica 19th-century town, stand beneath a towering woolly mammoth and explore Indigenous cultures. Across from the Parliament Buildings.
  4. Fisherman's WharfColourful houseboats, fresh seafood kiosks and working fishing vessels in a living neighbourhood on the water.

Day 2: Sooke coast

  1. Sooke Potholes Provincial ParkDeep, smooth rock pools on the Sooke River, carved from volcanic rock by boulders in swirling currents.
  2. Whiffin SpitA 1.5-kilometre gravel bar into Sooke Harbour: a quick coastal walk with mountain and water views.
  3. French Beach Provincial ParkA long pebble-and-sand beach on the Juan de Fuca Strait, about 20 kilometres west of Sooke, popular with families.

Day 3: Sidney and the Saanich Peninsula

  1. The Butchart GardensA 55-acre garden grown from a former limestone quarry, a National Historic Site of Canada transformed by Jennie Butchart from 1904.
  2. Sidney WaterfrontA flat, barrier-free 2.5-kilometre paved walkway with public art and views of the Gulf Islands and Mount Baker.
  3. Beacon AvenueCanada's only Booktown, with independent shops and cafes along a tree-lined avenue sloping down to the Salish Sea.
5days

Five days on southern Vancouver Island

Two days in Victoria, two in Sooke with beaches and coastal trails, and a Saanich Peninsula day with gardens and the waterfront.

Day 1: Victoria landmarks

  1. Inner HarbourThe heart of Victoria, framed by the Neo-baroque Parliament Buildings and the Fairmont Empress Hotel.
  2. BC Parliament BuildingsVictoria's premier architectural landmark: a grand Neo-baroque complex with a copper dome, on the Inner Harbour.
  3. Royal BC MuseumWalk through a replica 19th-century town, stand beneath a towering woolly mammoth and explore Indigenous cultures. Across from the Parliament Buildings.

Day 2: Victoria parks, history and coast

  1. Beacon Hill ParkA 200-acre urban park of Garry Oak meadows, floral gardens and rugged coastline.
  2. Dallas Road BeachPebbly inlets and rocky outcrops linked by the 4-kilometre Dallas Road waterfront path, with a direct connection to the Salish Sea.
  3. Craigdarroch CastleA bonanza castle built by coal baron Robert Dunsmuir between 1887 and 1890, with 39 rooms, intricate woodwork and fine stained glass.
  4. ChinatownCanada's oldest Chinatown: a preserved enclave of narrow alleys and heritage buildings.
  5. Fisherman's WharfColourful houseboats, fresh seafood kiosks and working fishing vessels in a living neighbourhood on the water.

Day 3: Sooke harbour and river

  1. Sooke Harbour and waterfrontThe heart of the community, with T'Sou-ke Nation maritime history and a sheltered basin for paddling.
  2. Whiffin SpitA 1.5-kilometre gravel bar into Sooke Harbour: a quick coastal walk with mountain and water views.
  3. Sooke Potholes Provincial ParkDeep, smooth rock pools on the Sooke River, carved from volcanic rock by boulders in swirling currents.

Day 4: Sooke wild coast

  1. East Sooke Regional ParkOften called a miniature West Coast Trail, with over 50 kilometres of trails through 1,400 hectares of rugged coastal wilderness.
  2. French Beach Provincial ParkA long pebble-and-sand beach on the Juan de Fuca Strait, about 20 kilometres west of Sooke, popular with families.
  3. China BeachA fine-sand beach in Juan de Fuca Provincial Park, roughly 35 kilometres west of Sooke and a trailhead for the Juan de Fuca Marine Trail.

Day 5: Sidney and the Saanich Peninsula

  1. The Butchart GardensA 55-acre garden grown from a former limestone quarry, a National Historic Site of Canada transformed by Jennie Butchart from 1904.
  2. Sidney WaterfrontA flat, barrier-free 2.5-kilometre paved walkway with public art and views of the Gulf Islands and Mount Baker.
  3. Beacon AvenueCanada's only Booktown, with independent shops and cafes along a tree-lined avenue sloping down to the Salish Sea.
  4. Lochside Regional TrailA 29-kilometre multi-use path on a former railway line, from Swartz Bay Ferry Terminal to downtown Victoria.

Vancouver Island end to end

Start in Victoria, work north through the east coast towns to Campbell River, then cross to the west coast for Tofino and Ucluelet. Towns: Victoria, Chemainus, Nanaimo, Parksville, Campbell River, Tofino, Ucluelet.

7days

Seven days from Victoria to Ucluelet

Victoria, Chemainus and Nanaimo, the Parksville and Qualicum beaches, Campbell River, then Cathedral Grove, Tofino and Ucluelet.

Day 1: Victoria

  1. Inner HarbourThe heart of Victoria, framed by the Neo-baroque Parliament Buildings and the Fairmont Empress Hotel.
  2. BC Parliament BuildingsVictoria's premier architectural landmark: a grand Neo-baroque complex with a copper dome, on the Inner Harbour.
  3. Royal BC MuseumWalk through a replica 19th-century town, stand beneath a towering woolly mammoth and explore Indigenous cultures. Across from the Parliament Buildings.

Day 2: Chemainus and Nanaimo

  1. Chemainus MuralsOnce a struggling lumber town, now a walkable outdoor gallery of murals telling the region's Indigenous, forestry and community history.
  2. Nanaimo Harbourfront WalkwayA scenic multi-use shoreline path connecting downtown with the harbour and Saysutshun (Newcastle Island).
  3. Old City QuarterA revitalized historic district a few blocks uphill from the waterfront, with colourful storefronts, boutiques and locally owned eateries.

Day 3: the Parksville and Qualicum area

  1. Rathtrevor Beach Provincial ParkMassive tidal range and ancient Douglas fir forests, with a woodland sanctuary that opens onto a vast sandy beach.
  2. QualicumA long, curved shoreline with panoramic views of the Coast Mountains, calmer than the bustling park in Parksville.
  3. Old Country Market, CoombsThe Old Country Market, famous for its goats on the roof, is a complex of eclectic shops and international eateries.

Day 4: Campbell River

  1. Elk Falls Provincial ParkTen minutes from downtown: massive trees, a rushing river and a suspension bridge over a thundering waterfall.
  2. Discovery Fishing PierCanada's first saltwater fishing pier, stretching 180 metres into Discovery Passage.
  3. Rotary SeawalkA paved 13-kilometre pathway along the ocean, connecting the south of the city to downtown.

Day 6: Tofino and the national park

  1. Long BeachPart of the Long Beach Unit of Pacific Rim National Park Reserve, between Tofino and Ucluelet. A Parks Canada pass is required.
  2. Rainforest TrailTwo boardwalk loops through the old-growth forest of Pacific Rim National Park Reserve, right off the Pacific Rim Highway.
  3. Cox Bay BeachA 1.5-kilometre crescent bay between rocky headlands, widely considered Canada's premier surfing destination.

Day 7: Ucluelet

  1. Wild Pacific Trail, Lighthouse LoopThe 2.6-kilometre Lighthouse Loop takes about 45 minutes along black volcanic rock and leads to Amphitrite Point Lighthouse.
  2. Ucluelet AquariumA catch-and-release aquarium at the foot of Main Street with touch tanks. Fully indoor and seasonal, typically March through early December.
  3. Ucluelet Harbour WalkBoardwalks through the inner boat basin, with fishing vessels, herons, bald eagles and the occasional sea otter.

Vancouver Island mountain biking

Vancouver Island is a biking road trip: pedal-access trail riding in a string of trail towns, each with its own style. Towns: Victoria, Nanaimo, Parksville, Campbell River.

3days

Three days of Vancouver Island mountain biking

One day each in Nanaimo, Cumberland and Cowichan: technical trails, classic Island singletrack, and flow or downhill-style riding.

Spring through fall. Fall is one of the best seasons, and spring dirt can be very good but wet.

Day 1: Nanaimo

  1. NanaimoTechnical riding with rocky sections, roots, climbs and ridgelines across several zones, including Doumont, Westwood, South Benson and Extension Ridge.

Day 2: Cumberland

  1. Cumberland and Comox ValleyOften considered the heart of Vancouver Island mountain biking: forest singletrack, loam, flow trails and technical descents beside a bike-town village.

Day 3: Cowichan Valley

  1. Cowichan ValleyA region of several riding zones: Cobble Hill for easier riding, Mount Tzouhalem for variety, Maple Mountain for technical forest and Mount Prevost for advanced downhill.
5days

Five days of Vancouver Island mountain biking

Spring through fall. Fall is one of the best seasons, and spring dirt can be very good but wet.

Day 1: Victoria

  1. Victoria and HartlandCity-access riding at Hartland Mountain Bike Park, rocky, rooty and technical. Not a soft beginner path.

Day 2: Cowichan Valley

  1. Cowichan ValleyA region of several riding zones: Cobble Hill for easier riding, Mount Tzouhalem for variety, Maple Mountain for technical forest and Mount Prevost for advanced downhill.

Day 3: Nanaimo

  1. NanaimoTechnical riding with rocky sections, roots, climbs and ridgelines across several zones, including Doumont, Westwood, South Benson and Extension Ridge.

Day 4: Parksville and Hammerfest

  1. Parksville Qualicum and HammerfestHammerfest trail network near Englishman River Falls Provincial Park: old-school singletrack, technical ridges, flow, bridges and boardwalks.

Day 5: Cumberland

  1. Cumberland and Comox ValleyOften considered the heart of Vancouver Island mountain biking: forest singletrack, loam, flow trails and technical descents beside a bike-town village.
