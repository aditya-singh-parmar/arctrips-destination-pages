Surfing in Tofino

Something breaks here nearly every day of the year. Which beach you drive to depends on the swell, the tide and how long you have been surfing, and those three things are what this page is about.

When to go

The swell is biggest in winter, and smallest exactly when you want to learn

There is almost always something to ride, so the question is not whether there are waves but how big they are when you arrive. The strip below is swell size, not weather. Beginners want the short bars. Experienced surfers want the tall ones.

Fall

September to November is the turn. The first winter swells arrive from a favourable angle, October and November bring the most frequent good alignments at Cox Bay, and South Chesterman opens its season in September.

Winter

Storm season sets in by November and usually holds until February or March. Three to six metre seas, gales to 70 km/h, and the rare days when Incinerator Rock is one of the best big wave spots in North America.

Spring

The northwest storm track is still running but easing. South Chesterman keeps working through to May, the light is getting longer, and the beaches are far emptier than they will be in July.

Summer

The smallest, cleanest waves of the year, which is exactly what a first lesson needs. The schools are busy, so book ahead. The water is still 8 to 15 C, so the wetsuit does not come off.

Where to surf

Seven breaks, ordered by what the wave asks of you

They sit inside twelve miles of the same coast, so you can look at all of them in a morning. What separates them is what is on the bottom, the angle each one takes swell from, and what it turns into when a storm lands. The order runs from the beach the schools teach on to the point a handful of people ride a few days a year.

Sand underneath, and where a first lesson happens

01 to 02

Beach breaks over soft sand. Nothing hard to hit, and enough whitewater to stand up in.

Beginner to intermediateLong mellow lefts, soft sand

North Chesterman Beach

This is the schools' home break, and the reason most people's first wave in Canada happens here. Beginners are taken to the north end, where the whitewater is forgiving and the sand is soft.

The long mellow left point unfurls better at mid to higher tides and runs about 200 yards down the beach. It favours a short to mid length groveler around 7 feet, or a nimble longboard. Less steep and far less hollow than the reef break at the southern end of the same beach.

Swell window: knee-high peelers up to overhead sets of 5 to 7 feet at a long 16 to 18 second interval, arriving on a 240 to 270 degree northwesterly fetch.

Good to know. Alicia Bennett of Surf Sister puts the best lefts 2 to 3 hours either side of the daily high tide, between 9am and 3pm, with the most push on new and full moon cycles. Chesterman shifts end to end as its bars and channels move, so the day's peak zone is not always in the same place.

All levels on an ordinary day16 km of sand, room for everyone

Long Beach

Long Beach runs roughly 16 km inside Pacific Rim National Park Reserve, north from Ucluelet to Grice Bay. It faces due west and gathers swell straight off the open Pacific, so there is nearly always something breaking somewhere along it.

On an ordinary day it is the least crowded surf in the area for the simple reason that there is so much of it. Pick a peak, walk to it, and paddle out.

Swell arrives from a due westerly direction. Its notorious break, Incinerator Rock, is listed further down: on most days it is not what you are here for.

Good to know. The same open exposure that makes it consistent makes rip currents a real hazard, and if you drift it is a long walk back along the sand. Keep a fixed point on the shore in view and check it often.

Beach breaks with real power when the swell lands

03 to 04

Still sand underneath, but these want a swell, and they get heavy once they have one.

IntermediateOne of the longest rides on this coast

Cox Bay

Cox Bay sits just short of the Pacific Rim National Park beaches and is where the shortboards come out when a real swell shows up. Locals rate it as one of the longest rides in the region when everything aligns.

Swell sweeps in from the west-southwest around 250 degrees and refracts off the islands lying offshore. The bay draws a crowd on overhead 6 to 10 ft cycles at 13 to 22 second intervals, on the falling afternoon tide after 1pm.

Alicia Bennett of Surf Sister rates it highest on a lower tide of 3 to 5 ft with a moderate south-southwesterly windswell in the 8 to 10 foot range at 15 seconds. The left can run 400 yards down the point towards the creek mouth when the sandbars and rips sync up, with easy speed and open walls.

October and November bring the most frequent ideal alignments, as the first winter swells arrive from a favourable angle.

Good to know. On smaller days the sheltered, sandy-bottomed cove is the warmest water of any break here, thin enough for a 3mm suit while the rest of the coast still wants a 4/3.

Florencia Beach North

About 12 minutes drive from Ucluelet, the north end of Florencia Beach refracts and grooms swell arriving from several angles into clean peaks. Alicia Bennett of Surf Sister, who has spent twenty years reading this coastline, calls it an A-frame left point that turns on with a south or westerly windswell pushing in at a moderate 6 to 8 feet at 14 to 17 second intervals, on a mid rising tide of 6 to 8 ft.

Interval is what makes or breaks it. Longer groundswells over 17 seconds running bigger than 8 ft tend to close out, and short period windswell around 11 seconds does not wrap around the point coherently.

It lights up several hours either side of the midday high tide, from October through April, when low pressure systems track down from the Gulf of Alaska on roughly a 260 degree line.

Good to know. This is the one to check first during a storm cycle. It is the most consistent break in the area through the peak months, which also means you will not have it to yourself.

Reef, rock and exposure

05 to 07

Something hard underneath, or nothing at all between you and the open Pacific. These ask for experience, not enthusiasm.

AdvancedReef and point break, hollow, both ways

South Chesterman Beach

The most popular high performance wave in the Tofino area, and the least forgiving. A reef and point break due west of town next to the airport, throwing thick hollow barrels and steep walls that break both left and right on swells arriving 220 to 250 degrees from several storm tracks at once.

It is a magnet for advanced shortboarders and confident longboarders. Local pros Pete Devries and his brother Dan built their reputations on these walls, which demand experience with shifty peaks, strong rips and a shallow reef sitting just under the surface.

Devries puts the season from September through May as the northwest storms kick in, with the most rippable sizes running 6 to 8 feet at 15 seconds up to 12 foot faces in heavier weather. Interval matters more than height: a steady pulse around 15 seconds shapes the wave best, without the long flat spells between sets that cool a wetsuit off.

Aim for the mid rising tide, from 3 to 5 feet up to the daily high, between 10am and 2pm, when 220 to 250 degree swell lights the reef up in front of the beach.

Good to know. Above 8 feet the shallow reef becomes extremely dangerous, and it is not a place to discover that. That size is for a handful of people here and nobody else.

Expert, a few days a yearBig wave left point

Incinerator Rock

The rocky left point at Wolf Creek, on Long Beach. It needs one specific setup: an extreme low pressure system tracking northeast well offshore towards Haida Gwaii, sending 15 to 20 ft swell in at 270 to 290 degrees so it wraps into the point.

Joe Martin, who started surfing this coast in 1971, puts that at maybe four or five days a year. On those days the faces run to 25 feet and it is one of the best big wave spots in North America. He is equally clear about the price of entry: nothing thinner than a 7mm suit, and proper safety support in the water with you.

If the forecast is not showing that exact alignment, Incinerator Rock is somewhere you park and walk onto Long Beach, not somewhere you paddle out.

Good to know. Cold is the constraint people underestimate, not size. Martin's phrase for it is the bone chilling Pacific, and it is what ends most sessions here long before the surfer is ready to stop.

Advanced, and rarelyRocky reef, remote, a few days a season

Wickaninnish Beach

North of Long Beach past Florencia, inside Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. It gets far less attention than Tofino's main breaks and holds both beach breaks and point break opportunities for surfers who already know this coast.

It comes alive a few times a season, when an intense northwest Pacific storm track sends swell directly ashore between 280 and 310 degrees. Local surfers Jeremiah Hermann and Gary Young have had 15 to 20 foot walls there, breaking over an unforgiving rocky reef along the beach.

Hermann's recipe: a winter day carrying over 10 feet of swell energy at 14 to 16 second intervals, a mid rising tide stage up to 6 feet, and a light easterly offshore breeze.

Good to know. Some of the point access crosses private and First Nations ground. Respect the signs and the people who live here, and treat any trip out there with care. Very few surfers ever see this place at its full size.

Getting in the water

Two schools, and no reason to bring a board

Board and wetsuit rental is easy to find in town, so most visitors arrive with nothing. Lessons run in the whitewater at the north end of Chesterman, where the sand is soft and the waves are patient. Start on a board that feels far too big: standing up is the whole of the first lesson, and a bigger board makes that much easier.

Lessons and rentalsYear round, every ability

Pacific Surf School

Runs surf and stand up paddle lessons all year, set to the ability of whoever turns up, from a first time on a board to a session working on turns.

It also rents surfboards, stand up paddle boards and wetsuits in every season, which is the practical answer for anyone who wants to go out twice without buying anything.

Lessons and coachingTeaching here since 1999

Surf Sister

Certified instructors teaching men, women, boys and girls, running since 1999 and long enough established that its owner, Alicia Bennett, is one of the voices quoted on the breaks above.

Beginner lessons are the bulk of it, but intermediate and advanced sessions run too, which is the harder thing to find here once you can already stand up.

Before you paddle out

Four things that decide how the session goes

The waves are rarely the problem. Cold, rips, what is under the water and the tide window are what turn a good forecast into a bad afternoon.

Dress for 8 to 15 C, in August too

The water sits between 8 and 15 C every month of the year. A 4/3 wetsuit with boots, and often a hood, is standard here in summer as much as in winter. All of it rents in town, so there is no reason to fly with a suit.

Rips are what catch people out

The sandbars move, and the channels between them run straight back out to sea. At South Chesterman local surfers name the rips alongside the reef as the reason it is an experienced surfer's wave. On Long Beach the exposure is the same and the walk back is far longer.

Know what is under you

Chesterman Reef sits shallow enough to be dangerous above 8 feet. Wickaninnish breaks over rock. Incinerator Rock is a rocky point. North Chesterman, Long Beach and Cox Bay are sand, which is where a mistake costs you nothing but your dignity.

Every break here has a tide window

Cox Bay wants a lower tide of 3 to 5 ft. South Chesterman wants the mid rising tide up to the daily high. North Chesterman peels best 2 to 3 hours either side of high. Florencia wants a mid rising tide of 6 to 8 ft. Check the tide before you drive, not after.

Questions

Asked before people book a lesson

Open the ones you need. Everything here comes from surfers who live on this coast.

Can a complete beginner surf in Tofino?

Yes, and most people who learn to surf in Canada learn here. Summer is the season for it, because the waves are at their smallest and cleanest. Lessons run in the whitewater at the north end of Chesterman Beach, where the sand is soft and the wave is forgiving. Book a lesson rather than renting a board and working it out yourself: an instructor puts you in the right part of the beach on the right tide, which is most of the difficulty removed.

What wetsuit do I need?

A 4/3 with boots, and often a hood. The water runs 8 to 15 C every month of the year, August included, so this is not a summer versus winter decision. On small days in the sheltered cove at Cox Bay a 3mm is enough, and that is the warmest water on this coast. At the other end, for the biggest days at Incinerator Rock, Joe Martin says nothing under 7mm.

When is the surf biggest?

October to February. Storm season sets in by November and usually holds until February or March, bringing three to six metre seas and gales up to 70 km/h. South Chesterman's season runs September through May, and Florencia is at its most consistent from October through April. If you want size, come in winter and dress for it.

Do I have to bring a board?

No. Board and wetsuit rental is easy to find in town, and both schools rent as well as teach. If it is your first time, take the biggest board you are offered. Standing up is the whole first lesson, and a larger board makes that far easier.

Which break should I stay away from until I know the coast?

South Chesterman above 8 feet, where the reef sits shallow and gets extremely dangerous. Incinerator Rock at any size, because it only breaks properly on the four or five days a year that the swell lines up and those are 25 foot faces. Wickaninnish, which breaks over rock and is remote enough that help is a long way off. All three are worth watching from the sand.

Where do the surf schools teach?

The north end of Chesterman Beach. It is the mellowest of the beach breaks, the bottom is sand, and the whitewater there is enough to stand up in without being enough to hurt you. The same stretch keeps working as you improve: its long left runs about 200 yards at mid to high tide.

Does Tofino break all year?

Nearly every day of it. That is the reason people call it Canada's surf capital: the coast faces straight into the North Pacific's storm track, so swell of some size is almost always arriving. What changes month to month is how big it is, not whether it is there.
