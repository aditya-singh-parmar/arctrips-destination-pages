Ucluelet, Vancouver Island

Hiking and trails in Ucluelet

Coastal loops on the headland the town sits on, rainforest boardwalks up the highway, and two trips that need permits before you go.

Walks
20
On the peninsula
9
In the national park
9

Almost everything here is easy. The Wild Pacific Trail runs along the edge of the Ucluelet peninsula on gravel paths with benches and viewpoints, and its Lighthouse Loop is the short section most people walk first. If it is raining, the boardwalks in the national park are the better call.

The list below runs from the walks you can reach from town, out to the park, and finishes with the two that are trips rather than walks.

On the Ucluelet peninsula

Nine walks on the headland the town sits on. Most are a few minutes from a parking spot, and the Wild Pacific Trail links a lot of them together.

  1. 01

    Wild Pacific Trail

    The Wild Pacific Trail is the most famous trail in Ucluelet. It follows the edge of the Ucluelet Peninsula and gives visitors dramatic views of the open Pacific Ocean, rocky shoreline, Barkley Sound, and the Broken Group Islands.

    This trail is one of the best reasons to visit Ucluelet. It is easy to follow, well-loved, and good for most visitors. You can walk just one short section or spend more time exploring different parts.

    The trail passes through coastal rainforest, old trees, salal, ferns, rocky viewpoints, and storm-watching areas. In winter, it is one of the best places to watch big waves from a safe distance.

    Good forEasy hiking · Ocean views · Storm watching · Photography · Families · Bird watching · Whale watching from shore · Short walks or longer walks

    Good to know. Stay on the marked trail and behind safety barriers. The ocean is powerful, especially during storms and high tide.

  2. 02

    Lighthouse Loop

    The Lighthouse Loop is the most popular section of the Wild Pacific Trail. It is a short loop near Amphitrite Point Lighthouse, at the southern end of Ucluelet.

    This trail gives you big ocean views without a hard hike. You can see rocky coastline, crashing waves, wind-shaped trees, and the lighthouse. It is one of the best short walks in Ucluelet for first-time visitors.

    Good forFirst-time visitors · Families · Lighthouse views · Ocean photos · Storm watching · Whale watching in season · Short walks

    Good to know. This is one of the easiest and most rewarding walks in town. Bring a jacket because it can be windy.

  3. 03

    Artist Loops

    The Artist Loops are part of the Wild Pacific Trail system. These small loop sections lead to scenic viewpoints, benches, forest corners, and quiet spots for looking out over the coast.

    They are called Artist Loops because the views are perfect for sketching, painting, photography, or simply sitting still.

    Good forQuiet viewpoints · Benches · Photography · Slow walks · Nature journaling · Ocean views

    Good to know. These loops are short, but they make the trail feel richer and more peaceful.

  4. 04

    Ancient Cedars Trail

    The Ancient Cedars Trail is a short forest section of the Wild Pacific Trail system. It is a good choice if you want a calmer rainforest walk instead of open ocean views.

    You can see large trees, moss, ferns, and thick west coast forest. It is easy, peaceful, and good for people who want to slow down.

    Good forForest walking · Big trees · Rainy days · Families · Quiet nature time · Short hikes

    Good to know. The Wild Pacific Trail Society describes a stand of trees on the trail as over 800 years old, making this a special stop for rainforest lovers.

  5. 05

    Bog Interpretive Loop

    The Bog Interpretive Loop is a short nature walk that shows a different side of Ucluelet. Instead of big ocean views, this route focuses on bog plants, wetland habitat, and small details in the landscape.

    It is a good choice for curious visitors, families, and people who like learning about ecosystems.

    Good forEasy walking · Nature learning · Kids · Bog plants · Photography · A short stop

    Good to know. Stay on the trail to protect fragile plants.

  6. 06

    Big Beach Park Trail

    Big Beach Park is a small beach and park area in Ucluelet. It is a nice place for a short walk, picnic, or beach stop before or after hiking the Wild Pacific Trail.

    This area connects well with nearby Wild Pacific Trail sections. It is not a long hike, but it is useful for families and anyone who wants easy beach access.

    Good forShort walks · Beach access · Picnics · Families · Sunset stops · Tide watching

    Good to know. Watch the tide and stay back from slippery rocks.

  7. 07

    Terrace Beach Trail

    Terrace Beach is a small beach in Ucluelet near the Wild Pacific Trail area. It is not a long hike, but it is a nice short beach walk or add-on stop.

    The beach feels quieter and more tucked away than the larger beaches near Tofino. It can be a good place for photos, a calm break, or a quick nature stop.

    Good forShort walks · Quiet beach time · Photography · Families · A peaceful stop near town

    Good to know. Check tide and wave conditions before exploring near rocks.

  8. 08

    Little Beach Trail

    Little Beach is another easy beach stop in Ucluelet. It is close to town and works well for a short visit, especially if you do not want a long hike.

    This is a good option for families, quick beach walks, and relaxed shoreline views.

    Good forEasy beach access · Families · Short walks · Picnics · Calm shoreline views · A quick stop

    Good to know. Like all west coast beaches, watch the tide and stay away from slippery rocks.

  9. 09

    Spring Cove Mudflats

    Spring Cove Mudflats is a quieter nature area in Ucluelet. It is a good place for bird watching, peaceful walking, and seeing a different habitat from the open ocean coast.

    Mudflats are important feeding areas for birds, especially during migration. This is a good place to slow down and look closely.

    Good forBird watching · Quiet nature time · Families · Easy walking · Photography · Learning about coastal habitats

    Good to know. Bring binoculars if you like birds.

In Pacific Rim National Park Reserve

Nine more up the highway between Ucluelet and Tofino. Boardwalks in the rainforest, stairs down to the beaches, and one paved pathway that runs the length of it.

  1. 10

    Rainforest Trail

    The Rainforest Trail is one of the best short hikes near Ucluelet. It is in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve and is known for boardwalks, huge trees, moss, ferns, and deep green forest.

    There are two short loops on opposite sides of the highway. You can do one loop or both.

    Good forBig trees · Boardwalks · Families · Rainy days · First-time visitors · Easy rainforest photos

    Good to know. The boardwalk can be slippery when wet. Wear shoes with good grip.

  2. 11

    Shorepine Bog Trail

    Shorepine Bog Trail is a short, flat boardwalk trail in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. It feels very different from the Rainforest Trail.

    Instead of huge trees, you will see twisted shore pines, bog plants, moss, and open wetland scenery.

    Good forEasy walking · Kids · Bog plants · Short stops · Rainy days · Learning about wetlands

    Good to know. Stay on the boardwalk to protect the fragile bog.

  3. 12

    South Beach Trail

    South Beach Trail starts near the Kwisitis Visitor Centre and Wickaninnish Beach area. It is a short coastal trail that leads through forest toward a rocky beach.

    This trail is a good choice if you want a quick walk with strong ocean sounds and dramatic coastal views.

    Good forShort hikes · Rocky beach views · Ocean sounds · Photography · A stop near the visitor centre · Pairing with Wickaninnish Beach

    Good to know. South Beach is better for viewing than swimming. Stay back from waves and wet rocks.

  4. 13

    Nuu-chah-nulth Trail

    The Nuu-chah-nulth Trail is one of the more meaningful walks in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. It connects rainforest, boardwalk, beach views, and cultural learning.

    This trail is longer and more active than the shortest boardwalk walks, but it is still manageable for many visitors.

    Good forRainforest walking · Ocean views · Learning · Photography · A more active nature walk · Visitors who want more than a quick stop

    Good to know. Expect stairs, boardwalks, and uneven sections. Take your time.

  5. 14

    Willowbrae Trail

    Willowbrae Trail is closer to the Ucluelet side of Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. It leads through forest and connects toward Florencia Bay and Halfmoon Bay.

    This is a good trail if you want a more active walk and beach access. It has stairs, and the return walk can feel harder than the walk down.

    Good forForest walking · Beach access · Florencia Bay · Halfmoon Bay · Photography · A quieter national park hike

    Good to know. Check tide times before spending time on the beach.

  6. 15

    Halfmoon Bay Trail

    Halfmoon Bay Trail branches from the Willowbrae area and leads down to a small, quiet beach. It is a beautiful stop if you want something calmer and less busy.

    The beach feels tucked away, but the stairs can make the return feel more tiring.

    Good forQuiet beach stops · Forest and shoreline scenery · Photography · A peaceful walk · More active families

    Good to know. Be careful near driftwood and waves. Do not get trapped by rising tide.

  7. 16

    Florencia Bay Trail

    Florencia Bay is one of the most beautiful beaches near Ucluelet. It is known for a quiet feel, sand cliffs, and wide shoreline views.

    The trail to Florencia Bay includes forest and stairs. It is worth the effort if you want a beach that feels less busy than Long Beach.

    Good forQuiet beach walks · Sand cliff views · Photography · Low-tide exploring · A less crowded national park beach · A scenic stop near Ucluelet

    Good to know. The stairs can feel harder on the way back up. Wear comfortable shoes.

  8. 17

    Radar Hill

    Radar Hill is a short viewpoint stop in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. It is not a long hike, but it is worth adding if you want a quick view and a little history.

    The hill was used as a radar station during World War II. Today, visitors come for forest, ocean, and island views.

    Good forQuick stops · Easy walking · Viewpoints · Families · History · Photography

    Good to know. This is more of a viewpoint walk than a full hike.

  9. 18

    ʔapsčiik t̓ašii Multi-Use Pathway

    ʔapsčiik t̓ašii, pronounced “ups-cheek ta-shee,” is a paved multi-use pathway in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. It runs through the Long Beach Unit and connects many areas between the Tofino and Ucluelet side of the park.

    It is good for walking, biking, and running. It is not a wilderness hike, but it is a great way to move through the park without driving to every stop.

    Good forWalking · Biking · Running · Families · Longer active days · Exploring Pacific Rim National Park

    Good to know. The path is paved, but there are hills and curves. Share the route with cyclists and other users.

Two that need planning first

Neither of these is a day out from town. One needs permits and a reservation, the other needs a boat.

  1. 19

    West Coast Trail

    The West Coast Trail is part of Pacific Rim National Park Reserve, but it is very different from Ucluelet’s easy day hikes.

    This is a famous multi-day backpacking route with mud, ladders, beach walking, tide timing, cable cars, and remote camping. It is not a casual day hike.

    Good forExperienced hikers · Multi-day backpacking · Remote coastal travel · Adventure trips · Serious planning

    Good to know. You need permits, reservations, fitness, gear, and tide knowledge. This is not a beginner trail.

  2. 20

    Broken Group Islands Walks

    The Broken Group Islands are in Barkley Sound and are part of Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. They are not regular hiking trails you drive to. They are reached by boat or kayak.

    If you are on a kayak camping trip or boat-supported visit, you may do short island walks near campsites or beaches.

    Good forKayak camping trips · Short island exploring · Wildlife watching · Remote beach walks · Experienced travelers · Guided trips

    Good to know. These areas need planning, proper permits, and respect for park rules.

Questions

What people ask before they walk

Four answers from the Ucluelet hiking guide.

What is the best hike in Ucluelet?

The Wild Pacific Trail is the best-known hike in Ucluelet. The Lighthouse Loop is the best short section for first-time visitors because it has lighthouse views, ocean views, and easy walking.

Is the Wild Pacific Trail easy?

Yes. Most of the Wild Pacific Trail is easy and well-marked, with gravel paths, viewpoints, and benches. Some areas may have stairs or uneven ground.

How long is the Wild Pacific Trail?

The Wild Pacific Trail has several sections. You can walk a short loop, such as the Lighthouse Loop, or combine sections for a longer walk.

Can you see whales from the Wild Pacific Trail?

Yes, sometimes. The Wild Pacific Trail and Amphitrite Point Lighthouse area are good places to scan for whales, especially during migration season. Bring binoculars.
