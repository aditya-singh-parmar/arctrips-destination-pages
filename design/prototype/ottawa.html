1. Parliament Hill and Buildings

Parliament Hill is the political heart of Canada, a massive neo-Gothic complex that serves as both the seat of the federal government and the nation’s most prominent public square. While the iconic Centre Block is currently closed for a long-term restoration (projected to last until the early 2030s), the Hill is more active than ever with new immersive experiences and relocated legislative chambers.

House of Commons (West Block): Visit the temporary home of the lower chamber. It features a spectacular, modern glass-roofed courtyard built into the historic stone shell of the West Block.

Senate (Senate of Canada Building): Located just off the Hill (2 Rideau St.), this former train station is a Beaux-Arts masterpiece. The tour showcases the opulent red-and-gold Senate chamber.

Rideau Canal National Historic Site

A UNESCO World Heritage Site, the Rideau Canal is an engineering marvel stretching 202 km from Ottawa to Kingston. It is the only 19th-century canal in North America still operating on its original route with most of its original structures intact.

Ottawa Locks (Downtown): Located right beside Parliament Hill, this flight of eight massive stone locks connects the canal to the Ottawa River.

Watch the Step-Up: In the summer, you can watch Parks Canada staff hand-crank the iron winches to raise and lower boats, a process that hasn't changed since 1832.

Canadian War Museum

The Canadian War Museum, located on the banks of the Ottawa River, stands as a significant landmark in Ottawa. It is one of the world’s most respected museums of military history known not only for its extensive collection of tanks, artillery, and aircraft but also for its powerful, brutally honest depiction of the human experience of conflict.

Canadian Experience Galleries: These four main galleries take you on a chronological journey from early Indigenous warfare and the War of 1812 through the World Wars and into the Cold War and modern peacekeeping.

LeBreton Gallery: A massive, sun-filled hall of heavy metal featuring scores of tanks, military vehicles, and artillery. You’ll find everything from a First World War ambulance to a T-34 tank and a CF-5 fighter jet suspended from the ceiling.

National Gallery of Canada

The National Gallery of Canada, designed by renowned architect Moshe Safdie, is one of the most significant art institutions in North America. Located in a stunning structure of glass and pink granite, it houses a vast collection that spans from indigenous artifacts and traditional Canadian art to European masterpieces and cutting-edge contemporary works.

Iconic Architecture: The building itself is a masterpiece, featuring the Scotiabank Great Hall, a soaring glass pavilion that offers what many consider the best panoramic view of Parliament Hill and the Ottawa River.

Maman Sculpture: Visitors are greeted outside by Louise Bourgeois’s famous 30-foot bronze spider sculpture, a world-known landmark that has become a favorite photo spot for tourists.

Notre Dame Cathedral Basilica

Located across from the National Gallery, the Notre Dame Cathedral Basilica is the oldest and largest church in Ottawa. This National Historic Site is a stunning example of Neo-Gothic architecture, famous for its twin silver spires that have defined the city's skyline since the mid-19th century.

Breathtaking Interior: While the exterior is impressive, the interior is truly spectacular. It features a deep blue vaulted ceiling studded with thousands of gold stars, intricately carved wooden altars, and over 200 statues of religious figures.

Artistic Heritage: Visitors often marvel at the brilliant stained-glass windows that depict various biblical scenes and the massive pipe organ, which is one of the most significant in the country.

Rideau Hall

Rideau Hall is the official residence and workplace of the Governor General of Canada. Located on a vast 79-acre estate just minutes from downtown Ottawa, it serves as a site for state ceremonies, international welcomes, and honors for deserving Canadians. Unlike many other world leader residences, the grounds and much of the building are open to the public, fostering a sense of a home for all Canadians.

Guided Tours of the Residence: Explore the elegant State rooms where the Governor General fulfills official duties and welcomes dignitaries. You'll see incredible collections of Canadian art and furniture.

Grounds and Gardens: Wander through the meticulously landscaped heritage gardens. The estate features over 10,000 trees, including many planted by world leaders and royalty during state visits.

Canadian Museum of Nature

Housed in a magnificent building that looks like a castle, the Canadian Museum of Nature is Canada's national natural history and sciences museum. Often referred to as a castle of nature, it combines historic Scottish baronial-style architecture with modern glass additions to create one of the most visually striking landmarks in the city.

Fossil Gallery: This world-class exhibit features more than 30 complete dinosaur skeletons and hundreds of fossils, including the iconic horned dinosaurs and a dramatic Death of a Dinosaur scene.

Water Gallery: Centered around a massive, 19-meter blue whale skeleton suspended from the ceiling, this gallery explores the diverse life found in Canada's oceans and freshwater systems.

Good to know. Note that the museum is typically closed on Tuesdays during the off-peak season (September to May), so plan your visit for a different day of the week.

ByWard Market

ByWard Market is one of Ottawa’s most vibrant and historic areas, offering a blend of fresh food, local goods, and cultural experiences. Established in 1826 by Colonel John By, it has long been a hub for farmers, artisans, and locals, making it one of the oldest and most well-known public markets in Canada. The area is famous for its eclectic mix of open-air farmer stalls, artisanal boutiques, and a high concentration of some of the city's most beloved restaurants and pubs.

Culinary Landmarks: No visit is complete without stopping at the original BeaverTails stand to try the world-famous cinnamon and sugar fried pastry.

Artisan Shopping: The main market building and surrounding streets are filled with local vendors selling everything from handmade jewelry and maple syrup to fresh seasonal produce and flowers.

National War Memorial

The National War Memorial in Ottawa stands as a powerful tribute to Canadian soldiers who have served and sacrificed in wars and peacekeeping missions. Located in Confederation Square, it is a monumental symbol of Canada's military history and resilience. Originally built to commemorate the First World War, it was rededicated over the years to include those who served in the Second World War, the Korean War, and the war in Afghanistan.

Response Statues: The memorial features 22 bronze figures representing the different branches of the Canadian forces during World War I, shown passing through a granite arch topped by the allegorical figures of Peace and Freedom.

Tomb of the Unknown Soldier: At the foot of the memorial lies the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier, containing the remains of an unidentified Canadian soldier from a cemetery near Vimy Ridge, France.

Peace Tower

The Peace Tower is the most iconic feature of the Canadian parliamentary complex and a national symbol of Canada. Standing at 92.2 meters (302 feet), this stone bell tower serves as a focal point of Parliament Hill and acts as a Victorian High Gothic monument to the sacrifices made by Canadians during the First World War.

Memorial Chamber: Located directly above the front arch of the tower, this solemn room houses the Books of Remembrance, which list the names of every Canadian who has died in military service.

Carillon: The tower contains 53 bells of varying sizes. The Dominion Carillonneur performs regular live recitals, filling the downtown core with music that ranges from classical pieces to contemporary arrangements.

11. Fairmont Château Laurier

Often referred to as the third chamber of Parliament, the Fairmont Château Laurier is a magnificent French Gothic Revival castle that has anchored downtown Ottawa since 1912. Built by the Grand Trunk Railway, its history is famously intertwined with the Titanic; the hotel's visionary, Charles Melville Hays, perished on the ill-fated ship just days before the scheduled grand opening. Today, it remains a focal point of Canadian political and social life, having hosted royalty, world leaders, and celebrities for over a century.

Design and Materials: The castle features a limestone façade, distinctive copper roofing, and soaring turrets. Inside, you'll find Belgian marble floors, hand-moulded plaster decorations, and original Tiffany stained-glass windows.

Karsh Connection: Famed portrait photographer Yousuf Karsh lived and worked in the hotel for 18 years. Several of his world-famous portraits, including his iconic Roaring Lion photo of Winston Churchill, are displayed in the hotel's public spaces.

Canadian Museum of History

The Canadian Museum of History is Canada's most visited museum, located on the Gatineau side of the Ottawa River. Its stunning architecture, designed by Douglas Cardinal, features sweeping curves inspired by the Canadian landscape. The museum is dedicated to chronicling the human history of Canada and the cultural diversity of its people.

Grand Hall: This iconic space offers a breathtaking view of Parliament Hill across the river and houses the world's largest indoor collection of totem poles.

Canadian History Hall: A signature gallery that tells the story of Canada through three distinct eras, featuring over 1,500 authentic artifacts and diverse perspectives.

Diefenbunker: Canada's Cold War Museum

Located in the rural village of Carp, just outside Ottawa, the Diefenbunker: Canada's Cold War Museum is a massive four-story underground bunker designed to withstand a nuclear blast. Built in secret between 1959 and 1961, it was intended to house the top officials of the Canadian government and military in the event of a nuclear war, ensuring the continuity of government. Today, the bunker serves as a haunting and fascinating museum that preserves the tension of the Cold War era.

Blast Tunnel: Enter through the same 115-foot metal tunnel designed to absorb the shockwave of a nuclear explosion.

War Cabinet Room: See the nerve center where the Prime Minister and advisors would have made critical decisions during a crisis.

Royal Canadian Mint

Housed in a magnificent historic Tudor-Revival building (established 1908), the Royal Canadian Mint headquarters is where Canada’s hand-crafted collector coins, gold bullion, and medals are produced. Unlike the high-speed circulation facility in Winnipeg, the Ottawa Mint is a place of artistry, precision, and immense wealth.

Guided Tours: Every visit centers on a 45-minute guided tour (available in English and French) that provides a bird’s-eye view of the production floor. You’ll learn how master engravers translate art into metal and see the state-of-the-art gold refining technology.

Million Dollar Coin: Witness the Guinness World Record-breaking 100-kg coin made of 99.999% pure gold. With a face value of $1 million, it is one of the rarest and most valuable coins in the world.

Canada Aviation and Space Museum

Located on a former military airbase, the Canada Aviation and Space Museum is recognized as one of the finest aviation museums in the world. It houses a vast collection of more than 130 aircraft and artifacts that chronicle the history of flight, from the pioneer era to modern space exploration. The museum is famous for its Reserve Hangar, which stores many of the collection's most rare and delicate specimens in a massive, open-concept space.

Avro Arrow: You can see the largest surviving pieces of the legendary Avro CF-105 Arrow, a source of enduring fascination in Canadian aviation history.

Canadarm: The museum proudly displays the original Canadarm used on the Space Shuttle Endeavour, a symbol of Canada's significant contributions to international space programs.

Ottawa Art Gallery

The Ottawa Art Gallery (OAG) is a contemporary cultural hub located in the heart of downtown Ottawa. Reopened in 2018 in a sleek, multi-story facility, it serves as a premier venue for the art of the Ottawa-Gatineau region while fostering connections to national and international art scenes.

Diverse Collections: The gallery is home to the Firestone Collection of Canadian Art, which includes significant works by the Group of Seven, alongside rotating contemporary exhibitions featuring photography, sculpture, and new media.

Architectural Landmark: The building features stunning modern design with light-filled galleries, a rooftop terrace with views of the city, and integrated spaces that connect it to the University of Ottawa and the Rideau Centre.

Canada Science and Technology Museum

Reopened in 2017 after a complete transformation, the Canada Science and Technology Museum is a highly interactive facility that celebrates Canada's history of innovation. It combines beloved heritage artifacts with cutting-edge digital technology to create an environment that encourages curiosity and hands-on discovery.

Crazy Kitchen: One of the most famous and longest-running exhibits in Ottawa, this tilted room uses optical illusions and physical sensations to challenge your sense of balance and perception.

Locomotive Hall: This massive hall features a collection of giant steam locomotives that visitors can walk around and even climb inside, showcasing the power of early Canadian rail travel.

Laurier House National Historic Site

Laurier House is a stately Victorian mansion that served as the residence for two of Canada’s most important Prime Ministers: Sir Wilfrid Laurier and William Lyon Mackenzie King. This National Historic Site offers a rare, intimate look at the private lives of the leaders who shaped the country for over 50 years, preserved exactly as it was when Mackenzie King lived there.

Second-Floor Library: This is the most famous room in the house, where Mackenzie King spent his evenings. It remains filled with his personal book collection, his crystal ball, and a portrait of his mother, whom he famously claimed to communicate with during spiritualist seances.

Political History: The house features original artifacts and documents that highlight key moments in Canadian history, including the development of the Commonwealth and the social reforms of the early 20th century.

The Tomb of the Unknown Soldier is Canada’s most significant site of individual and collective remembrance. Canadian Tire Centre

The Canadian Tire Centre is a premier multi-purpose arena located in Kanata, about 25 kilometers west of downtown Ottawa. Since opening in 1996, it has served as the home ice for the National Hockey League’s Ottawa Senators and is the region's primary venue for large-scale entertainment.

The Glebe

The Glebe is one of Ottawa’s most desirable and historic neighborhoods, offering a village-in-the-city feel just south of the downtown core. Bordered by the scenic Rideau Canal, it is a pedestrian-friendly enclave known for its high-end boutiques, lush parks, and stunning Victorian and Edwardian architecture.

Bank Street Promenade: The neighborhood's main artery is lined with independent businesses. You can find everything from rare vinyl at Compact Music to artisanal kitchenware at J.D. Adam Kitchen Company.

Rideau Canal & Patterson Creek: The canal provides a scenic backdrop for year-round recreation, including skating in winter and cycling in summer. Patterson Creek Park is a local favorite for its tranquil willow-lined inlet and stone bridges.

National Arts Centre

The National Arts Centre is Canada's premier bilingual performing arts hub, situated along the scenic Rideau Canal in downtown Ottawa. Since its opening in 1969 as a Centennial project, it has become a beacon for the arts, showcasing world-class theater, dance, and music across its four distinct stages.

Architectural Grandeur: The building is a striking example of Brutalist architecture, recently revitalized with a magnificent glass atrium that offers panoramic views of Confederation Square and Parliament Hill.

Performance Variety: It is the home of the NAC Orchestra and hosts everything from Broadway tours and contemporary dance to indigenous theater and French-language productions.

Sparks Street

Sparks Street is a historic pedestrian mall in the heart of downtown Ottawa, just a block south of Parliament Hill. Converted into a permanent outdoor mall in 1967, it was the first of its kind in Canada and remains a vibrant public space known for its heritage architecture, diverse dining, and major seasonal festivals.

Iconic Landmarks: You can find the famous Ottawa Sign for a perfect photo opportunity, alongside historic buildings that house unique shops like Canada's Four Corners.

Dining and Nightlife: The mall is lined with numerous patios and restaurants, ranging from the European-inspired Bier Markt to cozy pubs and cafes, making it a popular spot for both weekday business lunches and evening socializing.

Winterlude

Winterlude (Bal de Neige) is Ottawa’s flagship winter festival, typically held during the first three weeks of February. Established in 1979, it celebrates Canada's northern climate and culture, transforming the capital into a giant playground of ice and snow.

Rideau Canal Skateway: The world’s largest skating rink is the heart of Winterlude. Stretching 7.8 kilometers, it features food kiosks selling BeaverTails and hot chocolate directly on the ice.

Snowflake Kingdom: Located across the river in Gatineau's Jacques-Cartier Park, this is a massive snow playground featuring giant slides, snow sculptures, and activities designed specifically for children and families.

Canadian Tulip Festival

The Canadian Tulip Festival is a world-renowned event held annually in May, transforming Ottawa into a sea of vibrant color. The festival is a symbol of international friendship, originating from a gift of 100,000 tulip bulbs from the Dutch Royal Family in 1945 to thank Canada for providing them refuge during WWII and for the liberation of the Netherlands.

Commissioners Park: This is the festival's epicenter at Dows Lake, featuring over 300,000 tulips in dozens of garden beds. It’s the largest single display of tulips in the world.

Blacklight Boardwalk: Every evening, this boardwalk transforms into a glowing experience with ultraviolet lighting that reveals the hidden patterns on the tulips that pollinators see.

10. Gatineau Park

Gatineau Park is a sprawling 361-square-kilometer conservation area located just 15 minutes north of downtown Ottawa. It serves as the region's primary year-round destination for outdoor adventure, offering a rugged landscape of Precambrian hills, sparkling lakes, and dense forests that feel worlds away from the city.

Scenic Lookouts: The park is famous for its panoramic views from the Eardley Escarpment. The most popular spots are Champlain Lookout, which offers a sweeping vista of the Ottawa Valley, and Pink Lake, a rare meromictic lake known for its stunning turquoise water.

Mackenzie King Estate: This historic country estate was the summer home of Canada's longest-serving Prime Minister. Visitors can explore the landscaped gardens, the charming cottages, and the Abbey Ruins, a collection of architectural fragments King gathered from various demolished buildings.
