Niagara Falls

Niagara Falls, one of the most iconic natural wonders in the world, is a must-see for any traveler. The majestic waterfall spans the border between Canada and the United States, but the Canadian side offers the most spectacular views. This place’s history dates back centuries, as it was formed over 12,000 years ago by glacial movements.

World-Known Landmarks

10 places

1. Niagara Falls Canada

Niagara City Cruises (Operates April, November): Formerly known as Hornblower, this is the classic boat tour that takes you directly into the mist of the Horseshoe Falls.

Journey Behind the Falls: Descend 125 feet through bedrock to explore 130-year-old tunnels that open up to observation decks directly behind the thundering wall of water.

2. Horseshoe Falls

Horseshoe Falls, located on the Canadian side of Niagara Falls, is the largest and most powerful of the three waterfalls that make up Niagara Falls. With a width of 2,600 feet and a drop of over 160 feet, it creates a stunning visual and auditory spectacle. While the American and Bridal Veil Falls are impressive, the Horseshoe Falls is the main highlight, carrying roughly 90% of the Niagara River's volume over its massive 790-meter crest.

Thunderous Brink: Standing at Table Rock, you can stand just feet away from where the water crests. The sheer vibration of the ground and the roar of the water make it a visceral, multi-sensory experience.

Mist Cloud: The falls are so powerful they create their own localized weather system. The massive plume of mist can often be seen from miles away and frequently creates brilliant rainbows on sunny afternoons.

3. American Falls

Although the American Falls are physically located in the United States, the best panoramic views of them are found on the Canadian side. Because the American Falls face outward toward the Canadian shoreline, visitors in Niagara Falls, Ontario, get a front-row seat to see the water crashing over the rocks, whereas visitors on the U.S. side are often looking at them from the side or from directly above.

Wall of Water View: From the paved promenade of Queen Victoria Park, you can walk directly opposite the American Falls. This is the only place where you can see the full width of the American Falls and the Bridal Veil Falls (the smallest of the three) in one glance.

Rainbow Bridge: If you walk onto the Rainbow Bridge (which connects the two countries), you get a unique midway perspective of the American Falls. It’s one of the few spots where you can photograph both the American and Horseshoe Falls in the same frame without a wide-angle lens.

4. Bridal Veil Falls

The Bridal Veil Falls is the smallest and most delicate of the three waterfalls. While it is located entirely on the U.S. side (separated from the American Falls by Luna Island), the Canadian side provides a unique perspective that emphasizes its lacy and ethereal quality.

From the Canadian shore, you can see how it perfectly lives up to its name, appearing as a thin, elegant curtain of white water that billows in the wind next to its massive neighbors.

Lacy Contrast: While the Horseshoe Falls is all about raw power, the Bridal Veil is about finesse. From the Canadian promenade, you can see the wind catch the spray, causing the water to dance and fan out like a bride’s veil.

5. Niagara Whirlpool

The Niagara Whirlpool is a massive natural vortex located about 4.5 kilometers downstream from the falls. It was formed roughly 4,200 years ago as the Niagara River’s erosion encountered the ancient St. David’s Buried Gorge, forcing the water to make an abrupt 90-degree turn. This geological elbow creates a mesmerizing, counter-clockwise swirl of turquoise water that reaches depths of up to 38 meters (125 feet).

Whirlpool Aero Car: This antique cable car has been in operation since 1916. It carries passengers 76 meters above the river, traveling between two points on the Canadian shore. Curiously, because the river elbows here, you actually cross the international border into the U.S. and back four times during the 10-minute round trip, no passport required.

Reversal Phenomenon: Every night at 10:00 PM, the direction of the whirlpool actually reverses. This happens because water is diverted from the river to the nearby hydroelectric power plants; as the volume of the river drops, the pressure changes and the vortex begins to spin clockwise until the morning.

Good to know. Note that the Whirlpool Aero Car is not wheelchair accessible due to the historic nature of the boarding platforms. However, the White Water Walk is mostly accessible via an elevator that takes you 70 meters down to the gorge floor.

6. Niagara Glen Nature Areas

The Niagara Glen Nature Reserve is the ultimate hidden gem for travelers who want to escape the neon lights of Clifton Hill and experience the raw, ancient beauty of the Niagara Gorge. Designated as a nature reserve since 1894, this pocket of Carolinian forest sits on a massive terrace carved out by the falls thousands of years ago as they receded upstream. It is a world of towering limestone cliffs, massive prehistoric boulders, and some of the most vibrant flora in Ontario.

Hiking Trails: There are roughly 4 kilometers of well-marked trails ranging from moderate to difficult. As you descend the metal staircase (the only way in and out), you enter a microclimate that feels significantly cooler and more humid than the top of the gorge.

Bouldering: The Glen is world-renowned among climbers. The massive limestone rocks left behind by the retreating falls offer hundreds of established problems for boulderers. A permit is required from the Nature Centre if you plan to climb.

7. White Water Walk

The White Water Walk offers one of the most intense and intimate ways to experience the Niagara River's power without getting wet. Located about 4 km north of the falls, this attraction takes you down to the very edge of the Whirlpool Rapids, one of the wildest stretches of whitewater in the world.

The Descent: Your journey begins with a 70-meter (230-foot) elevator ride down into the Niagara Gorge. You then walk through a 73-meter tunnel that opens up to the shoreline.

Class 6 Rapids: You’ll walk along a 305-meter (1,000-foot) boardwalk directly beside Class 6 whitewater rapids. The water here travels at a staggering 48 km/h, creating massive standing waves that are among the largest in North America.

8. Niagara Parkway

The Niagara Parkway is a scenic 55-kilometer (34-mile) boulevard that follows the winding path of the Niagara River from Lake Erie to Lake Ontario. Sir Winston Churchill famously described this route as the prettiest Sunday drive in the world, and it remains the backbone of the region's natural beauty, connecting the thundering falls to quiet historic towns and lush gardens. The parkway is managed by Niagara Parks and serves as a museum without walls, featuring perfectly manicured gardens, bronze monuments, and hidden lookout points over the gorge.

Scenic Highlights and Stops

Floral Clock: Located north of the botanical gardens, this massive timepiece is 12.2 meters in diameter and is kept in bloom with approximately 16,000 carpet bedding plants that are changed twice a year.

9. Thompson Point

Thompsons Point is a significant geological and historical landmark located along the Niagara Parkway, specifically at the northern edge of the Niagara Whirlpool. It serves as one of the two anchoring points for the Whirlpool Aero Car, offering a dramatic vantage point over the churning Class 6 rapids below.

Aerial Gateway: It functions as the boarding and observation station for the Aero Car. From this height, you can clearly see the elbow of the Niagara River where the water is forced into its famous counter-clockwise vortex.

Geological Significance: The point marks a sharp turn in the Niagara Gorge. It is a prime location to observe the different layers of sedimentary rock that have been exposed by thousands of years of river erosion.

10. Rainbow Bridge

Rainbow Bridge is an iconic landmark connecting Canada and the United States, offering breathtaking views of Niagara Falls. Opened in 1941, this pedestrian and vehicular bridge spans the Niagara River, offering a perfectly centered view of all three waterfalls.

Million Dollar View: Because the bridge sits directly over the Niagara River gorge, it provides an unobstructed, high-angle perspective of both the American Falls and the Horseshoe Falls. It is widely considered the best spot for a single panoramic photo of the entire Niagara skyline.

Walkable Border: Unlike many international borders, the Rainbow Bridge is very pedestrian-friendly. You can walk from the center of the Canadian tourist district to the American state park in about 10, 15 minutes.

Art and History Lovers

9 places

1. Journey Behind the Falls

Journey Behind the Falls offers a one-of-a-kind experience for visitors to Niagara Falls. While boat tours take you to the falls, this attraction takes you inside them. Located at the Table Rock Welcome Centre, it allows you to stand directly behind the massive curtain of water that makes up the Horseshoe Falls.

The Descent: Your journey begins with an elevator ride that drops you 38 meters (125 feet) through solid bedrock.

Tunnels: You’ll explore 130-year-old tunnels that branch out to two distinct portals: the Cataract Portal and the Great Falls Portal. These openings look out directly into the back of the falling water. The sound here isn't just a noise, it's a deep, physical vibration that you can feel in your chest.

2. Bird Kingdom

Bird Kingdom is a world-class attraction located just a short walk from the Rainbow Bridge, holding the title of the world's largest free-flying indoor aviary. This multi-level tropical paradise is housed in the city's historic 1906 Spirella Corset Factory building, combining architectural history with a lush, exotic environment.

The experience is designed as a self-guided journey through several distinct zones, starting with a look at historic artifacts and leading into a massive, sun-drenched rainforest habitat.

Main Bird Aviary: The centerpiece of the attraction is a multi-story rainforest featuring a 40-foot waterfall. Hundreds of tropical birds from around the world fly freely here, allowing you to observe them without cages in a naturalistic setting.

3. Niagara Parks Power Station

The Niagara Parks Power Station is one of the most significant industrial heritage sites in North America. Once the beating heart of the region's hydroelectric industry, this 1905 powerhouse has been meticulously preserved and transformed into a world-class immersive museum. It offers a rare look at how the massive energy of the falls was first harnessed to power Ontario and New York.

Generator Hall: Walk through a cathedral-like space filled with massive, original blue Westinghouse generators. Interactive exhibits and augmented reality stations explain how these turbines converted rushing water into electricity.

Tunnel: This is the station's newest and most popular feature. You descend 180 feet in a glass elevator to reach a 2,200-foot-long brick-lined tunnel. Originally used to discharge water back into the river, it now leads to a spectacular new viewing platform at the very edge of the Niagara River.

4. Louis Tussaud's Waxworks

Located just a short walk from the iconic Niagara Falls, Louis Tussaud's Waxworks is an interactive museum featuring lifelike wax figures of famous personalities. This attraction is part of the Ripley’s family and is housed in a building that resembles a grand English Tudor-style mansion, inviting visitors to get up close with over 200 life-like wax figures.

Themed Galleries: The waxworks are organized into various themed rooms, ranging from Hollywood’s Best and The Royal Family to Music Idols and a Horror Hall for those seeking a bit of a thrill.

Interactive Photo Ops: Unlike traditional museums, this attraction encourages visitors to touch the figures and pose for selfies. You can stand beside world leaders, superheroes, or modern pop icons to capture fun memories.

5. Movieland Wax Museum

Movieland Wax Museum is a captivating attraction in Niagara Falls, offering an impressive collection of lifelike wax figures from the world of film and television. Visitors can walk through a variety of themed rooms, each showcasing famous characters, movie stars, and iconic scenes from both classic and modern cinema.

Diverse Film Eras: The museum features a wide array of characters ranging from the Golden Age of Hollywood to modern-day blockbusters, including figures from classic westerns, horror films, and current superhero franchises.

Interactive Set Pieces: Many exhibits are designed for photography, allowing you to pose alongside wax versions of stars in settings that mimic the original movie lighting and decor.

6. Nikola Tesla Statue

The Nikola Tesla Statue in Niagara Falls honors one of the greatest inventors in history. Located near the falls, this impressive bronze statue commemorates Tesla’s groundbreaking work in electricity and his ties to the region. What makes this place special is that Tesla's experiments with alternating current (AC) power helped shape the modern electrical grid, and Niagara Falls played a key role in his achievements. The statue serves as a tribute to his scientific legacy and the powerful connection between innovation and nature.

Commemorative Design: The statue depicts Tesla standing atop an AC motor, one of his most important inventions. He is shown looking toward the Horseshoe Falls, symbolizing the harness of nature's power.

Historical Significance: It was unveiled in 2006 to mark the 150th anniversary of Tesla's birth. The monument highlights how his vision fundamentally changed how the world consumes energy.

7. Winter Festival of Lights

The Winter Festival of Lights in Niagara Falls is one of the most popular events in the region, transforming the area into a dazzling display of color and light during the winter months. This annual festival spans several weeks, offering visitors a magical experience with its impressive light displays, festive atmosphere, and seasonal activities.

8. Lundy's Lane Battlefield

The Lundy's Lane Battlefield is one of the most significant historical sites in North America, marking the location of the bloodiest battle of the War of 1812. Fought on July 25, 1814, this fierce conflict between British and American forces took place on a hill overlooking the falls, and today, much of the site is preserved as a solemn commemorative space within the city.

Battle Ground Memorial: The site features a large stone monument dedicated to the soldiers who fought and fell during the six-hour nocturnal battle. It has a quiet, reflective atmosphere that stands in stark contrast to the nearby bustling tourist districts.

Drummond Hill Cemetery: Located on the battlefield, this historic cemetery is the final resting place for many soldiers from both sides, as well as prominent local figures like Laura Secord. It is considered one of the most historically significant graveyards in Canada.

9. Mount Carmel Spiritual Centre

The Monastery of Mount Carmel Spiritual Retreat and Conference Centre is a serene and historic landmark situated on the heights overlooking the falls. Established in 1894 as a Carmelite monastery, it serves as a peaceful sanctuary for those seeking spiritual reflection, offering a stark but welcome contrast to the neon lights and crowds of the nearby tourist districts.

Historic Chapel: The heart of the monastery is its beautiful chapel, renowned for its intricate wood carvings and stunning stained glass windows that date back over a century.

Spiritual Retreats: The centre is a functioning retreat facility, hosting organized group retreats and providing space for individuals looking for quiet contemplation and prayer.

Outdoor Attractions

9 places

1. Table Rock Welcome Centre

The Table Rock Welcome Centre is the heart of the Niagara Parks experience, serving as the primary entry point for millions of visitors each year. Perched on the very brink of the Horseshoe Falls, this historic complex is much more than a visitor center; it is a premier viewing gallery and a hub for the falls' most iconic attractions.

Originally built in 1926, the center has undergone extensive renovations to offer a modern, glass-fronted gateway that provides some of the most dramatic and intimidating views of the thundering water from just a few feet away.

Ultimate Vantage Point: The outdoor observation plaza and the indoor Grand Hall offer arguably the best close-up views of the Horseshoe Falls. You can stand right at the edge of the retaining wall and watch the river accelerate over the crest.

2. Queen Victoria Park

Queen Victoria Park is the front lawn of Niagara Falls, serving as the central green space that stretches along the Niagara Parkway directly opposite the American and Canadian Horseshoe Falls. It is widely considered the best spot for land-based viewing, offering a perfectly manicured landscape that changes dramatically with the seasons.

Floral Displays: The park is famous for its horticultural excellence, featuring a massive collection of native and international plants. In the spring, you'll see over 500,000 daffodils in bloom, followed by magnificent magnolia trees and hybrid tea rose gardens in the summer.

Prime Event Hub: This is the primary gathering spot for major public events, including the Winter Festival of Lights displays and the nightly falls illumination. It is also the best place to sit on the grass and watch the fireworks over the gorge.

3. Botanical Gardens

The Niagara Parks Botanical Gardens provide a lush, tranquil escape across 40 hectares (99 acres) of beautifully maintained landscapes. Established in 1936, these European-style gardens serve as a quiet sanctuary north of the main falls and are home to the Niagara Parks School of Horticulture.

World-Famous Rose Garden: The gardens feature one of Canada's largest Victorian-style rose gardens, boasting over 2,400 roses and 40 different varieties that create a stunning sensory experience when in full bloom.

Diverse Plant Collections: Beyond roses, you can explore specialized sections including formal parterres, herb and vegetable gardens, and an extensive arboretum featuring rare trees and towering perennials.

4. Whirlpool Aero Car

The Whirlpool Aero Car is a historic engineering marvel that has been transporting passengers directly over the center of the Niagara Whirlpool since 1916. Designed by Spanish engineer Leonardo Torres Quevedo, it remains the only attraction of its kind in the world, suspended by six sturdy steel cables.

The experience offers a unique perspective on the river's power as you soar 200 feet above the Class 6 rapids where the Niagara River makes a sharp, 90-degree turn.

Aerial Perspective: The car travels approximately 1,100 feet across the gorge. At the midway point, you are directly above the swirling vortex of the whirlpool, providing a view of the turquoise water that is impossible to get from the shore.

5. Niagara Speedway

The Niagara Speedway is North America’s largest elevated go-kart track, often described as a mario-kart style racing experience. Located in the heart of Clifton Hill, this massive attraction combines the traditional speed of go-karts with the thrills of a roller coaster, featuring a four-story helical spiral and two tiered ramps.

Elevated Racing: The track spans over four acres and takes racers through a series of corkscrew spirals and hair-raising drops. The elevated sections provide a unique vantage point of the surrounding neon district while you navigate the course.

High-Performance Karts: The karts are powered by large 9-horsepower Honda engines, capable of reaching speeds that feel quite intense on the tight turns of the elevated ramps.

6. Niagara SkyWheel

The Niagara SkyWheel is Canada’s largest observation wheel, standing 175 feet (53 meters) above the heart of Clifton Hill. It provides a world-class vantage point for viewing the Horseshoe and American Falls, the Niagara River, and the surrounding city skyline in total comfort.

Climate-Controlled Cabins: The wheel features fully enclosed gondolas equipped with heating and air conditioning, making it a year-round attraction regardless of the weather.

Aerial Panoramas: Each ride lasts approximately 8 to 12 minutes, consisting of three to four complete rotations. From the top, you can see for miles, offering a perspective that rivals the Skylon Tower.

7. Kingsbridge Park

Kingsbridge Park is a scenic riverside destination located at the mouth of the Welland River where it meets the Niagara River. It serves as a popular gateway for those exploring the southern end of the Niagara Parkway, offering a more relaxed, local atmosphere compared to the high-traffic parks directly adjacent to the falls. This park is known for its spacious layouts and its role as a key trailhead for the Niagara River Recreation Trail.

Family Amenities: The park is well-equipped for a full day out, featuring a large children's playground, a splash pad (seasonal), and several covered picnic pavilions that are ideal for group gatherings.

Recreational Hub: It acts as a major staging area for cyclists and hikers. From here, you can follow the paved Niagara River Recreation Trail all the way to Fort Erie or north toward the falls.

8. Skylon Tower

The Skylon Tower is an iconic feature of the Niagara Falls skyline, rising 775 feet (236 meters) above the thundering water. It is widely considered the best spot for a true birds-eye view, as it provides a 360-degree panorama of both the American and Horseshoe Falls, the Niagara River, and even the skylines of Buffalo and Toronto on a clear day.

The experience begins with a Yellow Bug elevator ride, a signature glass-enclosed lift that travels up the outside of the tower, taking you from the street level to the observation deck in just 52 seconds.

Observation Decks: The tower features both indoor and outdoor observation decks. The outdoor deck is protected by a safety fence but allows you to feel the wind and hear the distant roar of the falls, while the indoor deck is perfect for comfortable viewing in any weather.

9. Dufferin Islands Park

Dufferin Islands is a quiet, 10-acre nature area located just south of the Horseshoe Falls. This hidden gem consists of a series of small, secluded islands connected by small bridges and footpaths, offering a peaceful retreat from the more commercialized parts of the city. It is described as a sanctuary for local wildlife and a perfect spot for those who want to experience the natural beauty of the Niagara River ecosystem at a slower pace.

Scenic Exploration: The area features several winding walking trails and wooden footbridges that crisscross through lush greenery and over calm, shallow pools of water.

Wildlife Viewing: It is a prime location for birdwatching and observing small local animals. You can often see ducks, geese, turtles, and fish in the interconnected waterways.

Other Interesting Places

8 places

1. Butterfly Conservatory

The Butterfly Conservatory is one of the most popular year-round attractions in Niagara Falls, located on the grounds of the Niagara Parks Botanical Gardens. This lush, climate-controlled paradise features over 2,000 colorful tropical butterflies floating freely among exotic blossoms and greenery, providing a serene escape from the outdoor elements.

Self-Guided Tropical Journey: The experience begins with a short informative video before you enter the glass-domed conservatory. You then follow a 180-meter (600-foot) network of paths that wind through a rainforest environment complete with a waterfall and pond.

Emergence Window: One of the most fascinating features is the Emergence Window, where you can watch butterflies leave their pupae and prepare for their very first flight.

2. Niagara Parks Floral Showhouse

The Niagara Parks Floral Showhouse is a lush tropical oasis located just south of the Table Rock Welcome Centre. For over 70 years, this destination has provided a serene indoor escape where visitors can immerse themselves in exotic plant collections and seasonally themed floral displays. The experience is centered around several interconnected glass houses, each meticulously curated to showcase a variety of textures, colors, and scents.

Poinsettia and Orchids: The showhouse is famous for its eight annual floral shows. The Annual Poinsettia Show during the winter is a major highlight, while the orchid collection is one of the most extensive in the region.

Tropical Paradise: Inside the permanent collections, you'll find soaring palms, tropical plants, and succulent displays. The humidity and warmth make it a perfect year-round destination, especially during the colder months.

3. Clifton Hill

Clifton Hill, known as the Street of Fun, is the vibrant heart of the Niagara Falls tourist district. This world-famous promenade is a neon-lit stretch filled with high-energy attractions, themed restaurants, and eclectic shops that provide a carnival-like atmosphere just steps away from the natural wonder of the falls. The area is particularly magical after sunset when the massive LED signs and colorful storefronts illuminate the street.

Diverse Attractions: The hill is home to several of the city's most popular landmarks, including the Niagara SkyWheel, the Niagara Speedway, and the Movieland Wax Museum.

Themed Dining: You'll find unique eateries such as the Rainforest Cafe and the Hard Rock Cafe, alongside a variety of quick-service options and sweet shops like the Fudge Factory.

4. Ripley's Believe It or Not!

Ripley's Believe It or Not! is a world-renowned Odditorium that has been a staple of Clifton Hill since 1963. Housed in an eye-catching crooked building, this museum features over 500 bizarre exhibits, rare artifacts, and interactive displays that celebrate the strange and unusual from around the globe.

Mind-Bending Galleries: Explore themed rooms filled with shrunken heads from Ecuador, a life-sized animatronic of the world's tallest man (who stands up when you approach), and micro-sculptures so tiny they fit into the eye of a needle.

Immersive Illusions: Test your balance and perception in the Vortex Tunnel, a spinning visual illusion that makes you feel like the world is rotating around you, or get lost in the various optical and photographic illusion zones.

5. Fallsview Indoor Waterpark

The Fallsview Indoor Waterpark is a massive, 3-acre aquatic playground located right on the Clifton Hill Street of Fun entertainment complex. Consistently ranked as one of the top water parks in North America, it offers a tropical 84-degree environment year-round, featuring a sprawling collection of 16 slides, a giant wave pool, and unique play areas that cater to every age group. It has a direct indoor connections to several major hotels, making it a particularly popular choice for families visiting during the winter months.

Slide Highlights: The park features 16 thrilling waterslides, including the Toob Tower with four different winding paths, high-speed Extreme Racing mat slides, and the Canadian Plunge, a swirling bowl that spins riders around before dropping them into a splash pool.

Beach House Play Area: This multi-level interactive rain fortress is a centerpiece for younger guests, topped with a massive 1,000-gallon tipping bucket that drenches everyone below every few minutes.

6. Niagara Fallsview Casino

The Fallsview Casino Resort is a world-class entertainment destination and the largest gaming resort in Canada. Perched on a cliff overlooking the Horseshoe Falls, it offers a high-end experience that blends non-stop gaming excitement with luxury accommodations, fine dining, and premier live entertainment. Since its opening in 2004, it has served as the anchor of the Fallsview district, attracting visitors with its sophisticated atmosphere and breathtaking panoramic views of the water.

World-Class Gaming: The 200,000-square-foot casino floor features over 3,500 slot machines and 130 table games, including blackjack, roulette, and baccarat. There is also a dedicated poker room and a high-limit salon for those seeking a more exclusive experience.

OLG Stage and Avalon Theatre: The resort is a major hub for live performances, hosting world-famous musicians, comedians, and theatrical shows at the 5,000-seat OLG Stage and the more intimate 1,500-seat Avalon Theatre.

7. The Great Canadian Midway

The Great Canadian Midway is a massive 70,000-square-foot indoor entertainment complex located at the heart of Clifton Hill. It serves as one of the largest arcade destinations in North America, offering a high-energy environment filled with hundreds of games ranging from nostalgic classics to the latest high-tech simulators. The vibrant, bustling atmosphere,makes it a popular refuge for families, especially during the evening or on rainy days.

Game Variety: The facility houses over 300 games, including immersive 4D motion simulators, classic Skee-Ball, heavy-duty racing games, and elaborate redemption machines.

Redemption Center: A central highlight is the large prize center where players can exchange tickets earned from skill games for a wide array of rewards, from small novelties to high-end electronics.

8. Nightmares Fear Factory

Nightmares Fear Factory is a legendary psychological horror attraction located at the top of Clifton Hill. It is famous for being the world's most frightening experience, having allegedly caused over 180,000 visitors to chicken out before finishing the tour.

The attraction leans heavily on total darkness and psychological tension rather than just standard jump scares. It is widely known for its shriek-peek photos, which capture the genuine terror of guests at the most intense moment of the walkthrough.

Total Darkness Experience: Much of the walkthrough takes place in near-complete darkness, forcing guests to navigate by following small red lights while being subjected to unsettling sounds and tactile surprises.
