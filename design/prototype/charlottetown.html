World-Known Landmarks

7 places

1. Province House National Historic Site

The Neoclassical Exterior: Even while the interior is being finalized, you can admire the stunning architecture from Queen Square. The building features a majestic symmetrical design, Greek and Roman influences, and massive sandstone blocks that have been meticulously restored stone-by-stone.

The Story of Confederation Exhibit: Since the main building is currently closed, head next door to the Confederation Centre of the Arts. This immersive exhibit features a replica of the Confederation Chamber, where you can sit at the same table as the Fathers of Confederation and watch the film A Building of Destiny.

2. St. Dunstan's Basilica

St. Dunstan’s Basilica is a stunning masterpiece of French Gothic architecture and a defining silhouette of the Charlottetown skyline. As the only Roman Catholic basilica in Prince Edward Island and a National Historic Site of Canada, this Stone Cathedral is a place of profound beauty, resilience, and quiet reflection. Rebuilt in 1919 after a devastating fire, its soaring twin spires and 200-foot peaks serve as a beacon for visitors entering the city.

Admire the Twin Spires: Before entering, look up at the two massive spires, each topped with a 10-foot-high cross. They are among the highest points in the city and are beautifully illuminated at night, casting a serene glow over Great George Street.

The High Altar: Step inside to see the centerpiece of the basilica, a stunning 37-foot-high altar adorned with 23 statues of saints and angels. It is framed by a magnificent 14-foot-wide rose window that bathes the sanctuary in colorful, ethereal light.

3. Government House

Government House, often called Fanningbank, is a stunning National Historic Site that serves as the official residence of the Lieutenant Governor of Prince Edward Island. Built in 1834, this white-shingled Georgian mansion overlooks the entrance to Charlottetown Harbour. It is more than just a home for the King's representative; it is a living piece of history that has hosted royalty, delegates of the 1864 Charlottetown Conference, and generations of Islanders.

Guided Summer Tours: During July and August, the doors of Fanningbank swing open for free guided tours. You can explore the grand Main Hall with its impressive 15-foot Doric columns and the State Dining Room, home to an original 1834 mahogany table that still seats 24 guests for official functions.

The Royal Suite: Visit the second-floor apartments where Queen Elizabeth II and Prince Philip stayed during their 1959 visit. The rooms are preserved with period elegance, offering a rare glimpse into the vice-regal lifestyle.

4. Charlottetown City Hall

Charlottetown City Hall is a grand Victorian landmark that has stood at the corner of Queen and Kent Streets since 1888. A striking example of Romanesque Revival architecture, it is the oldest municipal hall in Prince Edward Island and was designated a National Historic Site in 1984. With its red brick facade, heavy stone arches, and soaring corner tower, it remains the active seat of local government and a proud symbol of the city's late-19th-century prosperity.

The Romanesque Architecture: Admire the building’s exterior character-defining elements, including the massive rounded arches over the windows and doors, the high square corner tower with its pyramidal roof, and the decorative Nova Scotia freestone bands that contrast with the red brick.

The Historic Bell: Look for the large, historic bell located on the Kent Street side of the building. It serves as a reminder of the era when the ground floor housed the city's fire hall and horse stables.

5. Victoria Park

Victoria Park is Charlottetown’s crown jewel, a sprawling 40-acre waterfront oasis that serves as the city's primary playground and scenic escape. Established in 1873 on former government farmland, the park gracefully balances its role as a historic military site with its status as a vibrant hub for families, athletes, and anyone seeking a breathtaking sunset over the harbor.

The Waterfront Boardwalk: Stretching for 1.2 km along the harbor’s edge, this wide wooden promenade is the heart of the park. It’s the city’s favorite spot for a sunset stroll, offering unobstructed views of the water, the Brighton Beach Range Light, and the harbor entrance.

Prince Edward Battery: Located on the park’s southern bank, this historic fortification features a row of massive 19th-century cannons and a restored stone magazine. It’s a tangible link to the Napoleonic Wars and the War of 1812, providing an open-air museum feel with spectacular vantage points for watching cruise ships.

6. Victoria Row

Victoria Row is the vibrant, beating heart of downtown Charlottetown’s social scene. Located on Richmond Street between Queen and Great George Streets, this historic block features a stunning row of Victorian-era red brick buildings. During the warmer months, the street is closed to vehicles, transforming into a bustling pedestrian-only thoroughfare where live music, artisan shopping, and alfresco dining create a festive European-style atmosphere.

Alfresco Dining: The Row is famous for its expansive outdoor patios. Grab a seat under a signature blue umbrella at The Cork & Cast or John Brown Richmond Street Grille to enjoy fresh PEI mussels and local craft beer while soaking in the street's energy.

The Victoria Row Stage: During the summer, a public stage hosts local musicians and performers daily. From jazz and folk to traditional fiddle music, the soundtrack of the city is best enjoyed from a nearby bench or cafe window.

7. Peake's Wharf

Peake’s Wharf is the bustling heart of Charlottetown’s historic waterfront. Once a center for the city’s industrial shipping, it has been transformed into a picturesque pedestrian village where colorful boutiques, floating marinas, and salty sea air create the ultimate Island summer vibe. It is the go-to spot for those looking to experience the maritime pulse of the city, complete with live music and world-famous treats.

Peake’s Wharf Boat Tours: Hop on a Transport Canada-approved vessel for a harbor cruise. These tours are famous for their Lobster Trap Hauls, where you can see a live trap pulled from the water and even hold a lobster. Sunset and seal-watching cruises are also top-rated for 2026.

Live Music at the Quay: Every afternoon and evening during the summer, the Peake’s Quay outdoor stage features local musicians. From traditional East Coast fiddling to acoustic indie sets, it’s the perfect place to sit back and enjoy the atmosphere.

Art and History Lovers

8 places

1. Confederation Centre of the Arts

The Confederation Centre of the Arts is Canada’s National Memorial to the Fathers of Confederation and the premier cultural hub of Prince Edward Island. Occupying an entire city block in the heart of Charlottetown, this brutalist-inspired landmark is a massive complex that brings together musical theatre, visual arts, and heritage learning.

As of March 2026, the Centre is in the midst of a historic $65 million renovation project, but it remains a buzzing hive of activity with its art gallery and theaters fully operational.

2. Beaconsfield Historic House

Beaconsfield Historic House is a stunning yellow-and-white masterpiece of Victorian architecture that serves as a testament to the Golden Age of Charlottetown. Built in 1877 for wealthy shipbuilder James Peake Jr., it was once the most modern home on the Island, featuring cutting-edge luxuries like running water, central heating, and gas lighting. Today, it is a National Historic Site and the headquarters of the PEI Museum and Heritage Foundation.

The Grand Tour: Walk through 25 meticulously restored rooms filled with original period furniture. Don't miss the Double Drawing Room, featuring ornate plasterwork, porcelain chandeliers, and eight unique marble fireplaces that showcase the opulence of the merchant class.

The Peake Window: Look for the stunning stained-glass window above the grand staircase. It features the initials of James Peake Jr., designed to be perfectly illuminated as you ascend the stairs, though locals joke the banisters were built low more for style than for safety.

3. Great George Street Historic District

The Great George Street Historic District is one of the most significant and visually stunning streetscapes in Canada. Spanning six blocks from the waterfront at Peake’s Quay up to the front steps of Province House, this National Historic District is a living museum of architectural history. It is the very path the Fathers of Confederation walked in 1864 when they arrived to debate the birth of a nation.

The Great George Heritage Walk: Follow the bronze footsteps embedded in the sidewalk that mark the historic route of the 1864 delegates. Along the way, you'll find four interpretive panels that use archival photos to tell the story of the street’s evolution.

Architectural Time Travel: The street is a masterclass in 19th-century styles. On one side, you’ll see perfectly preserved Georgian wooden row houses (1820s, 1860s), while across the street, the massive stone towers of St. Dunstan’s Basilica represent the peak of High Victorian Gothic design.

4. All Souls’ Chapel

All Souls’ Chapel is a small but beautifully detailed stone chapel located beside St. Peter’s Cathedral in Charlottetown. Designated a National Historic Site in 1990, it is widely considered one of the finest examples of High Victorian Gothic Revival architecture in Canada. It was built in 1888 as a labor of love by two of the Island's most famous brothers: architect William Critchlow Harris and artist Robert Harris.

The Harris Collaboration: Experience the unique harmony between the chapel’s structure and its art. While William designed the rust-red PEI sandstone exterior and the intricate wood-and-stone interior, his brother Robert, famed for painting the official portrait of the Fathers of Confederation, created the eighteen mural paintings that adorn the walls.

The Murals of the Faith: Look closely at the eighteen canvases that depict scenes from the life of Christ and the early Church Fathers. A local secret: Robert Harris used his friends, family members, and parishioners of St. Peter's as models for the biblical figures, making the art a literal snapshot of 19th-century Charlottetown.

5. The Mack (Theater)

The Mack (officially the MacKenzie Theatre) is the Confederation Centre’s intimate, cabaret-style venue. Located just across the street from the main complex at 128 Great George Street, it offers a completely different vibe from the grand Sobey Family Theatre. With its small tables, licensed bar service, and 200-seat capacity, it’s the place to go for high-energy kitchen parties, stand-up comedy, and experimental new Canadian works.

6. St. Peter's Cathedral

St. Peter’s Cathedral is a grand Victorian-era church that serves as the second cathedral of the Anglican Diocese of Nova Scotia and Prince Edward Island. While many visitors are drawn specifically to the attached All Souls’ Chapel, the main cathedral is a magnificent destination in its own right, known for its rich Anglo-Catholic high church traditions, superb choral music, and historic role in the West Bog neighborhood.

The Gothic Revival Nave: Explore the spacious interior of the main cathedral, built between 1867 and 1869. Unlike the sandstone chapel, the cathedral is constructed of red brick and features dark walnut-colored Douglas fir pews that were added in 1928.

Masterful Stained Glass: Admire the high-quality memorial windows, many of which were crafted in London, England, by Kemp & Son. The intricate designs fill the nave with a spectrum of colors, especially during the morning light.

7. Ardgowan National Historic Site of Canada

Ardgowan National Historic Site is a quiet, picturesque retreat located just a few minutes from downtown Charlottetown. While many of the city’s historic sites focus on the formal debates of 1864, Ardgowan tells the story of the social side of Confederation. This cottage ornée was the home of William Henry Pope, a Father of Confederation who famously wooed reluctant delegates with Island hospitality, oysters, and champagne on these very grounds.

Victorian Picturesque Grounds: The 5-acre estate is a beautifully restored example of 19th-century landscape design. You can stroll through the orchard, wander the perennial gardens, and enjoy the tranquil rural atmosphere that made this a favorite getaway for the Victorian elite.

The Cottage Ornée Architecture: Admire the exterior of the house, which was built in the early 1850s. Its Gothic Revival details, like the ornate fretwork on the veranda, the symmetrical H-shaped layout, and the pointed gables, make it one of the most charming buildings in the province.

What to See and Do

C'Mon In! An Island Kitchen Party: This is the flagship summer show for The Mack in 2026. Running from June 6, August 29, it’s an immersive, foot-stomping tribute to East Coast music. Expect fiddles, storytelling, and a lot of audience interaction that makes you feel like you’ve been invited into a local’s living room.

The Studio Experience: Keep an eye out for workshops and community series.

Cabaret Atmosphere: Unlike traditional theaters, The Mack often features table seating. You can enjoy a local craft beer or a glass of wine during the performance, making it a popular choice for a more relaxed date night out.

Outdoor Attractions

7 places

1. Charlottetown Marina

Charlottetown Marina is a popular waterfront destination offering visitors the chance to enjoy the scenic beauty of Charlottetown Harbour. Located in the heart of the city, this marina is a hub for both boating enthusiasts and tourists who want to relax by the water. This place is a collection of three distinct locations, the Peake’s Quay Marina, SeaPort Marina, and the Charlottetown Yacht Club.

The Floating Docks: Wander the boardwalks that connect the various marinas. In the summer, the docks are alive with activity, and it’s a popular spot for locals to congregate and watch the massive cruise ships dock just meters away at the SeaPort Marina.

On-Water Dining: Experience a uniquely Charlottetown meal at a floating restaurant tucked into the docks. There is nothing quite like enjoying fresh PEI oysters and local beef while literally floating on the historic waterfront.

2. Confederation Landing Park

Confederation Landing Park is a beautifully landscaped 6-acre waterfront park that marks the literal landing site of the Fathers of Confederation. In September 1864, the delegates from the Province of Canada arrived here by rowboat (because the town's only circus was in town and all the wharf space was taken) to begin the talks that created Canada. Today, it serves as the city's premier outdoor event space and a tranquil spot for harbor watching.

The Grand Promenade: Walk the wide, granite-paved path that leads from the water up toward Great George Street. The lighting fixtures and seating here were designed to mirror those in Ottawa’s Parliamentary precinct, reinforcing the connection between the birthplace and the capital.

Inline Skating & Recreation: The park features a unique dedicated area for inline skating and paved pathways that are a favorite for local joggers and cyclists. It’s is a key link in the city's Commuter Bike Lane system.

3. Rochford Square

Rochford Square is often called the most beautiful of Charlottetown’s five historic squares. Laid out in the original 1771 town plan, this 4-hectare park was transformed in 2000 into a magnificent Victorian-style garden. It serves as a lush, quiet sanctuary on the western edge of downtown, famous for its intricate floral displays and dramatic mature trees.

The Victorian Gardens: Unlike the more open play squares, Rochford is a formal ornamental park. Wander through perfectly manicured flower beds and shrubbery that change with the seasons. In the summer, the vibrant blooms make it the city's top choice for wedding and graduation photography.

The Drama Trees: Admire the massive, century-old trees that dominate the square. These include species planted as far back as the first Arbour Day in 1884. Their sprawling canopies provide deep shade and a secret forest feel in the middle of the urban grid.

4. Hillsborough Bridge

The Hillsborough Bridge (officially the Hillsborough River Bridge) is the vital gateway connecting Charlottetown to the fast-growing town of Stratford. Spanning the wide estuary of the Hillsborough River, a Canadian Heritage River, it is the busiest section of highway in Prince Edward Island. While most see it as a daily commuter route, the bridge is a fascinating piece of engineering with a history dating back to the early 1900s.

The first bridge (1905) was a narrow-gauge railway bridge that also allowed horse-drawn carriages, but only when a train wasn't coming. The current bridge was built in 1962 and significantly widened in 1995 to accommodate the massive increase in vehicle traffic from the growing suburbs across the water.

The Active Transportation (AT) Lane: Completed in 2021, a wide, physically separated path runs along the side of the bridge. It’s a favorite for joggers and cyclists, offering one of the most panoramic views of the Charlottetown skyline, the historic waterfront, and the rolling hills of Stratford.

5. Brighton Beach Range Lighthouses

Brighton Beach Range Lighthouses are two iconic structures that guide ships safely into Charlottetown Harbor. If you’ve ever wondered how massive cruise ships navigate the narrow channel into Charlottetown Harbour without hitting a sandbar, these two lights are the answer. By aligning the front and rear towers vertically, mariners know they are safely in the center of the deep-water channel.

The Front Range Light: This is the iconic postcard lighthouse sitting right on the shoreline at the end of York Lane. It’s a 40-foot square, tapered wooden tower with white shingles and a distinctive red vertical stripe. In 2026, it remains one of only two concrete-based lighthouses on the Island (following a major reconstruction of its foundation after a storm surge).

The Rear Range Light (The Apple Core): Located about 400 meters inland on Queen Elizabeth Drive, this lighthouse looks completely different. Built in 1968, it is a 60-foot octagonal concrete tower with a flared top, earning it the nickname The Apple Core. It is the only one of its design in Prince Edward Island.

6. Prince Edward Battery

Prince Edward Battery is a historic coastal defense site located on the southern tip of Victoria Park. While it may look like a peaceful spot for a picnic today, this battery was a critical piece of Charlottetown’s military infrastructure for over a century. It stands as the sole survivor of the various fortifications that once guarded the Birthplace of Confederation from potential sea-bound threats.

The Cannons: Explore the row of nine period guns that overlook the harbor. The collection includes:

Three 32-pounder smoothbore guns mounted on wooden ship-style carriages.

7. Harbour Experience on Solar Boat

The Harbour Experience on the Solar Boat is one of Charlottetown’s most unique and eco-friendly ways to see the city. Operated by Ride Solar, the vessel, named the Isola Solaretto, is actually a restored Canadian Heritage Vessel originally used as a "Vaporetto" at the 1967 Montreal Expo (the World's Fair). Today, it has been transformed into North America’s largest solar-powered tour boat, offering a whisper-quiet, emission-free cruise where you can literally see the batteries through a glass floor.

Silent Navigation: Because the boat is 100% solar-electric, there is no diesel noise, vibration, or smell. You can glide silently past the shoreline and hear the calls of the harbor birds clearly.

The Glass Floor View: Look down into the hull to see the battery banks and the electric motors that power your journey, all fueled by the massive solar panels on the roof.

Other Interesting Places

6 places

1. Queen Street

Queen Street is one of Charlottetown’s busiest and most walkable streets, lined with shops, cafés, theaters, and galleries. It stretches from the waterfront up to residential neighborhoods, offering a mix of local charm and urban energy.

The Anne of Green Gables Store: Located at 72 Queen Street, this is the ultimate pilgrimage site for fans. Housed in the historic 1855 Duncan Building, it offers everything from straw hats with red braids to raspberry cordial. Even if you aren't a superfan, the building itself is an excellent example of pre-1860s commercial design.

Charlottetown City Hall: This National Historic Site at 100 Queen Street is a Romanesque Revival masterpiece. Built in 1888, it originally housed the police station and firehall (with stables). Look for the commanding corner tower and the deep-set rounded arch windows.

2. Founders' Food Hall & Market

Founders' Food Hall & Market is the modern culinary and social heart of the Charlottetown waterfront. Housed in a massive, light-filled 1906 brick building that once served as the CN Railway Car Shop, it was meticulously restored and opened in 2019. It combines industrial heritage with a European-style food hall experience, featuring high ceilings, original steel beams, and a mezzanine with some of the best people-watching views in the city.

The Taste of the Island Crawl: With over 18 local vendors, this is the best place to sample PEI’s food scene under one roof.

Gourmet Comfort: Try the famous loaded fries at The Chip Shack or authentic Mexican street food from Taco Boyz.

3. Anne of Green Gables Store

The Anne of Green Gables Store on Queen Street is the ultimate destination for fans of L.M. Montgomery’s world-famous redhead. While there are several Anne shops across the Island, this downtown location is the flagship, serving as a year-round hub for literary pilgrims and souvenir hunters alike. Housed in a charming historic brick building, it offers a sophisticated retail experience that balances nostalgic whimsy with high-quality Island craftsmanship.

The AnnenEssentials: Find the most iconic souvenirs, including the classic straw hat with red braids, Kindred Spirits jewelry, and locally famous raspberry cordial.

Exclusive Literary Collections: The store carries the largest selection of L.M. Montgomery’s books in various editions, from beautiful leather-bound collector’s volumes to pocket-sized paperbacks. In 2026, look for the Kindred Spirits Publishing series, which features unique covers and photography by Island artists.

4. Anne of Green Gables Chocolates

Anne of Green Gables Chocolates is a beloved Prince Edward Island tradition that has been delivering happiness since 1999. While they have several seasonal spots, their two Charlottetown locations are the heart of the operation, offering handmade treats crafted from time-tested Island recipes.

You cannot visit without trying the COW Chips. These are arguably the most famous snack on the Island:

The Secret: Thick-cut, ridged potato chips (sourced from PEI’s famous spuds) are hand-dipped in premium milk chocolate.

5. Day Trip to Green Gables Heritage Place

A day trip to Green Gables Heritage Place in Cavendish is the ultimate pilgrimage for any Anne fan. Located about 40 kilometers (25 miles) north of Charlottetown, this National Historic Site is where L.M. Montgomery found the inspiration for the farmstead that became Anne Shirley’s home.

What to See and Do (In Season)

The Green Gables House: Walk through the meticulously restored rooms, including Anne’s bedroom (complete with the brown dress with puffed sleeves) and Marilla’s austere pantry.

6. The Great George Hotel

The Great George is a sprawling 5-star hotel that defines the historic character of downtown Charlottetown. Consisting of 17 meticulously restored heritage buildings spread across nearly an entire city block, it offers a scattered hotel experience where your room might be in a former bishop’s palace, a 19th-century warehouse, or a doctor's private residence.

The Pavilion (Main Lobby): Your journey starts at 58 Great George Street in the Pavilion building. Built in 1846, it originally served as a general store called London House before becoming a hotel that famously hosted delegates during the 1864 Charlottetown Conference.

A Landmark of Preservation: In the 1990s, local entrepreneurs Mike Cassidy and Kevin Murphy (of Murphy Hospitality Group) began buying and restoring the neglected buildings on this block. Today, it stands as the largest private heritage restoration project in Prince Edward Island’s history.
