World-Known Landmarks

15 places

1. West Edmonton Mall

World Waterpark: Home to the world’s largest indoor wave pool and over 17 waterslides. It maintains a tropical temperature of 28°C year-round.

Galaxyland Powered by Hasbro: One of the world's largest indoor amusement parks, featuring over 27 rides, including the triple-loop Mindbender and family favorites themed after brands like Monopoly and My Little Pony.

1. Fort Edmonton Park

Fort Edmonton Park is Canada's largest living history museum, offering an immersive journey through four distinct eras of Edmonton's development. Spread across 158 acres in the river valley, it uses meticulously reconstructed streets and costumed interpreters to bring the past to life.

Four Historical Eras: Visitors can explore the 1846 Hudson’s Bay Company Fort, followed by 1885 Street (Settlement Era), 1905 Street (Municipal Era), and 1920 Street (Metropolitan Era).

Vintage Transportation: The park features a functional steam train that transports guests from the entrance to the fort, as well as operational streetcars and horse-drawn carriages that travel the historic streets.

2. North Saskatchewan River Valley

The North Saskatchewan River Valley is a massive, winding ribbon of green that is 22 times the size of New York's Central Park. As the largest urban parkland in North America, it offers a wilderness experience right in the middle of a major city, connecting over 20 major parks and 150 kilometers of trails.

Traverse the Trail Network: Whether you are hiking, trail running, or mountain biking, the valley offers everything from paved, multi-use paths to single-track dirt trails that feel like deep forest.

High Level Bridge & Streetcar: For the best view in the city, ride the historic High Level Bridge Streetcar. It is the highest river crossing by streetcar in the world, providing a 360-degree panorama of the valley and the legislative grounds.

2. Royal Alberta Museum

The Royal Alberta Museum is the largest museum in Western Canada and a premier cultural landmark located in the heart of Edmonton's Arts District. Housed in a massive, modern facility that opened in 2018, it offers a deep dive into the natural and human history of Alberta through expansive, world-class galleries.

Human History Hall: This gallery explores the diverse stories of people who have called Alberta home for thousands of years. It features powerful displays on Indigenous cultures, including the sacred Manitou Stone, and stories of immigration and industry.

Natural History Hall: A favorite for all ages, this hall showcases the province’s diverse landscapes and prehistoric past. You can see life-sized Ice Age mammoths, ancient fossils, and an impressive collection of sparkling minerals and gems.

3. Rogers Place

Rogers Place is a state-of-the-art sports and entertainment arena in downtown Edmonton and the home of the NHL’s Edmonton Oilers. Opened in 2016, it anchors the ICE District and brings high-energy crowds to concerts, games, and other large events throughout the year.

This place’s history is closely tied to the city’s revitalization plans, transforming a once-quiet zone into a vibrant commercial and cultural hub. The arena’s glass design, massive scoreboard, and sound quality make it a standout even by global standards. Whether you're a hockey fan or not, the energy around Rogers Place during events is hard to miss.

Catch the Action: It is the home of the NHL's Edmonton Oilers and the WHL's Edmonton Oil Kings. With a capacity of over 18,500 for hockey and 20,000+ for concerts, it’s one of the most energetic venues in North America.

3. Art Gallery of Alberta

The Art Gallery of Alberta is a focal point of Edmonton’s downtown Arts District. Known as much for its striking architecture as for its collection, the gallery is a premier destination for visual arts enthusiasts in Western Canada.

Architectural Photography: Designed by Randall Stout, the building itself is a masterpiece. The exterior features a massive, winding stainless steel and glass borealis ribbon that mimics the movement of the Northern Lights and the North Saskatchewan River.

Diverse Collections: The gallery maintains over 6,000 works of art, ranging from historical and contemporary Canadian paintings and sculptures to significant international prints and photographs.

4. Elk Island National Park

Elk Island National Park is an ecological gem located 35 minutes east of Edmonton. It is Canada's only completely fenced national park and serves as a vital refuge for bison, elk, and over 250 species of birds. Unlike the mountain parks, Elk Island offers a serene, prairie safari experience through rolling aspen parkland and glassy lakes.

Bison Watching: This is the primary draw. You can often spot herds of Plains Bison along the Bison Loop Road (near the south entrance) or Wood Bison along the trails in the park’s southern half.

Stargazing & Northern Lights: As part of the Beaver Hills Dark Sky Preserve, the park is one of the best places in Alberta to see the Milky Way or the Aurora Borealis. The dark skies here are protected from city light pollution.

4. Ukrainian Cultural Heritage Village

The Ukrainian Cultural Heritage Village is an award-winning open-air museum located about 25 minutes east of Edmonton (just past Elk Island National Park). It offers a powerful, living history experience that recreates the life of early Ukrainian pioneers who settled in east-central Alberta between 1892 and 1930.

Interact with History: The village features over 30 historic structures, including sod houses, grain elevators, and blacksmith shops, all relocated from nearby communities. Costumed role-players act as the original settlers; they stay strictly in character and will speak to you as if it is still the early 20th century.

Cultural Traditions: Visitors can observe traditional crafts and activities such as woodstove cooking, gardening with heirloom seeds, and vintage blacksmithing.

5. Muttart Conservatory

The Muttart Conservatory is one of Edmonton’s most recognizable landmarks, featuring four iconic glass pyramids, each housing a different climate and collection of plants, that dominate the river valley skyline. This place showcases global ecosystems, including arid deserts, lush rainforests, and temperate landscapes, all in one spot.

Explore the Four Biomes: Each pyramid represents a different world climate, housing over 700 species of plants:

Tropical Pyramid: A lush, humid escape with towering palms, orchids, and a cascading waterfall. It’s the city's favorite refuge during the deep winter.

5. Rutherford House Provincial Historic Site

Rutherford House is a beautifully restored Edwardian mansion that served as the home of Alexander Cameron Rutherford, Alberta's first Premier. Located on the edge of the University of Alberta campus, it offers a glimpse into the lifestyle and politics of Edmonton's elite during the early 20th century.

Guided Heritage Tours: Costumed interpreters guide visitors through the elegantly furnished rooms, sharing stories about the Rutherford family's daily life and the historical significance of the premier's work.

Period Architecture and Decor: The house is a stunning example of Edwardian design, featuring ornate woodwork, stained glass, and authentic artifacts from the 1911 era.

6. Alberta Legislature Building

The Alberta Legislature Building, affectionately known by locals as The Ledge, is the seat of democracy in Alberta. Built between 1907 and 1913, this Beaux-Arts masterpiece sits on 57 acres of lush parkland overlooking the North Saskatchewan River valley. It is an architectural gem and a key landmark in Edmonton, known for its grand Beaux-Arts design and beautifully landscaped grounds. Visitors flock here to explore its historic halls and learn about Alberta’s government and political heritage.

Magic Spot: On the fifth floor, stand in a specific spot directly above the rotunda fountain. Due to the acoustic design of the dome, the sound of the falling water will seem to rain down on you from above rather than coming from below.

Rotunda: Marvel at the massive 16-ton Canadian marble pillars and the commemorative fountain built for Queen Elizabeth II’s 1959 visit. Look for the portraits of Alberta's Premiers and the hidden hamburger, a preserved 1969 burger kept in the Legislature Library.

Good to know. Note that the main public entrance for tours and visitors is located on the west side of the building.

6. Citadel Theatre

The Citadel Theatre is one of North America’s largest and most respected non-profit regional theatres. Located in the heart of downtown Edmonton, this massive complex is a cornerstone of the city’s vibrant Arts District and is renowned for its architectural beauty and commitment to high-quality performance.

Five Performance Spaces: The facility is unique in that it houses five distinct performance venues under one roof, ranging from the grand Shoctor Theatre for large-scale musicals to the more intimate Rice Theatre and Zeidler Hall.

Lee Pavilion: This stunning indoor glass garden features lush greenery, tropical plants, and a waterfall. It serves as a peaceful public space where visitors can relax before a show or during their lunch hour.

7. Fairmont Hotel Macdonald

The Fairmont Hotel Macdonald, known affectionately by locals as The Mac, is Edmonton's most storied hotel. Opened in 1915, this “Castle on the River” is a classic example of Canada’s grand railway hotels, blending chateau-style architecture with over a century of royal and celebrity history.

Confederation Lounge: Often cited as the best place for a drink in the city, this lounge feels like a private club from a bygone era, complete with library-style seating and a massive heritage painting of the Fathers of Confederation.

Famous Patio: During the summer, the hotel's terrace offers the most iconic panoramic view of the North Saskatchewan River Valley. It’s a premier spot for high tea or an evening cocktail while watching the sunset over the valley.

7. St. Joseph's Basilica

St. Joseph’s Basilica is a striking Roman Catholic church located just west of downtown Edmonton, known for its towering twin spires and stained-glass windows. The building blends Gothic Revival architecture with warm stone and wood interiors, offering a peaceful space for reflection and admiration. This place serves as the archdiocese’s central church and has hosted major religious and civic events, including a visit from Pope John Paul II in 1984.

Architectural Splendor: The building features a classic cruciform design with stunning stained-glass windows that depict various biblical scenes and saints, casting vibrant colors throughout the sanctuary.

Historical Significance: Originally started in 1924 and completed in 1963, it was designated a minor basilica by Pope John Paul II during his visit to Edmonton in 1984, an honor reserved for churches of particular historical or architectural importance.

8. Walterdale Bridge

The Walterdale Bridge is one of Edmonton’s most striking modern engineering feats, serving as a sleek gateway between the city’s south side and its downtown core. Replacing an older truss bridge, this signature cable-supported arch structure has quickly become a favorite subject for local photographers due to its graceful, minimalist design and bright white finish.

Architectural Significance: The bridge features two towering 56-meter-high arches that lean slightly toward each other, creating a unique silhouette that stands out against the lush greenery of the River Valley.

Pedestrian Experience: A dedicated, wide pedestrian path on the east side of the bridge offers safe and scenic passage for walkers and cyclists, providing some of the best unobstructed views of the Edmonton skyline and the North Saskatchewan River Valley.

Outdoor Attractions

5 places

1. William Hawrelak Park

Hawrelak Park, originally known as William Hawrelak Park, is a 68-hectare centerpiece of Edmonton’s river valley. It is widely regarded as one of the city's premier locations for large-scale community gatherings and outdoor recreation, though it is currently undergoing significant rehabilitation to preserve it for future generations.

A Festival Epicenter: Historically, the park is the primary home for major Edmonton events, including the Heritage Festival, which celebrates multiculturalism with food and performances, and the Silver Skate Festival, Edmonton’s longest-running winter festival.

Heritage Amphitheatre: This is Western Canada’s largest outdoor covered amphitheatre. It features excellent acoustics and is a favorite venue for the Symphony Under the Sky and various theatrical performances.

2. Mill Creek Ravine Park

Mill Creek Ravine is a beloved tributary of the North Saskatchewan River Valley, winding through some of Edmonton's most established neighborhoods. It is cherished by locals for its rustic charm, dense tree canopy, and peaceful atmosphere that makes you feel miles away from the city despite being just minutes from downtown.

Historic Trestle Bridges: The ravine is famous for its collection of historic wooden trestle bridges that offer perfect vantage points for viewing the creek and the changing seasons.

Off-Leash Adventures: It features one of the most popular off-leash dog areas in the city, particularly the Mill Creek Ravine South, where pets can roam through varied terrain.

3. Rundle Park

Rundle Park is an expansive recreational hub in Edmonton's northeast river valley, offering a massive array of sports facilities and family-friendly amenities that make it one of the city's most active green spaces.

Year-Round Recreation: The park features a unique IceWay during winter, a scenic outdoor skating trail that winds through the trees, while summer brings visitors to its popular Disc Golf Course and Par 3 Golf Course.

Family Amenities: It is a top choice for gatherings due to its numerous picnic sites, creative playgrounds, and several small lakes that are often used for paddleboarding or RC boating.

4. Terwillegar Park

Terwillegar Park is a sprawling 186-hectare riverfront destination in southwest Edmonton. It is widely considered one of the city's best locations for those seeking a more rugged, nature-focused escape, and it is a major hub for both outdoor enthusiasts and pet owners.

Elite Dog Haven: The park is most famous for its massive multi-use area, often referred to as Terwillegar Dog Park. It is one of the largest off-leash areas in North America, offering vast open fields and river access where dogs can run and swim freely.

Terwillegar Park Footbridge: A modern architectural highlight, this stressed-ribbon footbridge connects the park to the Oleskiw River Valley Park across the river. It is a favorite spot for walkers and cyclists, providing beautiful elevated views of the water and the valley.

5. Sir Winston Churchill Square

Sir Winston Churchill Square is a vibrant public space located in the heart of Edmonton, known for hosting events, festivals, and cultural celebrations. Surrounded by the city’s major attractions, including the Art Gallery of Alberta and City Hall, the square offers visitors a lively atmosphere and a chance to engage with Edmonton’s rich arts and culture scene.

Year-Round Festival Hub: From the Edmonton International Fringe Festival’s outdoor events in the summer to the festive holiday light displays in the winter, there is almost always a community event or market happening here.

Architectural Gateway: The square offers some of the best unobstructed views of the city's modern architecture, including the striking glass pyramids of the nearby Muttart Conservatory in the distance and the borealis ribbon of the Art Gallery of Alberta.

Other Interesting Places

7 places

1. High Level Bridge Streetcar

The High Level Bridge Streetcar offers one of the most unique and scenic ways to experience Edmonton's river valley. Operated by the Edmonton Radial Railway Society, this historic transit experience takes passengers across the top of the High Level Bridge, providing breathtaking 360-degree views from over 150 feet above the North Saskatchewan River.

Historic Fleet: The society operates a rotating fleet of beautifully restored streetcars from all over the world, including cars from Osaka, Japan, and Melbourne, Australia, as well as local Edmonton heritage cars.

Bridge Crossing: The highlight of the trip is the slow-paced crossing of the High Level Bridge. It is currently the highest streetcar river crossing in the world, offering a perspective of the city skyline and the lush river valley that you can't get any other way.

2. Old Strathcona Farmers' Market

The Old Strathcona Farmers' Market is Edmonton’s original indoor farmers' market, housed in a sprawling historic bus barn. Since 1983, it has been a Saturday morning tradition for locals, offering a vibrant, community-focused atmosphere where everything sold is made, baked, or grown by the vendors themselves.

Diverse Vendor Mix: With over 130 stalls, you’ll find everything from seasonal Alberta produce and locally raised meats to artisanal breads, handmade jewelry, and unique pottery.

Authentic Local Flavors: It is a prime spot to pick up classic regional treats like Ukrainian pierogies, locally fermented kombucha, and Saskatoon berry preserves.

3. Edmonton City Hall

Edmonton City Hall is a striking civic landmark that combines modern architecture with a rich sense of community. The building is home to the city's municipal offices, but it also serves as a public gathering place, featuring an impressive open atrium and various art displays.

What makes this place special is its stunning glass-and-steel design, which allows natural light to fill the space, creating a welcoming and open atmosphere. City Hall is also known for its seasonal events and live performances, making it a lively part of the city throughout the year.

City Hall Plaza and Wading Pool: In the summer, the large outdoor plaza features a shallow wading pool that is a favorite for families looking to cool off. In the winter, this same area is transformed into a scenic public skating rink, often accompanied by festive music and colorful lights.

4. Edmonton Convention Centre

The Edmonton Convention Centre is an architectural landmark built into the side of the North Saskatchewan River Valley. It is recognized globally for its innovative stepped design, which allows for stunning panoramic views of the river valley from multiple levels.

Sustainability Focus: The facility is a leader in environmental stewardship, featuring one of Canada’s largest building-integrated photovoltaic (solar cell) glass installations. This initiative helps power the building while showcasing its commitment to a green future.

A Living Atrium: The interior features a massive greenhouse-like atrium filled with lush plants and natural light. This serves as a serene public space where visitors can enjoy the greenery regardless of the weather outside.

5. 100 Street Funicular

The 100 Street Funicular, formally known as the Mechanized River Valley Access, is a modern engineering marvel that provides a seamless and accessible link between Edmonton's downtown core and the vast North Saskatchewan River Valley. It is much more than just a transit vehicle; it's a scenic destination that offers some of the best views of the city's natural landscape.

Scenic River Valley Access: The funicular features a glass-walled cabin that descends from the top of the bank near the Hotel Macdonald down to a beautiful promenade, offering an effortless way to navigate the steep terrain.

Frederick G. Todd Lookout: At the midway point, visitors can step onto a stunning cantilevered lookout that extends over the river valley. It provides an unobstructed panoramic view of the North Saskatchewan River and the Walterdale Bridge.

6. Whyte Avenue

Whyte Avenue, the heart of the Old Strathcona neighborhood, is Edmonton’s most iconic and vibrant shopping and entertainment district. Blending historic charm with a bohemian spirit, it is a bustling corridor known for its walkable streets, independent boutiques, and energetic nightlife.

Shopping and Boutiques: The avenue is a haven for unique finds, featuring everything from curated vintage clothing at the Old Strathcona Antique Mall nearby to local designer wares and specialized bookstores.

Dining and Nightlife: As a culinary hotspot, it offers an incredible density of pubs, high-end cocktail bars, and diverse eateries. It is particularly famous for its lively patio scene in the summer months.

7. Winspear Centre

The Winspear Centre is widely considered one of the finest concert halls in North America, renowned specifically for its near-perfect acoustics. Located in Edmonton's downtown Arts District, it serves as the home of the Edmonton Symphony Orchestra and hosts a diverse range of international performers.

The hall was designed specifically to provide a superior listening experience, with every architectural detail, from the adjustable acoustic canopies to the curvature of the walls, engineered to enhance sound quality.

Davis Concert Organ: A centerpiece of the hall, this massive pipe organ features 6,538 pipes and is often showcased in dedicated organ recitals and symphonic works.
