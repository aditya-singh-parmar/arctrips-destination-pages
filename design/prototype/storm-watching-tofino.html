Storm watching in Tofino

From November the Pacific throws everything it has at this coast. Seven places to watch it from, three of them fully exposed and one of them behind glass.

The season

November to March. Pacific storms are stronger, the waves are bigger, the wind is louder and the beaches feel more dramatic.

The peak

November to February bring the most frequent and the most dramatic storms of the year.

Time of day

Daylight, always. Low tide gives you more sand to walk; high tide gives you the bigger waves, but only from a raised, safe place. Never walk a beach in the dark in storm season.

How long to stay

Two to three days. Storms do not arrive to order, and two nights gives you a real chance of dramatic weather plus a calm break for a beach walk.

Where to watch

Seven places, from the most exposed to the driest

The order is the amount of weather you are willing to stand in. The last one is a room with a window, and on the worst days it is the right answer.

Fully exposed

01 to 03

Open to the Pacific with nothing in front of you. The biggest waves, and the least forgiving ground.

01

Best forBig waves, beach views, photos

Fully exposed

Cox Bay

Cox Bay is one of the most famous storm-watching spots in Tofino. It is wide, open, and exposed to the Pacific Ocean. This makes it a great place to see large waves roll in. On stormy days, the waves can look huge. You may also see surfers on calmer winter days, but do not enter the water unless you are trained and conditions are safe.

Good to know. Stay far back from the water. Waves can reach higher up the beach than expected.

02

Best forWide views, long walks

Fully exposed

Long Beach

Long Beach is part of Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. It is one of the most famous beaches on Vancouver Island. It is wide, long, and open to the ocean. This makes it a powerful place to watch winter waves. You can walk on the sand when conditions are safe, or watch from a safer distance.

Good to know. You may need a park pass. Always follow Parks Canada safety signs and closures.

Fully exposedA headland and white spray

Pettinger Point

Accessible via a trail from Cox Bay, this headland offers a spectacular vantage point where waves explode into white spray against the cliffs.

Easier, and part sheltered

04 to 06

Close to town, quicker to reach, and each has somewhere to retreat to when it turns.

04

Best forEasy access, beach walks, families

Part sheltered

Chesterman Beach

Chesterman Beach is a favorite place for beach walks and storm watching. It is close to town and easy to access. The beach is long, sandy, and scenic. On stormy days, you can watch waves crash along the shore. On calmer days, it is a great place for a winter walk.

Good to know. Watch the tide. Parts of the beach may be harder to walk during high tide.

05

Best forShort walk, forest trail, quiet views

Part sheltered

Tonquin Beach

Tonquin Beach is smaller and more sheltered than the larger surf beaches. It is reached by a forest trail from town.

This beach can be beautiful in stormy weather, but the trail may be wet and slippery. It is better for a short visit than a long storm watching session.

Good to know. Wear proper shoes because the trail can be muddy.

06

Best forA viewing deck and somewhere warm

Part sheltered

Wickaninnish Beach and Kwisitis Visitor Centre

The Kwisitis Visitor Centre area is a good choice if you want a safer, more comfortable storm watching spot. The viewing deck gives you a strong view of the beach and waves.

This is also a good place to learn about the area’s natural history, wildlife, and local First Nations culture.

Good to know. This is a good option when the beach itself feels too rough.

From behind glass

07

The version of this you can do in dry socks.

IndoorsA window, a fire and dry socks

Oceanfront Resorts and Cabins

One of the best ways to storm watch in Tofino is from inside. Many oceanfront hotels, cabins, and resorts have large windows facing the water.

This is perfect if the weather is too rough to be outside. You can watch the storm with a warm drink, a blanket, and dry socks.

The Wickaninnish Inn is the most iconic destination for this experience, specifically built on a rocky headland to withstand and showcase the Pacific's power.

The Pointe Restaurant at the Wickaninnish Inn gives you 240 degrees of waves against the rock below while you eat, and every guest room has floor-to-ceiling windows and a fireplace. The inn lends out rain slickers and gumboots to anyone who wants to go out onto the sand.

Long Beach Lodge Resort faces the swells rolling into Cox Bay, which is known for waves reaching up to 12 metres. Its Great Room is a single large space with oversized windows and a central fireplace, and you can often watch surfers working the winter breaks from the deck.

Pacific Sands Beach Resort also sits on Cox Bay and sells storm watching packages: beachfront suites with kitchens and balconies, and extras like arrival wine, hot chocolate and rain gear.

Safety

Four rules, and none of them are optional

Storm watching is not standing in danger. It is watching something dangerous from somewhere it cannot reach you.

Never stand near driftwood

A few inches of water will lift a full-size log and throw it. In a storm, keep well up the beach and well away from the log line.

Sneaker waves take people off ledges

Do not stand on tidal rocks or on a headland that is taking spray. The wave that reaches you arrives without a set warning you first.

Storm surge beats the tide table

Water can rise well above the predicted height and cut off your exit. Check the tide, then avoid the beach at peak high water anyway.

Waterproof from the boots up

Rain jacket, waterproof trousers and gumboots. The rain here blows sideways, so an umbrella is useless. Read the CoastSmart and Parks Canada wave hazard signs at the beach entrance before you step onto the sand.

Questions

Asked before people book

Open the ones you need. Everything here is answered in the same words the guide uses.

Is storm watching in Tofino dangerous?

It can be dangerous if you get too close to the ocean. Watch from a safe distance, avoid wet rocks, and never turn your back on the waves.

What are king tides?

King tides are extra high tides. In Tofino they can happen around the full moon or new moon, and during storm season they can make the ocean rise much higher than normal. That can make beach entrances unsafe and some beach walks impossible. During a king tide, choose a safe viewpoint instead of walking close to the water.

What should you wear for storm watching in Tofino?

Dress for rain, wind and cold air: a waterproof rain jacket, waterproof trousers, a warm sweater or fleece, a hat, gloves, warm socks, waterproof boots, extra dry clothes, a small backpack and your phone in a waterproof case. Umbrellas are not much use in strong wind.

Can you walk on the beach during a storm?

Sometimes. A beach walk can be safe in light rain, at low tide, in calmer conditions. In a strong storm, at high tide or with large waves, watch from a safe viewpoint instead. Ask yourself whether the tide is coming in, whether waves are reaching high up the beach, whether there are logs near the water, whether the wind is too strong, whether the beach is open, and whether you can get back. If you are not sure, do not go.

What is the best time of day for storm watching?

Daylight. Winter days are short, so plan around them. Low tide is better for beach walks because there is more sand. High tide is better for dramatic waves, but only from a safe raised place. Do not walk beaches in the dark during storm season.

Can you storm watch with kids?

Yes, and children usually love it. Keep them close, do not let them run near the waterline, and choose safer viewing areas: the Kwisitis Visitor Centre viewing area, an oceanfront room, short beach visits at low tide, or a rainforest trail on a calmer day. Bring snacks, dry clothes and warm drinks.

Can you storm watch with dogs?

Yes, but check local beach rules first. Some beaches have leash rules or seasonal restrictions, and during storm season a leash is the safe choice anyway. Waves, driftwood and fast-moving water are all risks, so keep dogs away from the surf line. Bring a towel and a dry blanket for the car.

What else is there to do in storm season?

Rainforest trails, local cafes, seafood, a spa treatment, watching surfers from shore, shops and galleries, moody beach photography, a cabin with a fireplace, and Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. Winter here is best taken slowly: one or two things a day, and time left over to sit still.

How many days do you need for storm watching in Tofino?

Two to three days. Storms do not follow a perfect schedule, so at least two nights gives you a better chance of dramatic weather, plus calmer breaks for beach walks.
