World-Known Landmarks

World-Known Landmarks

7 places

1. Delta Bessborough Hotel

This place’s history began in the 1930s when the Canadian National Railway commissioned it as part of a chain of luxury hotels across the country. Over time, it became a symbol of Saskatoon's hospitality and has hosted royalty, celebrities, and national events. Today, it serves as both a working hotel and a historic point of interest in the city.

Tudor-style towers and stone façade

2. University of Saskatchewan Campus

The University of Saskatchewan campus is a vibrant and historic space that blends stunning architecture with lush greenery. Located along the South Saskatchewan River, it offers both educational and cultural experiences for visitors.

This location's significance stems from its role as a major educational institution in Canada and its deep roots in research and innovation. With beautiful landscaping and iconic buildings, the campus is not only a place for students but also an ideal destination for tourists looking to explore Saskatoon’s history and natural beauty.

Explore the historic College Building

3. Broadway Bridge

The Broadway Bridge is one of Saskatoon’s most iconic river crossings, linking the downtown core with the vibrant Broadway district. Known for its graceful arches and art deco design, the bridge offers scenic views of the South Saskatchewan River and surrounding cityscape.

This place’s history began in 1932 during the Great Depression, built as a government relief project that provided much-needed jobs and lasting infrastructure. Its wide pedestrian walkways make it a favorite for locals walking or biking between neighborhoods. As both a functional link and visual landmark, it plays a daily role in Saskatoon life.

Concrete arches with city backdrop

4. Traffic Bridge

The Traffic Bridge is a steel truss bridge that offers both a practical crossing and a tribute to Saskatoon’s transportation past. The current structure opened in 2018 as a modern replica of the original 1907 bridge, which was the first to link both sides of the river.

This place’s history includes its role in connecting early settlers and serving as a key crossing for pedestrians, horses, and cars alike. The new bridge retains the old design’s silhouette while meeting today’s safety standards. It’s a visual link between the city’s roots and its evolving skyline.

Steel truss design with historic shape

5. Wanuskewin Heritage Park

Wanuskewin Heritage Park is an essential cultural and historical destination just outside Saskatoon, offering a deep dive into the rich Indigenous heritage of the region. The park spans over 1,600 acres and is home to archaeological sites, walking trails, and interactive exhibits.

What makes this place special is its importance as one of the oldest continuously inhabited sites in North America, with human activity dating back over 6,000 years. Visitors can explore the park's history through guided tours, traditional dances, and artwork, making it a unique blend of nature and culture.

Walking the trails through scenic landscapes

6. St. John's Anglican Cathedral

St. John's Anglican Cathedral is a historic and peaceful space located in the heart of Saskatoon. What makes this place special is its blend of historical significance and beautiful outdoor space, providing visitors with a serene escape in an urban setting. The cathedral itself is a magnificent example of Gothic Revival architecture, and the grounds offer a lovely spot for reflection or a leisurely walk through Saskatoon’s past.

Explore the Gothic Revival architecture

Attend a service or community event

7. Nutrien Tower

Nutrien Tower is Saskatoon’s tallest building, rising 18 stories above River Landing with a sleek glass design and modern green technologies. As the headquarters for Nutrien, the world’s largest provider of crop inputs, the building symbolizes Saskatoon’s growing presence in global agriculture and innovation.

This place’s history began in 2020 as part of a major redevelopment of the River Landing area, aiming to bring together business, culture, and public space. Its sharp angles and reflective façade stand out against the riverbank skyline. Though primarily an office space, the surrounding area is open to the public and great for city views.

Modern architecture along the riverfront

Art and History Lovers

Art and History Lovers

6 places

1. Remai Modern Art Gallery

Remai Modern Art Gallery in Saskatoon stands as a striking example of contemporary art, showcasing cutting-edge works in a sleek, architecturally impressive building. The gallery’s spacious design allows for a variety of exhibits, from visual arts to interactive installations.

This location's significance stems from its role in shaping Saskatoon’s cultural landscape, offering a vibrant space for local and international artists to share their creativity. With its diverse collections and commitment to contemporary art, the Remai Modern is an essential stop for any art enthusiast visiting the city.

World-class contemporary art exhibitions

2. Western Development Museum

The Western Development Museum in Saskatoon is a captivating journey through the province's past, showcasing the rich history and heritage of Saskatchewan. The museum houses over 60,000 artifacts, making it a must-see for anyone interested in the development of the Canadian prairies.

This place's history spans from the early days of Indigenous cultures to the region's industrial growth, with exhibits highlighting everything from farming tools to vintage cars. The museum’s Streetscape exhibit is a life-sized recreation of a 1910s Saskatchewan town, offering visitors a true step back in time.

Streetscape with historic buildings and shops

3. Ukrainian Museum of Canada

The Ukrainian Museum of Canada in Saskatoon offers a fascinating glimpse into the history, culture, and traditions of the Ukrainian-Canadian community. Founded in 1944, this museum is dedicated to preserving and showcasing the rich heritage of Ukrainian immigrants in Canada.

What makes this place special is its extensive collection of Ukrainian artifacts, folk art, and historical exhibits that bring to life the immigrant experience. Visitors can explore beautifully crafted items such as traditional clothing, religious icons, and intricate textiles, making this museum a must-visit for those interested in Canadian multiculturalism.

Admire the vibrant Ukrainian folk art

4. Museum of Antiquities

The Museum of Antiquities in Saskatoon is an exceptional destination for history enthusiasts. The museum showcases a vast collection of ancient artifacts from around the world, spanning thousands of years of human history.

This location’s significance stems from its impressive array of relics, including Greek and Roman sculptures, Egyptian mummies, and artifacts from the ancient Near East. It's a unique place to explore the ancient world without leaving Saskatchewan, making it a must-see for those interested in global history.

Explore ancient Egyptian mummies and artifacts

5. Broadway Theatre

The Broadway Theatre in Saskatoon stands as a beloved cultural hub, offering a unique blend of cinema, live performances, and community events. This location's significance stems from its deep roots in the city’s arts scene, having served the community since 1949.

The historic building features an inviting vintage atmosphere, where visitors can enjoy a variety of programming, from independent films to live theatre productions. With its mix of old-world charm and modern entertainment, the Broadway Theatre is a must-see for anyone seeking a cultural experience in Saskatoon.

Watch independent and foreign films

6. Persephone Theatre

Persephone Theatre is one of Saskatoon’s most beloved cultural landmarks, offering a diverse range of performances year-round. This professional theatre is known for its commitment to showcasing both classic and contemporary works, making it a must-see for any theatre lover.

This place's history dates back to 1984, when it first opened its doors, and since then, it has become a pillar of the local arts scene. With its intimate setting and exceptional productions, Persephone Theatre offers a unique cultural experience that is both enriching and entertaining.

High-quality theatrical performances

Outdoor Attractions

Outdoor Attractions

7 places

1. Meewasin Valley Trail

Meewasin Valley Trail is one of Saskatoon’s most beloved outdoor spaces, offering a scenic path along the South Saskatchewan River. Spanning over 60 kilometers, this trail connects various parts of the city and provides visitors with beautiful views of nature and wildlife.

What makes this place special is the perfect blend of urban accessibility and natural beauty, making it an ideal location for walking, cycling, or enjoying a peaceful day outdoors. The trail is named after the Cree word "Meewasin," which means "beautiful," reflecting the stunning landscapes visitors will experience.

Walking along the riverfront

2. Beaver Creek Conservation Area

Beaver Creek Conservation Area is a stunning natural escape located just outside of Saskatoon, offering visitors a peaceful retreat into the wilderness. With over 600 acres of forest, wetlands, and grasslands, it’s a perfect spot for nature lovers and outdoor enthusiasts.

What makes this place special is its incredible biodiversity, with a variety of wildlife and over 200 species of birds. The area is home to several endangered species, making it a vital spot for conservation efforts.

Walking the scenic nature trails

3. Saskatoon Forestry Farm Park & Zoo

Saskatoon Forestry Farm Park & Zoo is a unique blend of nature and wildlife, offering visitors a chance to explore both plant life and animal exhibits. Located just outside downtown Saskatoon, the park is home to diverse species from around the world, as well as a beautiful setting for outdoor recreation.

This place’s history dates back to 1906 when it was originally established as a forestry research station, before transitioning into a public zoo in the 1960s. With scenic walking paths, lush gardens, and family-friendly activities, it’s a perfect spot for a relaxing day out in the city.

Wide range of animal exhibits

4. Nutrien Playland at Kinsmen Park

Nutrien Playland at Kinsmen Park is a vibrant family-friendly amusement park located in the heart of Saskatoon. With its colorful rides, games, and attractions, it's the perfect destination for visitors of all ages looking for fun and excitement.

This place's history dates back to 1966, making it a long-standing symbol of community entertainment in the city. The park features a vintage carousel, adding a nostalgic touch to its modern offerings.

Classic carousel ride with historic charm

5. Diefenbaker Park

Diefenbaker Park is a spacious public park in Saskatoon’s south end, popular for sledding in winter and fireworks in summer. It features wide open fields, tree-shaded picnic spots, and views of the South Saskatchewan River.

This place’s history ties to the 13th Prime Minister of Canada, John Diefenbaker, for whom it was named in recognition of his Saskatchewan roots. Locals gather here year-round for casual sports, family outings, and community events. Its large toboggan hill is one of the city’s best-known winter attractions.

Open green space and river views

6. Gabriel Dumont Park

Gabriel Dumont Park is a peaceful riverside green space located just south of downtown Saskatoon. It features shaded picnic areas, wild prairie grasses, and a boat launch for paddlers and anglers. This place’s history honors Métis leader Gabriel Dumont, who played a key role in the North-West Resistance and is an important figure in Canadian history.

The park serves as both a recreational area and a cultural site, reflecting the city’s Indigenous roots and connection to the land. It's one of the quietest and most nature-focused parks within city limits.

Riverside paths and quiet scenery

7. Cranberry Flats Conservation Area

Cranberry Flats Conservation Area is a peaceful natural retreat located just outside of Saskatoon, offering stunning views of the South Saskatchewan River. The area features picturesque trails, wetlands, and rich wildlife, making it a favorite spot for outdoor activities.

This location's significance stems from its role in preserving local ecosystems and providing a habitat for many species of plants and animals. Visitors can enjoy the beauty of the landscape while learning about its ecological importance, making it a great destination for nature lovers and photographers.

Hiking the scenic riverfront trails

Other Interesting Places

Other Interesting Places

7 places

1. Museum of Natural Sciences

The Museum of Natural Sciences in Saskatoon offers an engaging look at the natural world through its diverse exhibits and educational displays. With a focus on the local environment, the museum features exhibits on everything from fossils to geology, botany, and wildlife.

This place's history dates back to 1906, making it one of the oldest museums in the area, and it has since become a cornerstone of Saskatoon’s cultural and educational landscape. Visitors can explore hands-on displays that bring the wonders of science and nature to life, making it a must-see for anyone interested in natural history.

Fossils and prehistoric animal exhibits

2. Saskatoon Farmers Market

The Saskatoon Farmers Market, particularly its outdoor portion, is a vibrant and lively spot where locals and tourists gather to shop, taste, and enjoy fresh, locally grown produce and handmade goods. What makes this place special is its variety, offering everything from organic fruits and vegetables to artisanal breads, crafts, and live entertainment.

This market has a deep connection to Saskatoon’s agricultural heritage, making it a key spot to experience the city’s local flavors and talents. This market is one of the oldest farmers' markets in the region, a true staple for the community.

Sampling fresh, local fruits and veggies

3. Ashworth Holmes Park

Ashworth Holmes Park is a charming urban park in the heart of Saskatoon, offering a peaceful environment for relaxation and outdoor activities. The park features expansive green spaces, well-maintained walking paths, and scenic views of the South Saskatchewan River.

This location's significance stems from its central role in the community, providing a place for both recreation and local events. With its combination of natural beauty and recreational facilities, Ashworth Holmes Park is an ideal spot for a casual day out in the city.

Scenic riverside walking and cycling paths

4. SaskTel Centre

SaskTel Centre in Saskatoon is a premier event venue that hosts concerts, sports events, and entertainment shows throughout the year. Known for its modern facilities and large capacity, the arena attracts top-tier artists and teams, making it a must-visit for anyone interested in live events.

This location's significance stems from being the home of the Saskatoon Blades, the city’s beloved junior hockey team, and for bringing high-profile performances to the area. Whether attending a game or a concert, the SaskTel Centre offers an unforgettable experience.

Watch a live Saskatoon Blades hockey game

5. Victoria Park Pagoda

Victoria Park Pagoda is an iconic landmark located in the heart of Saskatoon, offering visitors a blend of history, culture, and scenic beauty. The pagoda is a beautiful structure that sits atop a hill, overlooking Victoria Park and providing stunning views of the South Saskatchewan River.

What makes this place special is its blend of architectural charm and its role as a symbol of the city’s commitment to preserving natural beauty. The pagoda, built in the early 1900s, has stood as a focal point for both locals and tourists, making it a must-see in the area.

Enjoy panoramic views of the river

6. Midtown Plaza

Midtown Plaza is Saskatoon’s central shopping mall, located downtown and directly connected to major hotels and event venues. It offers a wide mix of national retailers, local boutiques, and food options spread across two levels.

This place’s history began in the late 1960s when it replaced the city’s old railway station, transforming the area into a retail hub. Over the decades, it has evolved alongside the city, remaining a key spot for errands, fashion, and people-watching. Its location makes it an easy indoor break during winter or busy travel days.

Clothing, tech, and specialty shops

7. Broadway Avenue

Broadway Avenue is one of Saskatoon’s most beloved streets, known for its mix of heritage buildings, independent shops, and artsy atmosphere. Running through the Nutana neighborhood, it offers cafés, bookstores, vintage boutiques, and live performance venues in a walkable setting.

This place’s history goes back to Saskatoon’s early days, serving as one of the first major commercial districts outside the city center. It has since developed into a cultural corridor that blends creativity, food, and community. Locals and visitors come here for everything from weekend brunch to music festivals.

Locally owned cafés and bakeries
