Jasper

25 places, written up one at a time

1. Maligne Lake & Spirit Island

Maligne Lake is the largest natural lake in the Canadian Rockies and is world-famous for its bright, glacial-blue water. Tucked away in the middle of this 22-kilometer (14-mile) lake is Spirit Island, a tiny, sacred grove of trees surrounded by massive mountain peaks.

While the 2024 wildfires affected parts of the surrounding valley, the lake area is open and provides a beautiful look at how the forest begins to grow back.

Boat Cruise: This is the most popular way to see the lake. The 90-minute Classic Cruise takes you deep into the mountains to Spirit Island. The boats are heated and have big glass windows so you can see the views even if it’s chilly outside.

2. Athabasca Falls

Athabasca Falls is known as the most powerful waterfall in the Canadian Rockies. While it isn't the tallest, the sheer amount of water from the Athabasca River slamming into a narrow canyon creates a thunderous roar you can feel in your chest.

This site is fully open and remains a must-see stop along the Icefields Parkway. The area around the falls is also a great place to see how the forest is naturally regrowing after the 2024 wildfires.

Main Overlook: Just a few steps from the parking lot, you’ll find the primary viewing platform. This is the best spot to see the river take its 23-meter (75-foot) drop into the gorge.

3. Athabasca Glacier

The Athabasca Glacier is the most visited glacier in North America and a true wonder of the natural world. It is a massive toe of ice that flows down from the Columbia Icefield, which is the largest icefield in the Rocky Mountains.

While it is melting (receding) every year due to climate change, it is still over 300 meters (1,000 feet) thick in some spots, that's as deep as the Eiffel Tower is tall.

Ice Explorer Adventure: This is the most famous tour. You board a giant, six-wheeled bus called an Ice Explorer (one of only 23 in the world) that drives you directly onto the glacier. You get to step out onto the ancient ice for about 20 minutes.

4. The Icefields Parkway (Hwy 93N)

The Icefields Parkway is widely considered one of the most beautiful drives in the world. This 232-kilometer (144-mile) highway connects the town of Jasper to Lake Louise, winding through the heart of the Canadian Rockies.

While the drive takes about 3 hours without stopping, most travelers spend a whole day (8, 10 hours) exploring the incredible lakes and viewpoints along the way.

Peyto Lake: This is the famous wolf-shaped lake. After a short 10-minute walk from the parking lot to the wooden platform, you’ll see water so bright blue it looks like it was painted.

5. Maligne Canyon

Maligne Canyon is the deepest canyon in Jasper National Park, with some sections dropping more than 50 meters (160 feet) down. It was carved out of limestone over thousands of years by the rushing Maligne River. It is famous for its narrow walls, hidden waterfalls, and unique rock formations.

Due to damage from the 2024 wildfires and the risk of landslides on the steep slopes, Maligne Canyon remains closed for the 2026 season. Parks Canada is working hard to fix the bridges and trails so they are safe again. While you cannot hike the main canyon trail right now, you can still see parts of the river from the nearby Fifth and Sixth Bridge areas, which were deemed safe to reopen.

What to Do and See (When Open)

6. Columbia Icefield Skywalk

The Columbia Icefield Skywalk is a world-class feat of engineering that lets you walk on air. It is a glass-bottomed observation platform that curves out from a sheer cliff, hanging 280 meters (918 feet) above the Sunwapta Valley. If you want a massive adrenaline rush and the best views of the high peaks, this is the place to be.

The Skywalk is closed during winter season. Because it is located in a high-altitude mountain pass, it only operates during the warmer months when the ice on the glass can be safely managed.

Glass Floor: You walk out onto a transparent horseshoe-shaped bridge. Looking straight down between your feet, you’ll see the Sunwapta River winding like a silver thread nearly 1,000 feet below.

7. Mount Edith Cavell & Angel Glacier

Mount Edith Cavell is one of the most striking peaks in the park, standing at 3,363 meters (over 11,000 feet). It is famous for its massive, vertical north face and the stunning Angel Glacier, which clings to the cliffside in a shape that looks just like an angel with outspread wings.

As of March 2026, the Cavell Road is closed to all vehicles and hikers. This road was heavily impacted by the 2024 wildfires in the first few kilometers. Because the trees are gone, the slopes are now unstable, and there is a high risk of falling rocks and landslides. Parks Canada is working on a plan to fix the road, but it will remain closed until further notice to keep everyone safe.

What to Do and See (When it Reopens)

8. Jasper SkyTram

The Jasper SkyTram is the highest and longest aerial tramway in Canada. In just 7 minutes, it whisks you up to an elevation of 2,263 meters (7,425 feet) on the side of Whistlers Mountain. It is the absolute best way to see the big picture of the Canadian Rockies without having to hike for hours.

The SkyTram is closed during the winter season. It is officially scheduled to open for the 2026 season on April 15, 2026.

The Flight: Each tram car carries about 26 people and a flight attendant who tells you cool facts about the mountain. Keep your eyes peeled as you go up, you can often see grizzly bears or mountain goats on the slopes below.

9. Medicine Lake (The Disappearing Lake)

Medicine Lake is one of the most mysterious places in the world. For half the year, it looks like a beautiful, deep blue mountain lake. But for the other half, the water completely vanishes, leaving behind a giant mudflat with a small stream running through it.

In early spring, the lake is in its disappeared state. You will see a snowy basin rather than a full lake, which is a great time to see the drain in action.

Watch the Bathtub Drain: The lake doesn't have a normal river leading out of it. Instead, the bottom is full of sinkholes, like a bathtub without a plug. The water drains into a secret underground cave system and travels 16 kilometers (10 miles) before popping back up at Maligne Canyon.

10. Sunwapta Falls

Sunwapta Falls is a dramatic pair of waterfalls fed by the Athabasca Glacier. The name Sunwapta comes from the Stoney Indigenous language and means turbulent water, a perfect name for the roaring river that crashes through this deep limestone canyon.

Sunwapta Falls is fully open and was not affected by the 2024 wildfires. It remains one of the most popular and scenic stops along the Icefields Parkway, located about 55 km south of the Jasper townsite.

The Upper Falls & Bridge: Just a short, 1-minute stroll from the parking lot, the Upper Falls is the main attraction. A footbridge takes you directly over the 18-meter (60-foot) drop. It’s famous for the tiny, forested island that splits the river just before it plunges into the canyon.

11. Pyramid Island

Pyramid Island is a tiny, peaceful gem located on Pyramid Lake, sitting right at the base of the massive, reddish-colored Pyramid Mountain. It is one of the most photographed spots in Jasper because of its iconic wooden bridge and the perfect mountain reflections in the water.

The Pyramid Lake area is fully open and was completely untouched by the 2024 wildfires. It remains a favorite quick escape because it is only a 10-minute drive from the Jasper townsite.

Cross the Wooden Bridge: The highlight of any visit is walking across the rustic wooden bridge that connects the shore to the island. It’s the perfect place for a family photo with the mountain in the background.

12. Patricia Lake

Patricia Lake is a calm, crystal-clear lake located just a few minutes from the town of Jasper. It is often quieter than its neighbor, Pyramid Lake, making it the perfect spot for travelers who want to escape the crowds. While it looks like a typical mountain paradise, it holds a top-secret history beneath its surface.

Patricia Lake is fully open. While some wildfire risk reduction (thinning out trees) is happening in the surrounding forest to keep the town safe, the lake itself remains a peaceful haven for visitors.

Find the Iceberg Ship Secret: During World War II, this lake was the site of a top-secret mission called Project Habbakuk. Scientists tried to build a giant aircraft carrier out of Pykrete (a mix of ice and wood pulp). Today, the remains of the prototype are at the bottom of the lake. You can read all about it on the commemorative plaque along the shore.

13. Miette Hot Springs

Miette Hot Springs is home to the hottest mineral water in the Canadian Rockies. While the water flows out of the mountain at a scorching 54°C (129°F), it is cooled down to a perfect 40°C (104°F) before it enters the pools. It is located at the end of a beautiful, winding 17-kilometer road in the Fiddle Valley.

The hot springs are closed during winter. In 2026 season, it is scheduled to reopen on May 15. Also, please note that a small section of Miette Road is still being repaired from a previous washout, so you may encounter a short delay with a one-lane traffic light on your way up.

Soak in the Pools: There are four outdoor pools to choose from: two hot pools for relaxing and two cool plunge pools for waking up your senses. Alternating between hot and cold is a local secret for feeling totally refreshed.

14. Jasper Planetarium & Dark Sky Preserve

Jasper National Park is the second-largest Dark Sky Preserve in the world. This means the town and park have special rules to keep artificial lights low, making the stars look brighter than almost anywhere else on Earth. Even right from the middle of town, you can see thousands of stars and the glowing stripe of the Milky Way.

The Jasper Planetarium is fully open and operating from its base at the Fairmont Jasper Park Lodge. March is actually one of the best times of year to visit, as the long, dark nights and clear mountain air make for perfect stargazing and frequent sightings of the Northern Lights (Aurora Borealis).

Planetarium Dome Experience: Step inside a 34-seat domed theatre for a live tour led by astronomy experts. You’ll see local Indigenous constellations, fly through the solar system, and see 4K instant replays of the Northern Lights caught in Jasper just days before.

15. Lac Beauvert

Lac Beauvert (French for Beautiful Green Lake) is famous for its surreal, neon-green water and incredible clarity. On a calm day, you can see all the way to the bottom, where ancient logs and white rocks make the lake look like a giant aquarium. It is located right next to the historic Fairmont Jasper Park Lodge.

Walk the Lake Loop: This is an easy, flat 3.5-kilometer (2.2-mile) trail that circles the entire lake. It takes about an hour to walk and offers constant views of Pyramid Mountain and Mount Edith Cavell.

Paddle in Paradise: Once the ice melts in May, the Boathouse opens for rentals. Renting a bright green canoe or a stand-up paddleboard is the best way to see the underwater forest of logs at the bottom of the lake.

16. Marmot Basin

Marmot Basin is the only ski resort in Jasper National Park. It is famous for having the highest base elevation of any ski area in Canada, which means the snow stays soft and powdery for a very long time. It is a hidden gem because it is rarely crowded, so you don't have to wait in long lines to get on the chairlifts.

Ski or Snowboard: With 91 runs, there is something for everyone. Beginners can stay on the lower easy slopes like School House, while experts can take the knob chair to the very top for steep, powdery bowls.

Jasper View: Even if you aren't a pro skier, take the Canadian Rockies Express (one of the fastest lifts in the world). From the top, you get a breathtaking view of the town of Jasper and the Athabasca Valley far below.

17. Valley of the Five Lakes

The Valley of the Five Lakes is one of the most beloved hiking spots in Jasper. It is famous because each of the five lakes has a slightly different shade of blue or green, ranging from clear jade to deep sapphire. The colors come from unique glacial minerals and different depths in each lake.

As of March 2026, this trail system is partially open. Parts of the valley were affected by the 2024 wildfires, and Parks Canada has been busy clearing hazard trees (burnt trees that might fall). The main loop to Lakes 1 through 4 is open, but the longer trail to the Fifth Lake is still being repaired.

The Red Chair: At the shore of the Third Lake, you will find the famous Parks Canada Red Chairs. Sitting here is the best way to soak in the view of the bright green water and the surrounding peaks.

18. Old Fort Point Loop

Old Fort Point is widely considered the best bang for your buck hike in Jasper. It is a prominent bedrock hill that stands 130 meters (426 feet) above the Athabasca River, offering a 360-degree view of the townsite, the winding river, and the surrounding mountain ranges.

As of March 2026, the trail is partially open. While it is a local favorite, you will notice a significant change: the 2024 wildfires moved through this area, leaving many of the trees as blackened skeletons. However, the lack of needles and leaves actually makes the panoramic views from the top even more wide-open and dramatic than before.

The Steep Stairs Challenge: If you go counter-clockwise, you start with a long flight of wooden stairs. It’s a workout for your lungs, but it gets you to the first great viewpoint in just a few minutes.

19. Jasper Lake

Jasper Lake is a unique geographical feature located about 20 minutes east of the Jasper townsite. It isn't a typical deep lake; instead, it is a massive, shallow widening of the Athabasca River. It is famous for its wind-swept sand dunes and a phenomenon that makes it look like you are walking on water.

Jasper Lake is a beautiful, windswept landscape. Because it is so shallow, large sections of the lakebed in winter are exposed or covered in a thin layer of ice and snow. This is a great time to see the bones of the lake before the spring melt fills it up in May.

Walk on Water: In the summer, you can wade out hundreds of meters into the lake and the water will only reach your ankles. In winter, the water is mostly frozen or receded, allowing you to walk far out onto the sandy, silty flats to get incredible photos of the surrounding peaks like Roche Miette.

20. Lake Annette & Lake Edith

Lake Annette and Lake Edith are the twin lakes of Jasper. Sitting side-by-side on a large glacial terrace just across the river from town, they are the go-to spots for locals to hang out. While most alpine lakes are freezing cold, these two are relatively shallow, meaning they warm up (to about 18°C or 65°F) enough for a brave summer swim.

The Lake Annette Loop: This is a 2.4-kilometer (1.5-mile) trail that is completely paved and flat. It is one of the most accessible trails in the park, perfect for wheelchairs, strollers, or an easy jog.

Ochre-Colored Sand: Lake Annette is famous for its wood-chip colored sandy beaches. In the summer, this is Jasper’s Tropical Beach. In March, you can walk along the shoreline and see the crystal-clear water lapping against the snowy sand.

21. Bald Hills Trail

The Bald Hills Trail is widely considered the crown jewel of day hikes in the Maligne Lake area. It follows an old fire lookout road that climbs steeply through the forest before breaking out into wide-open, treeless alpine meadows (the Bald Hills). From the top, you get what many call the best view in the entire park: a literal bird's-eye look at the entire 22-kilometer length of Maligne Lake.

The Panoramic Reward: Once you reach the ridge (about 2,300 meters high), you are treated to a 360-degree view. To the south, Maligne Lake stretches out like a turquoise ribbon surrounded by jagged, snow-capped peaks like Mount Charlton and Mount Unwin.

The Old Lookout Site: At the top of the first major climb, you’ll find the site where a fire lookout tower once stood. It’s a perfect spot for a high-altitude lunch while looking down at the tiny tour boats on the lake below.

22. Horseshoe Lake

Horseshoe Lake is a hidden gem located along the Icefields Parkway, about 25 km south of the Jasper townsite. Named for its distinct U shape, it is famous for its deep, crystal-clear water and the dramatic limestone cliffs that surround it.

Horseshoe Lake is open to the public. While the 2024 wildfires affected the forest immediately surrounding the lake, the water itself remains as stunningly clear as ever.

Cliff Jumping (Summer Only): This is the most famous activity at the lake. There are ledges ranging from 1 meter to over 20 meters (80 feet) high. Warning: This is extremely dangerous and Parks Canada does not recommend it due to the high number of injuries and rescues each year.

23. Sulphur Skyline Trail

The Sulphur Skyline is often called the ultimate Jasper hike because it offers a 360-degree mountain panorama that feels like it belongs in a movie. Starting from the Miette Hot Springs parking lot, the trail climbs steadily through the forest before breaking out onto a dramatic, rocky ridge that looks deep into the Fiddle Valley and the wild peaks of the Ashlar Ridge.

In winter, the Miette Road is seasonally closed. Even once the road opens, the upper sections of this trail usually remain covered in deep snow and ice until late June.

The Top of the World View: From the summit (2,050 meters / 6,725 feet), you can see the Fiddle River winding like a silver thread far below. On a clear day, the layers of mountain ranges look like blue waves stretching toward the horizon.

24. Overlander Trail

The Overlander Trail is a historic trek that follows the footsteps of the Overlanders, gold seekers who traveled across the Canadian Rockies in 1862. It is a unique, long-distance trail that stays relatively low in the Athabasca Valley, offering a mix of open meadows, pine forests, and dramatic riverside views.

The Moberly Homestead: About 5 km (3 miles) from the 6th Bridge trailhead, you will find the ruins of the Moberly family home. Built in the late 1800s, these log cabin remains offer a hauntingly beautiful look at early pioneer life in the Rockies.

Cinquefoil Mountain Views: As you hike through the open meadows, the massive, slanted rock face of Cinquefoil Mountain looms over you. In March, the contrast between the dark rock and the white snow in the crevices is a photographer's dream.

25. Wilcox Pass

Wilcox Pass is the ultimate alpine shortcut. Located right on the boundary between Jasper and Banff National Parks, this trail was used by early travelers to bypass the difficult, icy terrain of the Athabasca Glacier. Today, it is famous for providing the most spectacular front-row seat to the Columbia Icefield without actually having to step on the ice.

The Red Chair Viewpoint: About 1.5 km (0.9 miles) into the hike, you’ll find two Parks Canada Red Chairs. This is the most famous photo spot on the Icefields Parkway. Sitting here, you are looking directly across the valley at the massive Athabasca Glacier and the towering peaks of Mount Athabasca and Snow Dome.

Bighorn Sheep Haven: The wide-open alpine meadows of the pass are a favorite summer grazing spot for large rams. Because they are protected here, they are often quite calm, allowing for incredible wildlife photography (from a safe distance).
