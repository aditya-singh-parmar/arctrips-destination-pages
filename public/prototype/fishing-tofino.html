April to early June

Salmon fishing can already be rewarding and boats are easier to get on. Halibut is the thing you are waiting for, and it does not normally pick up until after June 1.

June

Halibut gets better as the month rolls on, often just a few miles offshore. Chinook is on throughout. From June 15 the season proper has started.

July to mid August

The busiest stretch. By July, halibut catches often involve limits. Coho are more prevalent after July 1, and from July 15 you can fish for Chinook anywhere rather than inside a mile of shore.

Mid August to mid September

The fall run of Chinook returning to Barkley Sound comes past Tofino. Halibut moves further out, sometimes more than ten miles offshore. Coho holds up to mid September.

What you can catch

Six fish, ordered by how much of the season each one carries

A Tofino skipper plans the day around the first two. The rest come over the side while you are out there. This is the order you are most likely to be fishing for, not the order of size.

What the day is planned around

01 to 02

Every Tofino charter is built around one of these two, or both. Everything else is what else was down there.

May to SeptemberThe salmon most commonly caught here

Chinook salmon (King)

Chinook, called King on the American side, is the salmon most commonly caught out of Tofino. Until July 15 you fish for them within a mile of shore. After July 15 you can fish anywhere.

Many of the Chinook from the fall run returning to Barkley Sound, beside Ucluelet, come past Tofino from mid August. That run is why the last month of the season is worth planning around.

On through the summer, with a second push from mid August to mid September.

Good to know. Where they sit moves from year to year. Colder water close to shore through 2022 and 2023 kept Chinook inshore. In 2024 more of them were offshore.

June 1 onwardBetter every week through June

Halibut

Halibut fishing is better after June 1 and picks up as the month rolls on. By July, catches often involve limits. In June and July the ground is often only a few miles offshore. Later in the summer it can be more than ten miles out.

Some summers Tofino pulls good numbers all season. Other summers the offshore ground out of Ucluelet is significantly better. If halibut is the whole reason for the trip, Tofino has been quite good recently.

Best from mid June to September, and best again on the calm water days.

Good to know. If you are American and you want to keep halibut, your licence has to be bought in person rather than online. In Tofino that is Method Marine.

In season alongside them

03 to 04

Both are real targets in the second half of the summer. Neither is usually the reason the trip was booked.

July 1 to mid SeptemberFin clipped only, outside the surfline

Coho salmon (Silver)

Coho, Silvers to Americans, are more prevalent after July 1 and fish well through to mid September. Outside the surfline you may only keep fin clipped Coho. Inshore of the surfline that restriction does not apply.

Clayoquot Sound has Coho at sporadic times through the summer. Barkley Sound, south of Ucluelet, is the more consistent Coho water, and much of that fishing happens in protected water.

Best from July 1 to mid September, often close in.

Good to know. The combined daily limit across all salmon is 4, so Coho and Chinook come out of the same allowance.

Bottom fishingThe white meat in the Tacofino tacos

Ling cod

Ling cod is not what most people book a charter for, and it is some of the tastiest white meat you will eat on this coast. It is the fish in the Tacofino tacos.

It comes off the bottom, which in practice means it comes aboard on the same drops as the halibut rather than on a trip of its own.

Alongside halibut, so from June onward.

Good to know. Daily limit 3, possession limit 6, on the west coast of Vancouver Island.

Rockfish

There are rockfish in the same water as the ling cod. Guides are not generally keen on cleaning them: they are old fish and there is less meat on them for the work involved.

Present through the season, targeted by almost nobody.

Good to know. Rockfish are not on the salmon and halibut limits below and are managed on their own terms. Ask the boat what is open before you keep one.

06

What it isA bonus fish, not a reason to book

Occasional

Pink and sockeye salmon

You can catch Pink salmon here. Guides are not generally happy about it, because the Chinook and Coho fishing is often very good and a Pink is the lesser of the three.

Sockeye can be caught inside Barkley Sound and up the Port Alberni inlet, which is a different day out from a Tofino charter, not a variation on one.

Turns up in summer, and is nobody's booking reason.

Good to know. Pink and chum each carry a daily limit of 4, and they still come out of the combined salmon limit of 4 a day.

A day on the water

Ten minutes to the dock, four to six hours out

Tofino's harbour is a working one. Float planes, whale watching boats, water taxis and private boats all use it, against a backdrop most people photograph before they have left the dock.

A heated cabin, a walk around deck and twin outboards. This is the shape most boats on this coast take, and the cabin is the part worth asking about.

Before you leave

The dock is ten minutes from town

Trips of four to six hours are the popular length here, because most visitors have something else planned for the same day. Eight hour mornings are the format when salmon and halibut are both on the list. Afternoon trips of four to six hours are usually salmon only.

The boat

Twenty four to thirty two feet, and the cabin decides the day

Charter boats out of Tofino average 24 to 32 feet. Most days are not the sunny flat calm of the photographs, so look for a heated cabin big enough for your whole group. Most boats have walk around cabins, which means less shelter on deck than people expect.

On the water

Ten to fifteen degrees, inshore or offshore

A normal temperature on the water is 10 to 15 C (50 to 60 F). Whether the boat runs inshore or offshore is decided on the year and on the morning, not booked in advance: 2022 and 2023 held the Chinook close in, 2024 put more of them out.

Licence and limits

One licence to sort out, and a table the boat confirms on the day

Charters in both towns provide all the gear and tackle. The licence is the one thing that is yours to arrange, and it is both cheap and quick.

Canadians buy a tidal waters licence online. Americans can buy online as well, with one exception: to keep halibut, the licence has to be bought in person. In Tofino that is Method Marine.

Before you go

Four things that decide how the day goes

The boat brings the gear and the knowledge. These four are the parts nobody can do for you.

The two things you have to bring

Your fishing licence and, if you have never been offshore, motion sickness medication or a patch. Both are worth sorting before the morning of the trip rather than on the way to the dock.

Dress for ten degrees and rain

A normal day on the water is 10 to 15 C. Walk around cabins mean less protection on deck than most people expect, so bring layers and a real rain shell. Ask whether the boat carries rain gear, because not all of them do.

Ask what is aboard before you book

Whether the cabin is heated and big enough for your group is the question that decides how the last two hours feel. Ask about the toilet at the same time: whether there is one, and how much room there is around it.

Do not plan around last year's fish

Where the fish sit moves. Colder water close to shore through 2022 and 2023 held Chinook inshore, and 2024 put more of them offshore. The skipper makes that call on the morning, so go with a target rather than a spot.

Questions

Asked before people book

Open the ones you need. The first one is the one this page exists to answer plainly.

Can you book a fishing charter on Arc Trips?

No. Charters out of Tofino are booked through ArcTrips Fishing, the sister brand, which lists guides for salmon and halibut in both Tofino and Ucluelet. Arc Trips books the stay. If you want both, book the stay here and the boat there.

What can you catch out of Tofino?

Chinook salmon and halibut are what trips are built around. Coho comes on after July 1. Ling cod and rockfish come off the bottom on the same drops as the halibut, and Pink salmon turns up. Sockeye is caught inside Barkley Sound and up the Port Alberni inlet rather than off Tofino.

When is the best time to fish in Tofino?

June 15 to September 15. April, May and early June can still bring rewarding salmon fishing. Halibut normally picks up after June 1, and by July catches often involve limits.

Do you need a fishing licence?

Yes, a tidal waters licence, and it is cheap and quick to get. Canadians buy online. Americans can buy online too, unless they want to keep halibut, in which case the licence has to be bought in person. In Tofino that is Method Marine.

How long is a trip?

Four to six hours is the popular length in Tofino, because most visitors have other plans the same day. Eight hour mornings are the format when salmon and halibut are both on the list, and afternoon trips of four to six hours are usually salmon only.

Should you fish out of Tofino or Ucluelet?

Mostly it comes down to how far you want to drive. The dock in Tofino is about ten minutes from town. Ucluelet is 35 minutes down the road and sits beside the largest salmon and halibut feeding grounds on this coast. Tofino runs slightly more expensive, because the cost of doing business here is higher, and many of the Chinook that pass Ucluelet come past Tofino as well.

Is the gear provided?

Yes. Charters in both towns provide all the gear and tackle. Rain gear is the thing to ask about separately, because it is not always aboard.

Can a solo angler get on a boat?

In Tofino, more easily than in most places. If you are on your own and looking to be matched up with other anglers, Tofino charters are the better bet.
