Beaches in Tofino

Wide, cold and open to the Pacific. Thirteen of them, from a five minute walk out of the village to the far end of the national park.

Where to go

Thirteen beaches, in the order you meet them

The seven in and around the village first, then the six down the park highway. The further you drive, the fewer people you share the sand with.

All nine subjects in Tofino

In and around the village

01 to 07

Close enough to reach on foot, by bike, or in a few minutes of driving.

TofinoWalks, surfing, sunsets, families

Chesterman Beach

Chesterman Beach is one of the most loved beaches in Tofino. It is long, sandy, and easy to enjoy. It is close to town and popular with walkers, surfers, families, and photographers.

Chesterman is split into North Chesterman and South Chesterman. At low tide, you can walk across the sandspit, also called a tombolo, toward Frank Island. This is one of the most scenic beach walks in Tofino.

Good for long beach walks, beginner surf lessons, sunset photos, and 3 more.

Good to know. Always check the tide before walking toward Frank Island. The water can come in and cover the sandspit.

Tofino areaSurfing, sunsets, storm watching

Cox Bay

Cox Bay is one of the best beaches in Tofino for surfing. It is wide, open, and faces the Pacific Ocean. Because of this, it often gets strong and steady waves.

Many surfers love Cox Bay. It is also a beautiful place to watch the waves from shore. On clear evenings, it can be a great sunset beach.

Cox Bay is also known for the short Cox Bay lookout hike. The trail can be muddy and steep, but the view over the beach is one of the most popular photo spots near Tofino.

Good for surfing, watching surfers, sunset views, and 3 more.

Good to know. Cox Bay can have powerful waves and currents. Beginners should go with a surf school or choose calmer conditions.

Pacific Rim National ParkBig beach views, walks, surfing

Long Beach

Long Beach is one of the most famous beaches on Vancouver Island. It is part of Pacific Rim National Park Reserve, between Tofino and Ucluelet.

As the name says, Long Beach is very long. It gives you that classic west coast feeling: open sand, big sky, rolling waves, and rainforest behind you.

Long Beach is a great place for walking, surfing, watching waves, and taking photos. It also has nearby trails and visitor areas in the national park.

Good for long walks, big beach views, surfing, and 3 more.

Good to know. You may need a national park pass when stopping in this area. Follow Parks Canada signs, closures, and beach safety rules.

Tofino areaFamilies, calmer beach days

Tinwis Beach, formerly Mackenzie Beach

Tinwis Beach, formerly known as Mackenzie Beach, is a calmer and more sheltered beach near Tofino. It does not face the open ocean as directly as Cox Bay or Long Beach, so the water is often gentler.

This makes tinwis a favorite for families, relaxed beach time, and people who want a softer beach experience. It is also a good place to enjoy tide pools and coastal views when conditions are safe.

Good for families, calmer beach days, paddleboarding in gentle conditions, and 3 more.

Good to know. The beach is now officially called tinwis, pronounced “ti-nu-wis.” Some maps and visitors may still use the older name, Mackenzie Beach.

Near the villageShort walks, quiet sunsets

Tonquin Beach

Tonquin Beach is a smaller beach close to the village of Tofino. You reach it by walking a short forest trail. It feels quieter and more hidden than the big surf beaches.

Tonquin Beach is not as wide as Chesterman or Long Beach, but it has a peaceful feel. It is a nice place for a short walk, a picnic, or a quiet sunset.

Good for short walks, quiet beach time, picnics, and 2 more.

Good to know. The trail can be wet or muddy after rain. Wear shoes with good grip.

Tofino areaQuiet views, short walks

Middle Beach

Middle Beach is a smaller beach area near Chesterman Beach. It has a more tucked-away feel, with rocky edges, forest, and ocean views.

It is a nice place if you want something quieter than the larger beaches. It is not always the best place for swimming or surfing, but it can be lovely for photos and peaceful walks.

Good for quiet views, photography, short walks, and 1 more.

Good to know. Check tide and wave conditions before walking near rocks.

Near Cox BayLow tide exploring, photos

Rosie Bay

At low tide, you may find interesting rocks, sand, and small pools. It is also a scenic area for photos.

Good for quiet exploring, low tide walks, photography, and 1 more.

Good to know. Be careful near rocks and changing tides.

Down the park highway

08 to 13

Not village beaches. They are wilder, quieter, and you may need a Parks Canada pass to stop.

Pacific Rim National ParkStorm watching, views, visitor centre

Wickaninnish Beach

Wickaninnish Beach is in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve, closer to the Ucluelet side of the Long Beach area. It is one of the best places near Tofino for big ocean views and storm watching.

The nearby Kwisitis Visitor Centre makes this beach extra useful for visitors. You can learn about the coast, local wildlife, and the history of the area. There is also a viewing area, which is helpful when the beach feels too rough or windy.

Good for storm watching, big ocean views, photography, and 3 more.

Good to know. This beach can feel very open and exposed. Stay far back from the water during storms and high tide.

Pacific Rim National ParkQuiet walks, sand cliffs

Florencia Bay

Florencia Bay is also in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. It is closer to Ucluelet than Tofino, but it is still a great stop on a Tofino beach trip.

Florencia Bay is known for its sand cliffs, quiet feel, and scenic shoreline. To reach the beach, you walk a trail and take a wooden staircase down to the shore.

It feels less busy than Chesterman, Cox Bay, and Long Beach. This makes it a good choice for people who want a slower, quieter beach visit.

Good for quiet beach walks, sand cliff views, photography, and 2 more.

Good to know. The stairs and trail can feel longer on the way back up. Wear comfortable shoes and check the tide before walking far.

Pacific Rim National ParkWild beach walks

Combers Beach

Combers Beach is part of Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. It is not a town beach, but it is one of the wilder beaches near Tofino.

Combers Beach is good for visitors who want a natural, open beach experience. It can feel quieter than Chesterman or Cox Bay. It is a good place for a long walk, but you should check trail access, tide times, and park updates before going.

Good for long beach walks, quiet nature time, photography, and 2 more.

Good to know. Dog rules and access rules can change in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve. Check current Parks Canada updates before visiting with a dog.

Pacific Rim National ParkForest to beach scenery

Schooner Cove

Schooner Cove is also in Pacific Rim National Park Reserve, closer to the Tofino end of the Long Beach area.

It is worth visiting if you like forest-to-beach scenery and quieter places. It has a more tucked-away feeling than the main beaches near town. It is best for people who want a slower nature stop, not a busy beach day.

Good for forest and beach views, quiet exploring, nature photos, and 2 more.

Good to know. Trail and beach access can change in national park areas. Check current Parks Canada updates before planning your visit.

Sheltered inlet sideKayaking, birding, calm water

Grice Bay

Grice Bay is different from the other beaches here. It is not a big open Pacific Ocean surf beach. It is a sheltered bay on the calmer inlet side near Tofino and Pacific Rim National Park Reserve.

This means Grice Bay is not the place to go for surfing, big waves, or classic sandy beach photos. But it is still worth visiting if you like calm water, kayaking, bird watching, and peaceful views.

Good for kayaking, paddle trips, bird watching, and 2 more.

Good to know. Choose Grice Bay for calm inlet scenery, not for surfing or storm watching.

Freshwater lake near both townsCanoeing, paddleboarding, calm views

Kennedy Lake

Kennedy Lake is not a beach in Tofino town, and it is not an open Pacific Ocean beach. It is a large freshwater lake near Tofino and Ucluelet, close to Pacific Rim National Park Reserve.

It is worth the drive because it gives you a different kind of water day. Instead of big waves, surf, and storm watching, Kennedy Lake is known for calmer water, forest views, and peaceful lake scenery.

Good for freshwater lake views, kayaking or canoeing, paddleboarding in calm conditions, and 5 more.

Good to know. Kennedy Lake is best for calm-water activities, not surfing or storm watching. Conditions can still change, so check the weather and wind before paddling.

Before you go

Four things the sand does not tell you

Four things that apply to every beach on this page, so you only have to read them once.

Nobody is watching the water

Tofino beaches are unsupervised. There are no lifeguards and no surf guards on duty. The Pacific here is cold all year and strong, so most people who go in wear a wetsuit, and beginners book a lesson.

Check the tide before you walk far

The sandspit to Frank Island at Chesterman covers at high water. Long walks at Long Beach and Combers can leave you a long way from your exit. Look at the tide first, then decide how far to go.

Bring rain gear even when it looks fine

Dress in layers and carry a shell. Weather here turns inside an hour, and the wind on an open beach makes an eight degree day feel like four.

Park early, and use the marked access

Parking fills by mid morning through the summer. Use marked beach access points, stay off slippery rocks, give wildlife room, and take everything home with you.

Questions

Asked before people book

Open the ones you need. Everything here is answered in the same words the guide uses.

What is the nicest beach in Tofino?

Many people think Chesterman Beach is the nicest beach in Tofino because it is long, sandy, scenic, and easy to enjoy. Cox Bay and Long Beach are also favorites.

What is the best beach in Tofino for surfing?

Cox Bay is one of the best beaches for surfing. Chesterman Beach and Long Beach are also popular surf spots, depending on conditions.

What is the best beach in Tofino for families?

Tinwis Beach and Chesterman Beach are good choices for families. tinwis is more sheltered, while Chesterman has lots of space for walking and playing.

Can you swim in Tofino?

Yes, but the water is cold and the ocean can be strong. Most people wear wetsuits. There are no lifeguards, so be careful.

Are Tofino beaches sandy?

Yes. Many Tofino beaches have wide sandy areas, especially Chesterman Beach, Cox Bay, Long Beach, and tinwis Beach.

Do you need a park pass for Tofino beaches?

You may need a Parks Canada pass when visiting beaches inside Pacific Rim National Park Reserve, including Long Beach. Beaches closer to town may have local parking rules instead.

Which beach is closest to Tofino town?

Tonquin Beach is one of the closest beaches to the village of Tofino. It is reached by a short trail.

Which Tofino beach is best for sunset?

Cox Bay and Chesterman Beach are two of the best sunset beaches in Tofino. Tonquin Beach can also be lovely at sunset.

Are there lifeguards on Tofino beaches?

No. Tofino beaches are unsupervised. There are no lifeguards or surf guards on duty.

Can you have fires on Tofino beaches?

Beach fire rules can change. Always check current local rules before lighting any fire. In many cases, fires may be restricted or not allowed.

Are dogs allowed on Tofino beaches?

Dogs are allowed in some areas, but rules can change by beach, season, wildlife activity, and park rules. In Pacific Rim National Park Reserve, dogs must be leashed where they are allowed, and some areas may be closed to dogs to protect wildlife. Check current rules for the beach you plan to visit.

Are Tofino beaches good in winter?

Yes. Winter is great for storm watching, photography, and quiet walks on calmer days. Stay far back from the ocean during storms and high tides.
