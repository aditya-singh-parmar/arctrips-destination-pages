Long Beach

One of the most famous beaches on Vancouver Island, on the park road between Tofino and Ucluelet.

As the name says, it is very long. Open sand, big sky, rolling waves, and rainforest standing behind you.

It turns up in four of our Tofino guides: beaches, hiking, storm watching and kayaking.

What Long Beach actually is

Long Beach is part of Pacific Rim National Park Reserve, the stretch of protected coast between Tofino and Ucluelet. It is the beach most people picture when they picture this coast, and it is the reason the road runs out here at all.

It gives you the classic west coast feeling in one look: open sand, big sky, rolling waves, and rainforest behind you. No promenade, no line of hotels, no boardwalk. The car park, then the sand, then the Pacific.

It is a good place for walking, surfing, watching waves, and taking photographs, and there are trails and visitor areas in the park within a few minutes of it.

In winter it becomes the storm beach

Long Beach is wide, long, and open to the ocean with nothing in front of it. That is what makes it a powerful place to watch winter waves. You can walk on the sand when conditions are safe, or watch from a safer distance back, which on the worst days is the better call and still the better view.

Walking it

A Long Beach hike is not a formal trail. It is simple: walk along the sand, watch the waves, enjoy the wide open views, and turn back when you are ready. It is the easiest big walk near Tofino, and the one that suits people who want scenery without a trailhead, a map, or boots.

Not a kayaking beach

Long Beach faces the open Pacific, so surf, swell, rip currents and wind make it a hard place to paddle. Expert sea kayakers and surf kayakers use it in the right conditions. Everyone else should enjoy it from shore and paddle the sheltered water instead: Lemmens Inlet, Meares Island, Grice Bay, Tinwis Beach or Kennedy Lake.

When should you go?

Summer for the long dry days, winter for the storms. The sand itself is open, free and walkable every day of the year. Pick a month to see what it is like.

Getting here

Long Beach is the middle of a run of park beaches. This is the order they come in, driving from the Tofino end to the Ucluelet end.

  1. Schooner CoveForest first, then sand. The quietest stop of the five, closest to the Tofino end.Tofino end
  2. Long BeachThe main strand, and this page. Surf, walking, and the winter waves.You are here
  3. Combers BeachNot a town beach. Wilder, emptier, and made for a long walk.
  4. Wickaninnish BeachBig ocean views, storm watching, and the Kwisitis Visitor Centre.
  5. Florencia BaySand cliffs and low tide exploring, down a set of stairs that feel longer coming back.Ucluelet end

The Long Beach Unit holds twenty places in all: five beaches on this road, ten trails and paths off it, and three pieces of water.

The road
Highway 4 runs behind the whole beach, with Parks Canada car parks off it at intervals. There is no single entrance to aim for.
From Tofino
You arrive at the north end, about twenty minutes out of town.
From Ucluelet
You arrive at the south end, about twenty minutes out of town, past Florencia Bay and Wickaninnish.
Without a car
The ʔapsčiik t̓ašii pathway, said ups-cheek ta-shee, is paved and runs the length of the unit between the two towns. It has hills, so plan the way back.

Before you go

Five things the guides say every time. Read these and you are set.

Check the tide
The beach is much wider at low water, and the walk back is longer than it looked on the way out. Today's readings are at the top of this page.
Watch the waves
Stay well back from big waves and never turn your back on the ocean. This is open coast, not a sheltered bay.
Swimming
Yes, but the water is cold and the current can be strong. Most people wear a wetsuit. There are no lifeguards.
Leave the kayak
Paddle the sheltered water instead: Lemmens Inlet, Grice Bay or Kennedy Lake.
Park pass and rules
You may need a national park pass to stop here, and dog and access rules change through the year. Follow the Parks Canada signs.

Where to stay

Nothing on Long Beach itself is bookable. It is a national park beach, so the rooms sit in the two towns at either end, both about twenty minutes away.
