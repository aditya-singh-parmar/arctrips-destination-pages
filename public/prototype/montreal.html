World-Known Landmarks

World-Known Landmarks

10 places, written up one at a time

1. Notre-Dame Basilica

Blue Vaults: Look up to see the breathtaking ceiling, a deep azure sky decorated with thousands of 24-karat gold stars.

AURA Experience: This world-renowned light and sound show by Moment Factory uses the Basilica’s architecture as a canvas. It starts with an exploration of illuminated art stations and culminates in a 25-minute orchestral light performance.

2. Old Montreal (Vieux-Montreal)

Old Montreal (Vieux-Montreal) is the heart of Montreal’s rich history, showcasing stunning colonial architecture and cobblestone streets. The district transports visitors back in time with its preserved 17th and 18th-century buildings, including charming boutiques and cafes. Old Montreal is best experienced on foot, where every corner reveals a piece of the city's 375-plus-year history.

Place Jacques-Cartier: The district's main social hub. This sloping pedestrian square is lined with flower stalls, street performers, and sun-drenched terrasses. In the winter, it often hosts holiday markets and ice sculptures.

Old Port (Vieux-Port): A massive recreational space along the St. Lawrence River. You can ride the Grande Roue de Montréal (Canada's tallest Ferris wheel), rent pedal boats, or visit the Montreal Science Centre.

3. Mount Royal Park (Parc du Mont-Royal)

Mount Royal Park (Parc du Mont-Royal) is one of Montreal's most iconic natural landmarks, offering stunning views of the city skyline. This large urban park, designed by landscape architect Frederick Law Olmsted (landscape architect behind New York's Central Park), covers 200 hectares of green space, with plenty of paths, forests, and lakes. This location's significance stems from its central role in Montreal’s landscape and as a recreational haven for both locals and visitors.

Kondiaronk Belvedere: This semicircular lookout in front of the Mount Royal Chalet offers the most famous panoramic view of Montreal's downtown skyline and the St. Lawrence River.

Beaver Lake (Lac aux Castors): A picturesque man-made lake that serves as a focal point for the park. In the summer, you can enjoy boat rentals and picnics on the surrounding grass; in the winter, it transforms into a popular skating rink.

4. Montreal Botanical Garden

The Montreal Botanical Garden is recognized as one of the world’s greatest botanical institutions. Spanning 75 hectares, it offers a global tour of flora through its intricate thematic gardens and massive greenhouse complex. It is a place where art, culture, and nature intersect, providing a tranquil escape right next to the Olympic Stadium.

Thematic Cultural Gardens: Explore the Chinese Garden, which is the largest of its kind outside Asia, the serene Japanese Garden with its Zen aesthetic and tea room, and the First Nations Garden highlighting the relationship between Indigenous peoples and the plant world.

Greenhouse Complex: Wander through 10 exhibition greenhouses that house everything from arid desert cacti to lush tropical rainforest plants and a world-class bonsai collection.

5. Saint Joseph’s Oratory of Mount Royal

Standing majestically on the northern slope of Mount Royal, this National Historic Site is Canada's largest church and a world-renowned pilgrimage site. Its massive copper dome reaches a height of 97 meters, making it the highest point in Montreal and a permanent fixture of the city's skyline. This impressive basilica is dedicated to Saint Joseph, the patron saint of Canada.

Dome and Basilica: Enter the Great Basilica to appreciate its sheer scale and the modern, minimalist interior. The outer dome is the third largest of its kind in the world, surpassed only by St. Peter’s in Rome and Yamoussoukro in Ivory Coast.

Crypt Church: A more intimate, 1,000-seat church located at the base of the Oratory. It features a beautiful mosaic and is where most daily masses are held.

6. Biodôme de Montreal

The Montreal Biodôme is a world-class house of life where you can walk through four distinct ecosystems of the Americas under one roof. Located within the Space for Life complex in a building originally designed as a velodrome for the 1976 Olympics, it offers an immersive experience that blurs the lines between a zoo, an aquarium, and a botanical garden.

Tropical Rainforest: This is the largest and most popular ecosystem, featuring lush vegetation, a humid climate, and free-roaming wildlife like parrots, sloths, and golden lion tamarins.

Laurentian Maple Forest: This section replicates the Quebec wilderness. Depending on when you visit, you will see the foliage change with the seasons, and you might spot North American beavers, river otters, and lynx.

7. Montreal Museum of Fine Arts

The Montreal Museum of Fine Arts (Musée des beaux-arts de Montréal) is the city's largest museum and one of the most prominent artistic institutions in North America. Spread across five interconnected pavilions on the historic Golden Square Mile, the museum is celebrated for its encyclopedic collection that spans from ancient archaeology to cutting-edge contemporary design.

Jean-Noël Desmarais Pavilion: The museum's main entrance, which houses modern and contemporary art galleries, as well as major temporary international exhibitions.

Michal and Renata Hornstein Pavilion: Known for its classical architecture, this section is dedicated to archaeology and ancient art, featuring impressive collections from the Mediterranean and beyond.

8. Old Port of Montreal

The Old Port of Montreal (Vieux-Port) is a sprawling historic riverfront complex that stretches over two kilometers along the St. Lawrence River. Formerly the commercial heart of the city, it has been reimagined as one of Canada's most popular recreational and cultural destinations, offering a mix of year-round adventures and maritime charm.

La Grande Roue de Montréal: Ride Canada's tallest Ferris wheel for spectacular panoramic views of the city and the river from climate-controlled gondolas.

Montreal Science Centre: A family-favorite destination featuring interactive exhibitions, an IMAX TELUS theatre, and hands-on workshops focused on technology and innovation.

9. Jean-Talon Market

Jean Talon Market is one of North America's largest and most famous open-air public markets, serving as a vibrant focal point for Montreal's Little Italy neighborhood since 1933. It offers an incredible sensory experience where the city's diverse culinary culture comes to life through local farmers, artisans, and specialty food producers.

Fresh Local Produce: The central aisles are filled with seasonal fruits and vegetables from Quebec farms, known for their high quality and vibrant displays.

Specialty Food Boutiques: Explore the permanent shops surrounding the main stalls, which include world-class cheesemongers (fromageries), butchers, spice shops, and bakeries.

10. Biosphere

The Biosphere, Environment Museum is a striking architectural icon located on Saint Helen's Island. Originally designed as the United States pavilion for Expo 67, this massive geodesic dome now serves as a museum dedicated to environmental awareness and sustainability.

Geodesic Dome: Observe the intricate steel structure designed by Buckminster Fuller, which stands as the largest of its kind in the world and provides an incredible backdrop for photography.

Environmental Exhibits: Explore interactive displays that focus on climate change, water conservation, and biodiversity, designed to educate visitors of all ages on ecological challenges.

Art and History Lovers

Art and History Lovers

10 places, written up one at a time

1. Pointe-à-Callière, Montreal Archaeology and History Complex

Built upon the actual birthplace of the city, the Montreal Museum of Archaeology and History (Pointe-à-Callière) offers a unique underground journey through centuries of North American history. It is the only major archaeology museum in Canada and is situated on a site that has seen over 1,000 years of human activity.

Archaeological Crypt: Walk through a unique underground circuit that takes you through the remains of the city's first cemetery, the first Catholic cemetery in Montreal, and the original stone foundations of the 17th-century settlement.

Collector’s Sewer: Experience a world-first by walking through a 110-meter section of North America's first stone-built vaulted collector sewer, dating back to 1832.

2. Château Ramezay Historic Site and Museum

The Château Ramezay is a remarkable portal into the daily life of Montreal's 18th-century elite. Built in 1705 as the residence for the Governor of Montreal, it was the first building in Quebec to be classified as a historic monument and famously served as the Canadian headquarters for the American Revolutionary Army in 1775.

Governor's Garden: Wander through this meticulously recreated 18th-century urban garden, divided into sections for kitchen produce, medicinal herbs, and ornamental flowers.

Nantes Panels: Marvel at the intricate mahogany carvings in the Salle de Nantes, which were originally designed for the French India Company in the 1700s.

3. McCord Stewart Museum

The McCord Stewart Museum is a social history museum dedicated to the preservation and appreciation of Montreal's history, its people, and its communities. Located directly across from McGill University, it houses one of the most significant historical collections in North America, offering a deeply human perspective on the city's evolution.

Indigenous Cultures Collection: This is one of the museum's core strengths, featuring over 16,000 artifacts that document the history and artistry of Indigenous peoples from across Canada, with a focus on the nations of the Northeast.

Dress, Fashion, and Textiles: Explore an extensive collection of over 20,000 garments and accessories that trace the history of fashion in Montreal and Canada from the 18th century to the present day.

4. Musée d'art contemporain de Montréal

The Musée d'art contemporain de Montréal (MAC) is Canada's premier institution dedicated exclusively to contemporary art. It is currently in a fascinating stage of its history; while its main building in the Quartier des Spectacles undergoes a massive architectural transformation (scheduled to finish around 2028), the museum has set up a sleek, temporary home in the heart of downtown.

Temporary Gallery: Visit the current museum space at Place Ville Marie. While smaller than the original building, it hosts cutting-edge temporary exhibitions that often challenge traditional perceptions of art.

Permanent Collection Highlights: The museum owns over 8,000 works. Since space is limited at the temporary site, the MAC frequently collaborates with the Montreal Museum of Fine Arts to display recent acquisitions and key pieces of Quebec and Canadian art.

5. Mary, Queen of the World Cathedral

Mary, Queen of the World Cathedral is a striking piece of Renaissance-inspired architecture right in downtown Montreal, modeled after St. Peter’s Basilica in Rome. Though smaller in scale, the cathedral impresses with its ornate interior, massive dome, and finely detailed chapels.

Façade and Statues: Unlike St. Peter's, which features statues of the twelve apostles, the roofline of this cathedral is adorned with statues representing the patron saints of the thirteen parishes of Montreal that contributed to its construction.

Baldachin: Inside, you can see a magnificent copper baldachin (canopy) over the high altar, which is a hand-sculpted reproduction of the famous Bernini masterpiece found in Rome.

6. Redpath Museum

The Redpath Museum in Montreal is a must-see for those interested in history and natural sciences. Located at McGill University, it houses an impressive collection of artifacts and exhibits that span geology, paleontology, and ancient cultures.

Dinosaur Skeletons and Fossils: The second-floor gallery is a major draw, featuring an Albertosaurus skeleton, prehistoric fish, and a vast collection of local Quebec fossils.

World Cultures and Mummies: The museum houses a significant collection of Egyptian antiquities, including three mummies, alongside artifacts from various indigenous cultures around the world.

7. Place d'Armes

Place d’Armes is one of the oldest public squares in Montreal, surrounded by striking architecture that reflects the city’s evolving history. Anchored by the Notre-Dame Basilica, the square offers a clear view of Montreal’s past through its surrounding buildings, ranging from 18th-century banks to modern office towers.

Architectural Diversity: The square is framed by iconic landmarks, including the Gothic Revival Notre-Dame Basilica, the neoclassical Bank of Montreal Museum, and the Art Deco Aldred Building, which was modeled after the Empire State Building.

Maisonneuve Monument: At the center of the square stands a majestic bronze monument dedicated to Paul de Chomedey, Sieur de Maisonneuve, the founder of Montreal, commemorating the city's 250th anniversary.

8. Habitat 67

Habitat 67 is one of the most recognizable and significant architectural landmarks in the world. Originally created as a pavilion for Expo 67, this housing complex designed by Moshe Safdie was intended to reinvent the apartment building by combining the benefits of suburban homes, namely gardens, fresh air, and privacy, with the density of a modern city.

Architectural Marvel: The complex is made up of 354 identical, prefabricated concrete forms (cubes) arranged in various combinations to create 148 residences, each with its own private roof terrace.

Brutalist Icon: It is a prime example of Brutalist architecture and remains a pilgrimage site for architects and students from around the globe.

9. Montreal Science Centre

The Montreal Science Centre is a must-visit destination for anyone interested in science, technology, and interactive exhibits. Located in the heart of Old Montreal, this modern museum offers a variety of hands-on displays that engage visitors of all ages.

Interactive Exhibitions: The centre features several permanent galleries where you can explore themes like human biology, physical forces, and environmental sustainability through tactile experiments and creative challenges.

IMAX TELUS Theatre: Home to a massive screen and state-of-the-art sound system, this theatre regularly screens breathtaking 3D documentaries that transport viewers into the deep ocean, outer space, or remote wilderness.

10. The Marguerite Bourgeoys Site

The Marguerite Bourgeoys Site (formerly known as the Marguerite Bourgeoys Museum) is an exceptional historical complex located in Old Montreal. It is uniquely situated within and beneath the Notre-Dame-de-Bon-Secours, one of the oldest churches in the city, and serves as a tribute to Montreal's first teacher and the founder of the Congrégation de Notre-Dame. The museum offers a rare opportunity to explore the layering of Montreal's history, moving from an 18th-century chapel down into archaeological excavations.

Archaeological Site: Descend into the stone foundations of the original 1675 chapel, where you can see 2,000-year-old Indigenous artifacts and traces of the earliest French colonial life.

Sailors' Church: Explore the stunning 1771 chapel, famous for its maritime theme and small wooden ships hanging from the ceiling, left by sailors as votive offerings for safe passage.

Outdoor Attractions

Outdoor Attractions

9 places, written up one at a time

1. Parc Jean-Drapeau

Parc Jean-Drapeau is a large urban park in Montreal, comprising two islands in the St. Lawrence River: Saint Helen's Island (Sainte-Hélène) and the man-made Notre Dame Island. Originally the site of the legendary Expo 67 world’s fair, it is now a 268-hectare recreational hub that hosts a variety of attractions, including art installations, festivals, and natural spaces for outdoor activities.

Circuit Gilles-Villeneuve: This is the world-famous Formula 1 track that hosts the Canadian Grand Prix. When the race isn't on, it is open to the public for cycling, rollerblading, and walking.

La Ronde: Operated by Six Flags, this is the largest amusement park in Quebec, featuring world-class roller coasters and the site of the International Fireworks Competition.

2. La Fontaine Park

La Fontaine Park is a beloved 34-hectare urban oasis in the Plateau-Mont-Royal borough, cherished by locals for its dual-style landscaping. The western section of the park is designed in the formal French style with symmetrical paths, while the eastern portion offers a more natural, English-style garden feel with winding trails and mature trees.

Scenic Lakes and Fountains: The park features two interconnected lakes, with the iconic high-shooting fountain serving as a central landmark and a favorite backdrop for picnics.

Théâtre de Verdure: This recently renovated open-air theater hosts free cultural performances, including dance, music, and film screenings, during the summer months.

3. Circuit Gilles Villeneuve

Located on Notre Dame Island within Parc Jean-Drapeau, the Circuit Gilles Villeneuve is a world-renowned motor racing venue. While it is most famous for hosting the Formula 1 Canadian Grand Prix, its unique setting and public access make it a versatile destination for both high-speed sports fans and local recreation enthusiasts.

Public Access: Outside of race events, the track is open to the public as a paved multi-use path. It is a legendary spot for cyclists, in-line skaters, and runners who want to test their speed on the same asphalt as professional racers.

Iconic Landmarks: As you navigate the 4.36 km loop, you'll pass through scenic sections like the Wall of Champions and enjoy views of the Saint Lawrence River and the Montreal skyline.

4. Lachine Canal National Historic Site

The Lachine Canal National Historic Site is a 14.5-kilometer waterway that served as the industrial heart of Canada during the 19th century. Today, it is one of Montreal's most popular linear parks, stretching from the Old Port to Lake Saint-Louis and offering a unique blend of heritage architecture, green spaces, and recreational activities.

Industrial Heritage: As you travel along the canal, you can see significant landmarks like the Old Port Locks and historical industrial structures that have been converted into modern residential and commercial lofts.

Multi-use Path: The canal features one of the city's most scenic paths for cycling, running, and in-line skating. It is a key link in Montreal's bike network, providing a flat and paved route away from car traffic.

Good to know. Note that swimming and magnet fishing are strictly prohibited. If you are cycling, be mindful that the path can become very crowded on sunny weekends, so keep a moderate speed.

5. Mont Royal Chalet

The Mount Royal Chalet is a landmark 1932 heritage building perched atop Mount Royal. It is most famous for its location adjacent to the Kondiaronk Belvedere, offering what is arguably the most iconic and sweeping panoramic view of Montreal's downtown skyscrapers, the St. Lawrence River, and the hills of the South Shore.

Historic Architecture: The stone chalet features a grand interior with high ceilings and impressive paintings that depict various stages of Montreal's history, providing a regal resting spot for hikers.

Belvedere: Just outside the chalet’s front doors, this massive semicircular terrace is a favorite for photographers, sunrise seekers, and anyone looking to capture the classic shot of the city.

6. Olympic Park

The Montreal Olympic Park is a colossal architectural and cultural hub built for the 1976 Summer Games. Today, it stands as one of the city's most recognizable symbols, blending high-performance sports facilities with a vast array of tourist attractions and public event spaces.

Olympic Stadium: Known for its striking Big O shape, this is the largest stadium in Canada by seating capacity. While it no longer hosts a permanent sports team, it is a major venue for massive concerts, monster truck shows, and trade fairs.

Montreal Tower: Leaning at a 45-degree angle, this is the tallest inclined tower in the world. Visitors can typically ride a glass-encased funicular up the spine of the tower to an observation deck that offers breathtaking, 80-kilometer views of the city and the Laurentian mountains.

7. Mount Royal Summit

The Mount Royal Summit offers stunning panoramic views of Montreal, making it one of the city's most beloved outdoor spots. As the highest point in the city, it provides a breathtaking perspective of the skyline, the St. Lawrence River, and the surrounding areas.

Summit Cross: A short walk from the chalet leads you to the Mount Royal Cross, an illuminated 31-meter steel structure that has stood as a symbol of the city since 1924, commemorating a vow made by the city's founder.

Kondiaronk Belvedere: This massive semicircular plaza directly in front of the summit chalet offers the most famous postcard view of the Montreal skyline, looking out over the downtown core toward the St. Lawrence River.

8. Parc des Rapides

Rapids Park (Parc des Rapides) is a hidden gem located in the LaSalle borough, offering a front-row seat to the powerful Lachine Rapids. This 30-hectare waterfront park is a sanctuary for nature lovers and birdwatchers, providing a peaceful contrast to the city's more urban green spaces.

Lachine Rapids: The park offers several lookout points where you can witness the raw power of the St. Lawrence River's rapids, which are a designated migratory bird sanctuary.

Birdwatching: It is one of the best spots in Montreal to see various species of herons, ducks, and even the occasional bald eagle, especially during migration seasons.

9. Atwater Market

Established in 1933, Marché Atwater is one of Montreal’s most iconic public markets, housed in a striking Art Deco building featuring a prominent clock tower. Located right along the Lachine Canal, it is a favorite for both locals and tourists looking for high-quality gourmet products and a lively community atmosphere.

Gourmet Specialities: The market is renowned for its high-end butchers, specialty cheese shops (fromageries), and artisanal bakeries. Reviewers often highlight the selection of unique olive oils, spices, and locally produced maple products.

Seasonal Highlights: During the summer, the outdoor stalls overflow with fresh Quebec produce and vibrant flowers. In the winter, the market transforms into a festive destination, often hosting a popular Christmas market with artisanal crafts and holiday treats.

Other Interesting Places

Other Interesting Places

11 places, written up one at a time

1. Bonsecours Market

Inaugurated in 1847, the Bonsecours Market is an architectural masterpiece of the neoclassical style, easily recognized by its grand silver dome. Once serving as Montreal's main public market and even as the temporary Parliament of United Canada, it is now a prestigious multi-purpose center housing high-end boutiques and exhibition spaces.

Quebec Craftsmanship: The market is a premier destination for discovering Made in Quebec products. Visitors often highlight the unique selection of designer clothing, jewelry, and artisanal home decor found in its various boutiques.

Art Galleries: Several galleries within the building showcase local art, providing a sophisticated cultural experience alongside your shopping.

2. Underground City (Réso)

The Montréal Underground City, locally known as RÉSO, is one of the largest subterranean pedestrian networks in the world. Spanning over 32 kilometers, this climate-controlled labyrinth connects shopping malls, office towers, hotels, and transit hubs, making it a vital part of Montreal life, especially during the cold winter months.

Shopping and Dining Hub: The network links several major shopping complexes, including Eaton Centre, Place Montréal Trust, and Complexe Desjardins, offering hundreds of stores and a vast array of food courts ranging from quick bites to upscale dining.

Cultural and Transit Connectivity: Beyond retail, it provides indoor access to major cultural venues like Place des Arts, several universities, and over 10 Metro stations, allowing you to traverse much of the downtown core without ever stepping outside.

3. Rue Ste.-Catherine

Spanning over 11 kilometers, Rue Sainte Catherine is the primary commercial artery of Montreal and one of the longest shopping streets in North America. It serves as the backbone of the downtown core, connecting major department stores, entertainment venues, and diverse neighborhoods.

Shopping Destination: The street is home to flagship locations of major retailers like La Maison Simons, Hudson's Bay, and various international brands, alongside several interconnected shopping malls like the Eaton Centre.

Entertainment and Festivals: A significant portion of the street passes through the Quartier des Spectacles, where it often becomes pedestrian-only during the summer to host massive festivals like the Montreal International Jazz Festival and Just for Laughs.

4. Plateau Mont-Royal

The Plateau-Mont-Royal is arguably Montreal’s most colorful and creative borough. This densely populated neighborhood is famous for its distinct architectural identity, specifically its iconic exterior spiral staircases, and its vibrant, bohemian atmosphere that has long attracted artists, students, and young professionals.

Architectural Charm: The neighborhood is a photographer's dream, known for rows of Victorian-style houses with brightly painted doors and winding outdoor staircases designed to save interior space.

Artistic Soul: You’ll find some of the city's most impressive murals and street art here, particularly in the alleys and along Saint Laurent Boulevard, which hosts the annual MURAL Festival.

5. Place Jacques-Cartier

Place Jacques-Cartier is a historic square that serves as the heart and soul of Old Montreal. Sloping gently from City Hall down to the Old Port, this lively pedestrian plaza is the primary gathering spot for visitors, known for its vibrant atmosphere, street performers, and European-inspired charm.

Street Performance and Art: During the summer, the square becomes a stage for caricaturists, musicians, and buskers, creating a festive environment that reviewers frequently describe as the quintessential Old Montreal experience.

Dining and Terraces: Both sides of the square are lined with historic buildings housing restaurants and cafes that spill out onto large outdoor terraces, offering some of the best people-watching spots in the city.

6. Little Italy

Little Italy (Petite Italie) is a charming and historic neighborhood in Montreal, centered around Saint Laurent Boulevard and Saint Zotique Street. It is the heart of the city's Italian community, offering a warm, village-like atmosphere filled with traditional cafes, trattorias, and specialty grocery stores. While the neighborhood has modernized over the years, it retains a deep sense of heritage and is home to some of the most authentic culinary experiences in Montreal.

Jean-Talon Market: Located at the edge of the neighborhood, this is one of North America's largest open-air markets, offering an incredible selection of fresh produce, local cheeses, and artisanal products.

Traditional Coffee Culture: The area is famous for its long-standing coffee shops, like Caffè Italia, where locals have gathered for decades to enjoy traditional espresso and talk soccer.

7. Chinatown

Montreal’s Chinatown offers visitors a vibrant blend of rich cultural heritage and modern urban charm. Located in the heart of the city, this bustling neighborhood is home to a variety of restaurants, shops, and temples that reflect the deep influence of Chinese culture in Montreal.

Pedestrian Paifang Entrances: The neighborhood is marked by four impressive traditional gates that define its boundaries, making it a highly photogenic and walkable area.

A Culinary Destination: The district is world-renowned for its diverse food scene, ranging from legendary hand-pulled noodle shops and dim sum halls to modern bubble tea spots and Dragon’s Beard candy stalls.

8. Crescent Street

Located in the heart of downtown, Rue Crescent is one of Montreal's most famous nightlife and dining strips. While only three blocks long between Sherbrooke and René-Lévesque, it is packed with Victorian architecture housing upscale boutiques, lively pubs, and fine-dining restaurants, making it a major hub for the city's festive energy.

Formula 1 Festival: During the Grand Prix weekend in June, the street is closed to traffic for a massive festival featuring luxury car displays, live music, and outdoor bars.

Mural Landmark: The street features the iconic Leonard Cohen Mural (technically overlooking Crescent from a nearby building), a massive tribute to the legendary Montreal poet and singer.

9. Quartier des Spectacles

The Quartier des Spectacles is the cultural heart of Montreal, a square-kilometer district dedicated to the arts, performance, and year-round festivities. It is world-renowned for hosting some of the globe's largest festivals, including the Montreal International Jazz Festival and Just for Laughs, transforming its streets into massive open-air stages.

Place des Festivals: This massive public plaza serves as the district's focal point, featuring the country's largest interactive fountain with 235 jets and sophisticated lighting systems that animate the space during major events.

Luminous Pathway: At night, the district is unified by a unique lighting design, a red Luminous Pathway of dots on the sidewalk, that guides visitors to the more than 80 cultural venues and performance spaces in the area.

10. Montreal Insectarium

The Montreal Insectarium has recently completed a massive $38 million renovation known as Metamorphosis, transforming it into one of the most innovative and immersive natural science museums in the world. Rather than just looking at bugs in boxes, the experience is designed to make you feel like an insect, utilizing cutting-edge architecture and sensory storytelling to dissolve the boundaries between humans and the natural world.

The museum is organized as a "choreographed route" that moves visitors through different emotional and sensory states.

The Alcoves: This underground, labyrinthine space is designed to disorient your human senses. You’ll walk through tunnels with vibrating floors, squeeze through tight passages, and see flickering screens that mimic a fly's compound vision.

11. Casino de Montreal

The Casino de Montréal is Canada's largest casino, uniquely housed within two iconic pavilions from the 1967 World Exposition (Expo 67). Located on Notre Dame Island in Jean-Drapeau Park, it offers a distinct architectural experience with its unconventional numerous windows and low ceilings, set against a backdrop of river views. The casino is a major entertainment hub, featuring over 500,000 square feet of floor space dedicated to a wide variety of games and live performances.

Gaming Variety: It boasts more than 3,000 slot machines and over 80 gaming tables, including classics like Blackjack, Roulette, and Baccarat.

The Zone: An interactive and immersive area that features electronic gaming tables with lower minimum bets and a festive atmosphere hosted by live presenters.
