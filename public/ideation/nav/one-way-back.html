ArcTrips navigation, round two

One Way Back

There is exactly one navigational control on the page, and it is the way back. The sticky row is 64px and holds three things in a fixed order: a back control that names its destination in words, the title of the page you are on, and search. No tabs, no rail, no breadcrumb, because the back control is the breadcrumb collapsed to the single step that matters. Depth is carried by the label alone: on /tofino/hiking it reads "Things to do in Tofino", on /tofino/things-to-do it reads "Tofino", on /tofino it reads "Vancouver Island". One control, one meaning, every page, every depth.

Lateral movement between subjects is not deleted, it is relocated. It sits at the foot of the article as content, under a heading, where a reader who has finished reading is the only person who wants it. Frame 3 shows it.

Reference pattern: the iOS UINavigationController back button. The most-used navigation control on the most-used computer in the world, and it is one labelled thing in a fixed position. It works because the position never moves, it always names the parent rather than describing an action, it never changes shape as depth changes, and the title beside it answers "where am I" without the reader having to parse the bar. This direction takes all four properties and adds the one thing a 44px phone target could not afford: the label is set large enough to be the visual anchor of the row.

Decisions a reviewer should push on

  • Weight. The back control is a 44px filled pill in azure tint with a 1.5px azure border and 15px bold label. It is deliberately not solid azure: the page's booking CTA is the solid one, and two solid azure pills on a screen is two primary actions. Within the row it is still by a wide margin the largest and highest-contrast object, which is the property the brief asked for.
  • Hit area. 44px tall, and the whole pill including the chevron is the target. Pressed state is a 3.5% scale-down into the darkest azure, so the press registers before the page moves.
  • Motion. Going back, the outgoing page leaves to the right and the new page arrives from the left, 400ms on the system easing. The back label slides in from the left on the same curve. Direction of travel is legible without reading anything.
  • Depth without reading. A 2px azure rail on the bottom edge of the bar runs 25 / 50 / 75 / 100 percent of the width for region / town / index / subject. It is not a control and cannot be clicked. It is the only concession to signalling depth by something other than the label.
  • The root. On Vancouver Island there is nothing above, so the back slot holds the ArcTrips wordmark instead. That is the one place the control changes, and it changes to the only other thing that can honestly occupy the position.
