The Trail

The place bar is deleted. The breadcrumb is promoted from decoration into the only navigation the site has. One 64px sticky row carries the whole trail, British Columbia / Tofino / Things to do / Hiking and trails, with every ancestor a real target and the current leaf as plain text. There is no back button, no tab row and no subject rail in the chrome, because the trail already is all three: it is the way back, it is the sense of depth, and it is the sense of place. Three systems became one, which is the whole of round two.

The jitter problem, solved by a fixed lattice rather than by alignment. Breadcrumb segments with menus were rejected because the control moved sideways as URL depth changed. Right-aligning the trail would have fixed the leaf and moved every ancestor instead, which is the same defect pointed the other way. So the trail is not a flow of text at all: it is four fixed-width slots and three fixed separators, always rendered, at every depth. British Columbia occupies the same 150px whether you are one level down or three. The 20px of room the back arrow needs is reserved in all four slots and painted in only one, so a label does not shift when it stops being the parent. Slots ahead of the leaf are present and invisible, holding their width. The consequence is that a segment's x position is a property of the segment and never of the depth, which is directly testable across the three desktop frames below.

Depth is read as a position, not as a count. A 3px azure marker sits on the bar's own bottom rule under the leaf slot, and slides right as you go deeper. You do not count the segments to know how far in you are. You see where the marker sits.

Eighteen walks, from the village outward

The ones you can start on foot first, then the national park trails strung along the highway, then the three that need a boat. Difficulty climbs with the distance.

Tonquin Trail

The Tonquin Trail is one of the best easy trails in Tofino. It starts near the village and leads through coastal forest to Tonquin Beach, Third Beach, and Middle Beach. You will pass old-growth forest, ocean viewpoints, stairs, and small beaches. It is also a popular sunset walk.

Easy to moderate Forest, beaches, sunset

Good to know. The trail can be muddy after rain, and there are stairs in some areas. Wear shoes with good grip.

Cox Bay Lookout

Cox Bay Lookout is one of the most popular viewpoint walks near Tofino. The trail is short, but it can be steep, muddy, and slippery. The reward is a view over Cox Bay, the beach, the forest, and the coastline.

Moderate Views and photos

Good to know. This is not a polished boardwalk trail. Wear proper shoes and avoid it if conditions are very slippery.
