Two directions, one complaint

What the destination navigation should be

The owner's note was "navigation is cumbersome here". Two audits ran against the same live site, independently and without seeing each other's work: one through Apple's interface guidelines, one through a UX and information architecture review. Each then built its own recommendation as a working mockup.

They agreed on the diagnosis. Three stacked sticky bars take 175px, about 19 percent of a 900px viewport, on a site whose job is long-form reading. The place bar means three different things on three different page types. On a subject page "Things to do" appears four or five times before the hero. Search and the primary CTA are both deleted below 760px.

They disagreed on the cure. One puts the whole tree into a menu behind the place name. The other strips the chrome and moves the tree into the page as content. Both mockups are interactive, at desktop and at 390px, over the same two real pages.

Direction one

Title as Menu

From the apple-design audit

One 56px translucent row on every route, with the place name itself as the menu. Tapping "Tofino" opens a popover holding the town's sections, all nine subjects, and the nearby places. Matching the iOS and macOS navigation bar whose title is the menu: Safari tab groups, the Finder path popup.

  • Every lateral move is one tap from any page, including from a town overview, which today costs three
  • The two rows read as one translucent material with a scroll edge fade, not three bordered slabs
  • Search and "Find a stay" survive at 390px

Direction two

One Rail

From the impeccable audit

Strip the chrome instead of enriching it. One global rail plus a town context bar, and the nine subject chips demoted out of the sticky stack into the page body, where they appear once under the breadcrumb and again at the foot under "More things to do in Tofino". The breadcrumb becomes the only way up.

  • "Things to do" drops from five appearances before the hero to two
  • Fixes the aria-current lie the generator writes on all nine subject pages
  • Gives the fourteen towns with zero inbound links a route in, with a QA gate to keep it that way

How to read them against each other

The real question

  • Should the tree live in a control, or in the page?
  • Title as Menu bets that a reader wants to jump sideways at any moment, and hides the cost behind one tap.
  • One Rail bets that a reader wants to read, and that the sideways move belongs after the article, not above it.

What to click first

  • Title as Menu: click "Tofino" in any frame. That popover is the entire proposal.
  • One Rail: look at the top 130px of the subject frame and count the repeats, then scroll to the foot.
  • Both: the 390px frame, where the live site currently has neither search nor a CTA.

What is settled either way

  • No sidebar and no breadcrumb dropdown. Both were built before and rejected.
  • The breadcrumb stays a plain trail.
  • Sticky offsets move to one variable instead of the two hardcoded values that already disagree.
Exploration only. Neither direction is built into the site. Palette and typeface are the locked brand set; composition, density and motion are what is being decided here.
